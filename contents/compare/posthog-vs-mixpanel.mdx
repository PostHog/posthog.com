PostHog and [Mixpanel](/compare/best-mixpanel-alternatives) are both product analytics platforms helping teams understand user behavior and build better products. Both expanded beyond core analytics, but their approaches and feature sets differ.

1. **Mixpanel** is a product analytics platform with web analytics, session replay, heatmaps, experiments, feature flags, and Metric Trees, plus a built-in AI agent and an MCP server. It's built for product managers and growth teams who want deep behavioral insights.

2. **PostHog** is your product's context layer. On top of [product analytics](/product-analytics), it includes [web analytics](/web-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [experiments](/experiments), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), [AI observability](/ai-observability), [surveys](/surveys), an [AI assistant](/ai), and more. A [data warehouse](/warehouse) and [data pipelines](/data-pipelines) tie it all together and unify that context into one source agents can read across. You can work with all of it from [Slack](/slack), the [CLI](/docs/cli), or your own editor via [the MCP](/mcp). It's built for engineers, AI builders, and product teams.

In this post, we compare features, pricing, AI and MCP access, and the best fit for different teams.

## How is PostHog different?

### 1. We're an all-in-one platform

PostHog puts all your data in one place and combines it with every tool you need to build a successful product. This means:

- **[Product analytics](/product-analytics)** for analyzing behavior, funnels, activation, and retention
- **[Web analytics](/web-analytics)** for tracking your marketing website and content
- **[Session replay](/session-replay)** for observing how people use your product and diagnosing problems
- **[Feature flags](/feature-flags)** for testing in production and shipping new features
- **[Experiments](/experiments)** for verifying improvements to your product and website
- **[Error tracking](/error-tracking)** for monitoring exceptions and problems in your code
- **[Logs](/logs)** for searching what your servers wrote, next to the replay and error they relate to
- **[Surveys](/surveys)** for capturing user feedback, tracking NPS, and booking interviews
- **[AI observability](/ai-observability)** for understanding how users interact with AI features
- **A built-in [data warehouse](/warehouse)** for analyzing your business and event data together

In other words, it's [everything you need in one app](/products) with a single login and contract. A _genuine_ single source of truth for your product and customer data.

### 2. It's built by developers, for developers

This means you get support from the engineers who _actually build the product_, [extensively documented APIs](/docs/api), and a [SQL query builder](/docs/data-warehouse/sql), so you can analyze data how you want. 

Your agents get the same access you do, through a free [MCP server](/docs/model-context-protocol), a [Slack app](/slack), and a [CLI](/docs/cli).

We're open source, so you can inspect [our source code](https://github.com/PostHog) and see what's coming next on [our public roadmap](/roadmap).

### 3. We promise transparent and cheap pricing (forever)

We pride ourselves on [having a generous free tier](/pricing). Your first 1 million events every month are free, and 97% of companies use PostHog for free.

Beyond the free tier, our pricing is usage-based and transparent. Anonymous events start at $0.00005 each and identified events at $0.000248, and both get cheaper as your volume grows. There's no minimum spend and you can set [billing limits](/docs/billing/limits-alerts) (unlike in Mixpanel) so you're never surprised.

Want to know how much we'll charge? See our [pricing calculator](/pricing).

### Session replay

PostHog [session replay](/session-replay) can be used by anyone, but it includes numerous developer-level features that make it useful for engineers, product managers, and support engineers who want to diagnose issues and identify potential improvements. 

Features include [console logs](/docs/session-replay/console-log-recording), [network request monitoring](/docs/session-replay/network-recording), a [DOM explorer](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings), performance metrics, and the ability to jump directly from [errors](/docs/error-tracking) or [funnel](/docs/product-analytics/funnels) drop-offs into relevant replays.

PostHog keeps recordings for 30 days on the free plan, 90 days on pay-as-you-go, and up to 5 years on Enterprise. Mixpanel keeps them for 30 days, or for up to a year with an Enterprise add-on. With [Replay Vision](/replay-vision), AI can also watch your recordings in bulk with your own prompts, monitors, and classifiers. Mixpanel can summarize replays with AI, but it has no custom scanners.

### AI and MCP
 
Both tools now have a built-in AI agent and a hosted MCP server, so the question is what your agents can see in each.
 
- **Mixpanel** has Mixpanel Agent, an AI analyst that builds reports and answers questions in plain language, AI summaries of session replays, and Agent Intelligence for analyzing AI agent traces, which is in beta for a limited set of customers. Its hosted MCP server can read and write, with its experiment and feature flag tools in open beta.

- **PostHog** has [PostHog AI](/ai) for questions, SQL, and fixes (500 credits a month free), a free [MCP server](/mcp) that can read and write, a [Slack app](/slack) you can tag in a thread, a [CLI](/docs/cli), [Replay Vision](/replay-vision) for scanning recordings in bulk, and [Scouts](/blog/what-is-a-scout) that watch your data and send reports to an [Inbox](/docs/self-driving/inbox) where agents can open PRs.
 
The difference is what's behind them. Mixpanel's agent and MCP server work from your events, replays, and experiments, and Mixpanel Agent can also read tools like GitHub, Jira, Linear, and Notion through MCP Connections. PostHog's work from your events, replays, and experiments plus your errors, logs, survey responses, and the external data in your warehouse.

### Price comparison

**PostHog charges based on usage.** Products come with a [free monthly allowance](/pricing), and past that you pay published per-unit rates, with billing limits you can set on most products. You don't need a contract, though [pre-paid annual plans](/docs/billing/pre-paid-plans) come with a discount. We've also written up [what you pay for per event](/docs/billing/what-you-pay-for-per-event).
 
**Mixpanel charges by event volume.** The first 1 million events a month are free. Past that, its Growth plan is priced per event, with a lower price if you pay yearly, and add-ons like Group Analytics and Data Pipelines cost extra. Prices are public up to 20 million events a month.

## When to choose PostHog vs Mixpanel

Choosing the right product analytics platform depends on your team's needs and technical depth. Here's a quick guide:

- Want one platform that goes beyond analytics with [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), [AI observability](/ai-observability), and [surveys](/surveys), plus a [data warehouse](/warehouse) for your business data and agents that work from [Slack](/slack) or [the MCP](/mcp), with transparent pricing and a generous free tier? Go with **PostHog**.

- Prefer a polished UI optimized for product managers and growth teams, with Metric Trees and connectors that sync data in from your warehouse? **Mixpanel** is a solid choice.

## Recommendations by team type

**For engineering-led product teams**
- **PostHog** – SQL access, open-source codebase, and direct support from the engineers who build the product. Tightly integrated feature flags, experiments, and error tracking mean fewer tools to manage.

**For product management teams**
- **Mixpanel** – Purpose-built for non-technical users with a polished interface, metric trees for aligning teams on goals, and strong collaboration features.

**For teams building AI products**
- **PostHog** – Native [AI observability](/ai-observability) for tracking AI usage, model performance, token costs, and debugging LLM interactions, with 100k events free every month. Mixpanel's equivalent, Agent Intelligence, is in limited beta.

**For privacy-conscious and regulated organizations**
- Both are SOC 2 certified and GDPR-ready with EU data residency options, and both sign BAAs for HIPAA: **PostHog** on its [Boost, Scale, and Enterprise packages](/platform-packages), and **Mixpanel** on its Enterprise plan. **PostHog** also offers raw data access via its [data warehouse](/warehouse). SAML SSO is on PostHog's Scale and Enterprise packages, and on Mixpanel's Growth and Enterprise plans.

**For early-stage startups**
- **PostHog** – A single platform that scales from MVP to growth stage without rebuilding your stack. The generous free tier means you won't outgrow it quickly. [Startups can also qualify for $50k in free credits](/startups).

## Frequently asked questions
 
<details>
<summary>What does it mean for PostHog to be your product's context layer?</summary>

Mixpanel can tell you what your users did and what they saw; PostHog knows that too, as well as what broke, what your servers logged, and what they told you in a survey. That's because [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [logs](/logs), [surveys](/surveys), and [AI observability](/ai-observability) all share one model of users and sessions.
 
The [data warehouse](/warehouse) and [data pipelines](/data-pipelines) bring in the business data that lives elsewhere, like Stripe, HubSpot, or Postgres, and push data back out, so your agents can read across all of it. They can work from [Slack](/slack), the [web app](/ai), or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp), and they see your whole product, not just your events.
 
</details>

<details>
<summary>PostHog says it makes your product "self-driving" – what does that mean?</summary>

It means PostHog digs through your product data, finds what's worth fixing, and has agents do the work.

It starts with context. A full suite of developer tools – [AI observability](/ai-observability), [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [experiments](/experiments), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), and more – captures everything happening in your product, and a [data warehouse](/warehouse) unifies it into one source agents can read across.

<details>
    <summary>What's the difference between PostHog and Mixpanel?</summary>

- **Scope:** Mixpanel covers product and web analytics, session replay, heatmaps, experiments, and feature flags; PostHog adds error tracking, logs, surveys, AI observability, and a built-in data warehouse
- **Pricing:** Both charge by event with 1 million free a month; PostHog charges less for anonymous events and is pay-as-you-go by default, while Mixpanel is cheaper on yearly billing
- **Data warehouse:** PostHog has one built in, with sources like Stripe and Zendesk; Mixpanel syncs data in from your own warehouse with Warehouse Connectors on paid plans
- **Goal tracking:** Mixpanel has Metric Trees (an Enterprise add-on); PostHog has no equivalent
- **Audience:** PostHog is built for engineers and product teams; Mixpanel is built for product managers and growth teams
- **Open source:** PostHog is open source (MIT, open core); Mixpanel is closed source

</details>

<details>
    <summary>Does PostHog replace Mixpanel?</summary>

**Yes, for most teams.** PostHog offers all of Mixpanel's core analytics features (funnels, retention, cohorts, user paths) plus additional tools like error tracking, AI observability, and surveys. If you're an engineering-led team that values SQL access, open-source code, and transparent pricing, PostHog is a strong replacement.

**Consider keeping Mixpanel if** your team is heavily invested in Mixpanel's metric trees for goal alignment, or you prefer their polished UI for non-technical users.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is PostHog cheaper than Mixpanel?</summary>

It depends on how you pay and how many of your events are anonymous. Both include 1 million free events a month. Beyond that, PostHog's pay-as-you-go price is lower than Mixpanel's month-to-month price at every volume up to 20 million events, even if all your events are identified: $352 against $378 a month at 3 million events, and $1,928 against $2,289 at 20 million.
 
Mixpanel is cheaper if you commit to yearly billing ($270 a month at 3 million events), unless about a third or more of your events are anonymous, which PostHog charges up to 80% less for.
 
**PostHog works best if:**
- You want to pay as you go without committing to a year
- A lot of your traffic is anonymous, like a marketing website
- You want session replay, error tracking, and surveys on the same bill

**Mixpanel works best if:**
- You're happy to commit to a year upfront
- Almost all of your events come from identified users

</details>

<details>
    <summary>How much does Mixpanel cost?</summary>

Mixpanel has three tiers:

1. **Free:** 1M events a month, 10,000 session replays, and limited experiments and feature flags
2. **Growth:** First 1M events free, then priced by event volume, from $140 a month for 1.5M events ($100 on yearly billing)
3. **Enterprise:** Custom pricing, custom experiment and feature flag volumes, advanced features

Add-ons like Group Analytics and Data Pipelines are separately priced. Experiments and Feature Flags are included on every plan, with limits on monthly experiment users and active flags (1,000 and 10 on Free; 5,000 and 50 on Growth). Session Replay is included up to 10,000 replays a month on Free and 20,000 on Growth. For comparison, PostHog publishes its usage-based rates on its [pricing page](/pricing).

</details>

<details>
    <summary>Which has better session replay – PostHog or Mixpanel?</summary>

Both offer solid session replay with web and mobile support, console logs, and network activity. **PostHog** adds a DOM explorer and performance metrics, and lets you jump from an error or a funnel drop-off straight to the replay. **Mixpanel** has a heatmap comparison mode. Both can summarize replays with AI, which PostHog does through [Replay Vision](/replay-vision). PostHog's free tier includes 5,000 web and 2,500 mobile replays a month; Mixpanel's includes 10,000.

</details>

<details>

Yes. Mixpanel launched feature flags with Experimentation 2.0 in October 2025 for Enterprise plans, and added them to its Free and Growth plans in August 2026 with limits on active flags. It supports local evaluation on server SDKs. PostHog also has [scheduled flag changes](/docs/feature-flags/scheduled-flag-changes) and [early access management](/docs/feature-flags/early-access-feature-management), which Mixpanel's docs don't cover.

<details>
    <summary>Does Mixpanel have error tracking?</summary>

No. Mixpanel does not offer error tracking or crash reporting. If you need to monitor exceptions alongside your analytics, you'd need a separate tool like Sentry or Bugsnag. **PostHog** includes [error tracking](/error-tracking) natively, connected to session replays and user behavior data.

</details>

Not as a generally available product. </details>

<details>
    <summary>Which is easier to set up – PostHog or Mixpanel?</summary>

Both are designed for quick implementation. **PostHog** offers an [AI install wizard](/docs/ai-engineering/ai-wizard) that gets you running in minutes, plus autocapture that starts collecting events immediately. **Mixpanel** also supports autocapture and has straightforward SDK installation. The main difference is PostHog's broader feature set means there's more to explore, but you can adopt features incrementally.

<details>
    <summary>Which has better data warehouse integration?</summary>

Different approaches. **Mixpanel** connects to external warehouses to import data and run queries in Mixpanel. **PostHog** offers an integrated data warehouse approach: you can use PostHog as your warehouse (data stays in PostHog and is accessible across all PostHog tools), or connect external warehouses (like Snowflake or BigQuery) as sources to sync the tables and fields you need into PostHog.

**PostHog is better if:**
- You want to eliminate tool sprawl (Mixpanel is analytics-focused, with Warehouse Connectors on its paid plans)
- You value having the warehouse and analytics in one platform
- You want features Mixpanel doesn&apos;t offer, like surveys
- You want transparent, usage-based pricing (1 million synced rows free per month)

**Mixpanel&apos;s approach is better if** your existing warehouse is already your source of truth and you only want product analytics on top of it.

Companies like [HeadshotPro](/customers/headshotpro) and [Webshare](/customers/webshare) use PostHog&apos;s integrated warehouse as their single source of truth, eliminating the need to stitch together multiple vendors.

</details>

<details>
    <summary>What about ad blockers?</summary>

We recommend all users deploy a reverse proxy, which enables you to send events to PostHog Cloud using your own domain. Events sent from your own domain are less likely to be intercepted by tracking blockers, ensuring you capture the best data possible.

We make this super easy by offering a [managed reverse proxy](/docs/advanced/proxy/managed-reverse-proxy) – a no code solution that takes minutes to set up. We also have [reverse proxy setup guides](/docs/advanced/proxy) for AWS CloudFront, Caddy, Cloudflare, Netlify, Vercel, and more in our docs.
