PostHog is your product's context layer. It combines [product analytics](/product-analytics), [web analytics](/web-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [experiments](/experiments), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), [AI observability](/ai-observability), [surveys](/surveys), and more. 

All your data lives in one place, tied to the same users and sessions. A [data warehouse](/context-warehouse) pulls in the business data that lives elsewhere – [Stripe](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/stripe), [HubSpot](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/hubspot), [Postgres](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/postgres), and [dozens more](/docs/data-pipelines/sources) – and a [CDP](/data-pipelines) pushes it back out to the tools you already use. You can query all of it with SQL, or hand it to an agent from [Slack](/slack) or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp).

As an all-in-one developer platform, it replaces _a lot_ of legacy tools. PostHog is great if you:

1. Need quantitative _and_ qualitative insights into user behavior
2. Want usage-based pricing with a free tier on every product and no seat fees
3. Want one centralized dataset that you and your agents can query from the app, [Slack](/slack), or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp)

If that sounds good, feel free to skip to the [install wizard](/wizard). 

If you'd rather see what else is out there and how PostHog stacks up against it, keep reading: this guide covers the most popular PostHog alternatives – what they're good at, where they fall short, and who they're actually for.

<details>
<summary>What does PostHog actually do?</summary>

PostHog is a product development platform that includes:

- **[Product analytics](/product-analytics):** Funnels, retention, user paths, cohorts, and trends. SQL access for power users.
- **[Session replay](/session-replay):** Watch real user sessions with console logs, network monitoring, and a DOM explorer, or let [Replay Vision](/replay-vision) watch them for you and turn what it finds into events you can chart and alert on.
- **[Feature flags](/feature-flags):** Roll out features to specific users or groups without redeploying.
- **[Experiments](/experiments):** Run multivariate experiments tied directly to your analytics data.
- **[Error tracking](/error-tracking):** Monitor exceptions linked to session replays and user behavior.
- **[Web analytics](/web-analytics):** Traffic sources, pageviews, UTM tracking, and conversion goals.
- **[AI observability](/ai-observability):** Track token usage, latency, costs, errors, and user feedback across your AI pipelines, tied to the user and session that triggered them.
- **[Data warehouse](/context-warehouse):** Sync Stripe, HubSpot, Postgres, Salesforce, and dozens of other sources next to your product data and query all of it with SQL. 1M rows a month are free.
- **[CDP](/data-pipelines):** Send data to and from PostHog with a library of integrations and transformations.
- **[Logs](/logs):** Capture and search log data alongside your analytics and session replays.
- **[Workflows](/workflows):** Automate actions and notifications based on product events.
- **[Surveys](/surveys):** Targeted, multi-step surveys with NPS, CSAT, and PMF templates.
- **[PostHog AI](/ai):** Built-in AI tools including an MCP server, natural language querying, AI-powered session summaries, and more.
- **[MCP](/mcp), [Slack](/slack), and [CLI](/docs/cli):** Use everything below from your editor, a Slack thread, or your terminal, so your coding agent can query product data and ship a fix without you copying anything between tabs.

PostHog is built by engineers, for engineers. It's priced per product with a [generous free tier](/pricing) – 97% of companies use PostHog for free!

 
</details>
 
<details>
<summary>When should you consider a PostHog alternative?</summary>

PostHog isn't the right fit for everyone. You might want an alternative if:

- **You need a no-code builder for product tours or in-app guides** – A coding agent can add tooltips, walkthroughs, and banners to your app with PostHog's [product tours skill](/changelog?id=2956) (`npx @posthog/wizard@latest skill creating-product-tours`), but there's no UI for building them. If your CS or product team needs to ship guides without an engineer, [Pendo](/compare/posthog-vs-pendo) is a good alternative here.
- **You need multi-touch attribution** – PostHog's [marketing analytics](/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics) (beta) imports ad spend and reports cost per conversion, but has no attribution models. [Amplitude](/blog/posthog-vs-amplitude) is stronger for growth and marketing teams who need this.
- **You only need one specific tool for a lightweight use case** – For example, you just want cookieless, privacy-friendly traffic stats with a minimal script and no consent banners, a focused tool like [Plausible](/compare/posthog-vs-plausible) or [Fathom](/compare/best-fathom-alternatives) might be simpler and cheaper than a full platform.
- **You prefer to fully self-host** – PostHog can be self-hosted, but it's operationally complex at scale. Tools like [Matomo](/compare/posthog-vs-matomo) or [OpenReplay](/blog/best-open-source-session-replay-tools) are designed with self-hosting as the primary deployment model.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is it hard to deploy PostHog?</summary>

Not at all for PostHog Cloud. Our [AI setup wizard](/wizard) walks you through installation and configuration in a few minutes – run the command, answer a few questions, and you're tracking events. No credit card required, and you don't need to jump on a sales call. Most teams are up and running within a single session.

If you want to go further, PostHog has [SDKs for every major platform and framework](/docs/libraries), and the [docs](/docs) cover everything from basic pageview tracking to advanced group analytics and feature flag targeting.
 
Self-hosting is a different story – it's more involved and requires managing your own infrastructure. 

For most teams, PostHog Cloud (with US or [EU hosting options](/blog/posthog-cloud-eu)) is the right choice.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is PostHog for me?</summary>

If one or more of these sounds like you, yes:
 
- **You're an engineer or technical founder** – PostHog is built for people who like SQL, MCPs, APIs, and open-source code, and whose agents work from Slack, the CLI, or the editor. You'll feel at home.
- **You're an early-stage startup or solo founder launching your MVP** – The free tier covers most early-stage needs, and [PostHog for Startups](/startups) offers $50k in additional credits. You won't need to swap tools as you scale.
- **You're building an AI product** – PostHog has first-class support for [AI observability](/ai-observability), error tracking for AI pipelines, and an MCP server that connects your product data to your AI coding tools.
- **You don't want to pay upfront** – No credit card, no sales call, no minimum contract. You only pay when you exceed the [(very generous) free tier](/pricing).
- **You want one source of truth** – Instead of stitching together multiple tools (Mixpanel, LaunchDarkly, Sentry, and Hotjar), PostHog gives you the same capabilities and keeps all your product data in one place, and the [data warehouse](/context-warehouse) brings your external data in alongside it.

If you're primarily a marketer or a non-technical product manager, or you'd rather do most of your work in a polished UI than through an agent, SQL, or the MCP, PostHog might not be the best fit.

Keep reading to explore the alternatives.

</details>

## Amplitude

- **Founded:** 2012
- **Who it's for:** growth and marketing teams who need multi-touch attribution, forecasting, and AI Visibility (AEO), and product teams who want polished analytics with guides, surveys, and experiments in one vendor.
- **Who it isn't for:** engineering-led teams who want SQL access, open-source code, error tracking, or logs alongside their analytics, or anyone who wants a price before talking to sales on the Growth plan.

![Amplitude](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/pasted_image_2026_10_30_T18_01_04_844_Z_f8fa5d05d4.png)

### What is Amplitude?

[Amplitude](/blog/posthog-vs-amplitude) is a product analytics platform with a strong focus on behavioral analytics, retention, and growth. It's well-established in the product analytics space and has built a broader platform that includes a CDP (now called Amplitude Activation), a separate A/B testing product (Amplitude Experiment), session replay, and guides and surveys. 

Since May 2026 it has also operated [Statsig](/compare/posthog-vs-statsig), the experimentation platform OpenAI acquired in 2025.

[Amplitude](/compare/best-amplitude-alternatives) is particularly strong for marketing and growth teams who need multi-touch attribution and predictive analytics.

### Key features
 
- **Behavioral analytics:** Charts, funnels, user paths, and retention with a polished interface.
- **Predictive analytics:** Predictions (likelihood to convert or churn) and anomaly detection with forecasting on Growth and Enterprise plans.
- **Multi-touch attribution:** First-touch through data-driven models, with the full set on Growth and Enterprise.
- **Guides and Surveys:** Behavior-based in-app tooltips, walkthroughs, NPS surveys, and popups triggered by cohorts and behavioral data.
- **Amplitude Activation:** The built-in customer data platform for managing user data, identity resolution, and syncing audiences out.
- **Amplitude Experiment:** Separate A/B testing product with feature flagging (unlimited flags on every plan, one active experiment on Free and Plus).
- **Amplitude AI:** AI Agents that monitor dashboards and replays, AI Feedback, AI Visibility (how your brand shows up in LLM answers), Agent Analytics for scoring AI agent traces, and an MCP server for querying Amplitude data from Claude, Cursor, and other AI tools.

<details>
<summary>What are the main differences & similarities between Amplitude and PostHog?</summary>

1. **Marketing vs. engineering focus:** Amplitude offers multi-touch attribution, predictive forecasting, and AI Brand Visibility (which tracks how your brand appears in LLMs), which PostHog doesn't have. PostHog is designed for engineers and product teams: both have [CDPs](/data-pipelines) and MCP servers, but PostHog adds SQL access, open-source code, and debugging products such as error tracking and logs. It also [ships relentlessly](/changelog), with new features landing every week, which tends to matter to engineering teams who want their tools to keep up.

2. **AI capabilities:** Both have AI assistants and MCP servers for querying data from tools like Claude and Cursor. Amplitude's AI Feedback stands out for marketing teams – it aggregates and synthesizes user signals from Zendesk, G2, Reddit, app store reviews, and more into prioritized product insights – and its 2026 Agent Analytics scores AI agent traces for task success. 

PostHog's [AI observability](/ai-observability) goes deeper into the pipeline itself: token usage, cost, latency, and errors per generation, tied to the user's session, replay, and any exception it caused. On the replay side, [Replay Vision](/replay-vision) runs AI scanners across many recordings at once and logs each finding as an event, where Amplitude's Session Replay agent summarizes sessions you point it at.

## Mixpanel

![Mixpanel](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/mobile_analytics_3_2237x1480_9b1931dc95.jpg)

- **Founded:** 2009
- **Who it's for:** product managers and growth teams who want polished, approachable analytics with session replay, experiments, and flags in one focused tool, and startups who qualify for its first-year-free program.
- **Who it isn't for:** teams that need error tracking, surveys, logs, or a data warehouse alongside analytics, or who want to self-host or read the code.

### What is Mixpanel?

[Mixpanel](/blog/posthog-vs-mixpanel) is a platform that has expanded significantly beyond its analytics-only roots. It added session replay in 2024 and heatmaps in 2025, launched Experiments with feature flagging in late 2025 and brought them to its free and Growth plans in 2026, and now has an MCP server, an AI analyst (Mixpanel Agent), and an early-access product for AI agent traces.
 
[Mixpanel](/compare/best-mixpanel-alternatives) is strong on event-based analysis, funnel visualization, and its usage-based pricing model, which makes it popular with startups.

### Key features
 
- **Event analytics:** Trends, funnels, user paths, and retention analysis.
- **Experiments:** End-to-end A/B testing with feature flags, cohort targeting, sticky bucketing, and statistical significance across primary, secondary, and guardrail metrics.
- **Session replay:** Available on all plans (10k replays/month free), integrated directly with funnel and cohort analysis.
- **Heatmaps:** Click and scroll heatmaps built into the same workflow as analytics and replay.
- **Metric trees:** Interactive, living frameworks that map relationships between metrics, track goals, and connect experiment results to business outcomes (an Enterprise add-on).
- **Warehouse connectors:** Import from BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift, Databricks, and Postgres; a separate Data Pipelines add-on exports back out on Growth and Enterprise.
- **Mixpanel Agent:** An AI analyst (formerly Spark) that builds reports and cohorts and investigates replays from a plain-English question, plus Agent Intelligence (early access) for AI trace cost, latency, and errors.

## FullStory

- **Founded:** 2014
- **Who it's for:** UX research and customer experience teams who want autocaptured replays with frustration signals, in-app guides, and AI summaries, and who are comfortable with enterprise sales.
- **Who it isn't for:** engineering teams who want feature flags, experiments, error tracking, or open-source code with their replays, or anyone who wants a public price list.

![FullStory](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/pasted_image_2026_10_30_T19_15_27_803_Z_903eff66b9.png)

### What is FullStory?
 
[FullStory](/compare/posthog-vs-fullstory) is a digital experience intelligence platform best known for [session replay](/compare/best-session-replay-tools). It captures every user interaction automatically and lets teams search, filter, and analyze sessions without manual event setup.
 
[FullStory](/compare/best-fullstory-alternatives) is particularly popular with enterprise UX and customer experience teams who need to diagnose friction, understand user journeys at scale, and share session evidence across product, support, and design.

### Key features
 
- **Session replay:** Fullcapture, FullStory's autocapture of every user interaction, so you can search and segment sessions retroactively.
- **Heatmaps:** Click, scroll, and conversion maps.
- **Frustration signals:** Automatic detection of rage clicks, dead clicks, error clicks, and thrashed cursors.
- **Product analytics:** Funnels, journeys, retention, and segmentation on top of the captured data.
- **Guides and Surveys:** No-code product tours, onboarding checklists, banners, smart tips, and in-app NPS surveys triggered on behavioral data, launched in 2026 after FullStory acquired Usetiful and sold as an add-on.
- **StoryAI:** AI agents (an add-on) that summarize sessions, surface friction points prioritized by conversion impact, and answer behavioral questions in natural language.

## Heap

- **Founded:** 2013
- **Who it's for:** non-technical product and marketing teams who want autocapture with retroactive, point-and-click event definition, and who are already evaluating the wider Contentsquare platform.
- **Who it isn't for:** teams that need session replay on a free or Growth plan, feature flags, experiments, error tracking, or a product whose roadmap isn't tied to a parent company's migration.

![Heap interface](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/pasted_image_2026_10_30_T19_42_24_231_Z_e2ff2f02e9.png)

### What is Heap?
 
[Heap](/compare/posthog-vs-heap) was acquired by [Contentsquare](/compare/best-contentsquare-alternatives) in 2023 and is now sold as Contentsquare Product Analytics. Existing Heap accounts and heap.io still work, but Contentsquare says it's unifying Heap, [Hotjar](/compare/posthog-vs-hotjar), and its own platform into one product, so if you're evaluating Heap today, you're really evaluating Contentsquare.
 
[Heap's](/compare/best-heap-alternatives) key innovation was autocapture combined with retroactive event definition – you capture everything, then define what matters later. This remains genuinely useful for teams who don't know what they'll need to analyze in advance.

### Key features
 
- **Autocapture:** Capture every user interaction automatically, define events retroactively.
- **Visual labeling:** Tag events through a visual interface without code.
- **Session replay:** An add-on on Pro and Premier plans.
- **Funnels and paths:** Analyze user journeys and drop-off points.
- **Sense:** Contentsquare's chat-based AI analyst that answers plain-language questions about user behavior, builds charts on demand, and suggests follow-up questions.
- **Illuminate:** A data science layer that automatically surfaces insights from your behavioral data – including top events influencing conversion and group suggestions to identify high-impact segments – without requiring you to know what to look for in advance.
- **Heap Connect:** Sync to Redshift, BigQuery, Snowflake, S3, and Databricks (a Pro add-on, included on Premier).

## LogRocket

- **Founded:** 2016
- **Who it's for:** front-end engineering and support teams who want replays with console logs, network requests, and Redux state attached to JavaScript errors, and who can budget for a paid plan from day one.
- **Who it isn't for:** small teams or side projects (there's no free plan), anyone who needs backend error tracking, feature flags, or experiments, or teams that want open-source code.

![LogRocket interface](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/pasted_image_2026_10_30_T19_42_51_202_Z_972dcd9fdb.png)

### What is LogRocket?
 
[LogRocket](/compare/posthog-vs-logrocket) positions itself between FullStory and [Sentry](/compare/posthog-vs-sentry) – it combines session replay with front-end error monitoring and some product analytics. 

It's popular with engineering teams who want a developer-focused session replay tool with performance monitoring built in. [LogRocket](/compare/best-logrocket-alternatives) has also added Galileo, an AI layer that surfaces issues and recommendations proactively. 

### Key features
 
- **Session replay:** Full DOM replay with console logs, network requests, and Redux state.
- **Front-end monitoring:** JavaScript error tracking and performance profiling.
- **Heatmaps:** Click, rage click, and scroll maps.
- **Product analytics:** Basic funnels, user paths, and conversion tracking.
- **Galileo AI:** Session summaries, proactive issue detection, and UX recommendations, on Pro and Enterprise plans.

## Sentry
 
- **Founded:** 2012
- **Who it's for:** engineering, DevOps, and SRE teams who need deep stack traces, distributed tracing, profiling, and a mature triage workflow, with SDKs for 100+ platforms including game engines and consoles.

### What is Sentry?
 
[Sentry](/compare/posthog-vs-sentry) is the default application monitoring tool for a lot of engineering teams: error and performance monitoring first, with session replay, logs, metrics, uptime and cron monitoring, and AI agent monitoring added around it. The main web app is source-available under the Functional Source License (it converts to Apache-2.0 after two years) and free to self-host, and its hosted MCP server is free on every plan.
 
### Key features

- **Tracing and profiling:** Distributed tracing with span-level detail, plus continuous profiling on paid plans.
- **Session replay:** Replays attached to issues so you can see what happened right before an error.
- **Logs and metrics:** Both generally available, with 5 GB of each free every month.
- **Seer:** A paid AI agent ($40 per active contributor per month) that diagnoses issues and proposes fixes.
- **Uptime and cron monitoring:** Alerts when a check fails or a scheduled job doesn't run.

## Langfuse
 
- **Founded:** 2022
- **Who it's for:** AI engineering teams who want open-source LLM observability with deep prompt management, evals, and datasets, self-hosted or hosted, and who don't need anything about the product around the model.
- **Who it isn't for:** teams who want to connect an LLM trace to the user's session replay, their product analytics, the error it caused, or the flag they were in, or who need analytics, replay, or flags at all.

![Langfuse interface](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/pasted_image_2026_10_30_T19_44_43_495_Z_0d0350fc6d.png)

### What is Langfuse?
 
[Langfuse](/compare/posthog-vs-langfuse) is an open-source LLM engineering platform: tracing for LLM calls and agents, prompt management with a playground, evaluations, datasets, and dashboards. It joined ClickHouse in January 2026 and continues as a standalone product, MIT-licensed (open core) and free to self-host, with a hosted version in EU, US, Japan, and HIPAA regions.
 
It's the deepest tool on this page for the model layer itself, and it has nothing for the product around it, which is the trade.
 
### Key features
 
- **Tracing:** Nested traces and observations for every LLM call and tool use, with cost, latency, token counts, and conversation-level sessions.
- **Prompt management:** Versioned prompts with a playground, fetched by your app at runtime.
- **Evaluations:** LLM-as-a-judge, code-based evals, user feedback scores, and human annotation queues.
- **Datasets and experiments:** Run prompt or model variants against test sets and compare results.
- **MCP server:** Native server for prompt management and observation queries from Claude, Cursor, and other clients.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What does it mean for PostHog to be your product's context layer?</summary>

Every alternative on this page is good at one or two slices of what a product generates: Amplitude and Mixpanel at events, FullStory and LogRocket at sessions, Sentry at errors. PostHog stores all of those slices in one place with one model of users and sessions, so a funnel drop-off, the replay of a user falling out of it, the flag they were in, and the error they hit are the same record seen from different sides.
 
The [data warehouse](/context-warehouse) and [CDP](/data-pipelines) extend that to the business data that lives elsewhere – Stripe, HubSpot, Postgres, Salesforce – synced in and pushed back out, so "which paying customers hit this error this week" is one query rather than an export from each tool. And because it's one store, an agent working from [Slack](/slack), the [web app](/ai), or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp) sees the whole product, not the slice one vendor happens to hold.
 
</details>
 
<details>
<summary>PostHog says it makes your product "self-driving" – what does that mean?</summary>

It means PostHog digs through your product data, finds what's worth fixing, and has agents do the work.

It starts with context. A full suite of developer tools – [AI Observability](/ai-observability), [Product Analytics](/product-analytics), [Session Replay](/session-replay), [Feature Flags](/feature-flags), [Experiments](/experiments), [Error Tracking](/error-tracking), [Logs](/logs), and more – captures everything happening in your product, and a [Context Warehouse](/blog/what-is-a-context-warehouse) unifies it into one source agents can read across.

From there, [Scouts](/blog/what-is-a-scout) read across all of it and sort what's worth knowing from what's just noise. What clears the bar becomes a report in your inbox: an agent picks it up, roots out the cause, and opens a PR. You review and merge.

You can steer it from [Slack](/slack), the [web app](/ai), or your own editor via [the MCP](/mcp) or [CLI](/docs/cli).

</details>
