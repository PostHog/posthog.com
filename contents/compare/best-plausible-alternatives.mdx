[Plausible Analytics](/compare/posthog-vs-plausible) does simple, privacy-friendly website analytics really well. No cookies, no complex setup, no overwhelming dashboards.

But simplicity has its drawbacks. Plausible doesn't offer session replay, A/B testing, or feature flags. Key features like funnels and ecommerce tracking are locked behind its Business plan. And there's no free tier – just a 30-day trial.

If you've outgrown Plausible or need more from your analytics stack, this guide compares the best Plausible alternatives – whether you want a similarly lightweight tool or a full platform for building better products.

| Tool | Best for | Free plan | Open source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PostHog** | Engineers and product teams | 1M events a month | Yes (MIT, open core) |
| **Fathom** | Content and marketing sites | No (7-day trial) | No |

| **Umami** | Developers and small sites | 100k events a month, 1 website | Yes (MIT) |
| **Google Analytics** | Marketing teams in Google's ecosystem | Free, with sampling and retention limits | No |
 
**PostHog** is the pick if you want to follow a visitor past the pageview: from the [traffic source](/web-analytics) to the [funnel](/product-analytics), the [session replay](/session-replay), and the [error](/error-tracking) they hit. From there you can [A/B test](/experiments) a fix behind a [feature flag](/feature-flags), [survey](/surveys) your visitors, and query it all from your editor through [the MCP](/mcp), with [1 million events](/pricing) free every month.
 
**Umami** and **Fathom** are the closest to Plausible: simple, cookieless dashboards for content sites. Umami is open source with a free hosted plan, and Fathom includes 50 sites on its entry plan.
 
**Matomo** and **Piwik PRO** are closer to the old Google Analytics. Matomo is open source and free to self-host, and Piwik PRO adds consent management for regulated teams. **Heap** is built for product analytics, and **Google Analytics** is the free default if you rely on Google Ads.

## 1. PostHog

- **Founded:** 2020
- **Who it's for:** Startups, engineers, and product teams who want web analytics connected to product analytics, session replay, experiments, and error tracking.
- **Who it isn't for:** Sites that only need a simple traffic dashboard with cookieless tracking out of the box; PostHog's cookieless mode is opt-in.
- **Cookieless tracking:** Opt-in ([cookieless mode](/docs/privacy/data-collection))
- **Open source:** Yes

![PostHog](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/Posthog_web_analytics_679d394b35.png)

### What is PostHog?

[PostHog](/) (that's us 👋) is your product's context layer. It combines [web analytics](/web-analytics) with [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [experiments](/experiments), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [user surveys](/surveys), and [other developer tools](/products), all built on the same events, users, and sessions.

A [data warehouse](/warehouse) and [data pipelines](/data-pipelines) connect it all to the business data you keep elsewhere, like Stripe or HubSpot. You can query everything with [SQL](/docs/sql), or hand it to an agent from [Slack](/slack), the [CLI](/docs/cli), or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp).

### Key features

- **Web analytics:** Monitor your [web traffic](/docs/web-analytics) by automatically capturing and calculating metrics like [visitors, pageviews, session duration, and bounce rate](/docs/web-analytics/dashboard). See the sources, entry and exit paths, [channels](/docs/data/channel-type), and more.

- **Heatmaps:** [Clicks, rage clicks, scroll depth, and mouse movement](/docs/toolbar/heatmaps). Heatmap data is free.

- **Product analytics:** Custom [trends](/docs/product-analytics/trends/overview), [funnels](/docs/product-analytics/funnels), [user paths](/docs/product-analytics/paths), [retention analysis](/docs/product-analytics/retention), and segment user [cohorts](/docs/data/cohorts). Also, direct [SQL querying](/docs/data-warehouse/sql) for power users.

- **Agent access:** The [MCP server](/docs/web-analytics/surfaces/mcp) lets an AI agent query your web analytics [from your editor](/docs/model-context-protocol).

- **Session replays:** [View exactly how users are using your site](/docs/session-replay). Includes [event timelines](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings), [console logs](/docs/session-replay/console-log-recording), [network activity](/docs/session-replay/network-recording), and [90-day data retention](/docs/session-replay/recording-retention) on the pay-as-you-go plan.

- **A/B tests:** [Optimize your app and website](/docs/experiments) with up to 19 test variants and track impact on [primary and secondary metrics](/docs/experiments/metrics#primary-and-secondary-metrics). Automatically calculate [test duration, sample size](/docs/experiments/sample-size-running-time), and [statistical significance](/docs/experiments/analyzing-results).

- **Surveys:** [Target surveys](/docs/surveys/creating-surveys#display-conditions) by event or user properties. Templates for [net promoter score (NPS)](/templates/nps-survey), [product-market fit (PMF)](/templates/pmf-survey) surveys, and more.

### How does PostHog compare to Plausible?

Plausible focuses on web analytics. PostHog includes [web analytics](/web-analytics) but also offers a [full suite of tools](/products) to help you build a better site and product. On top of this, PostHog has a [free tier](/pricing) with 1 million events a month, while Plausible costs a minimum of $9/month.

Read our [PostHog vs Plausible comparison](/compare/posthog-vs-plausible) for an in-depth look at the differences.

## 2. Fathom

- **Founded:** 2018
- **Similar to:** Plausible, Google Analytics
- **Typical users:** Founders and content teams
- **Typical customers:** Privacy-conscious, content-focused websites

![Fathom](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/Clean_Shot_2024_10_02_at_16_12_06_c41b89ea2f.png)

### What is Fathom?

[Fathom](/compare/best-fathom-alternatives) is a privacy-focused [Google Analytics alternative](/compare/best-ga4-alternatives). It is a simple web analytics tool that captures details on traffic, sessions, referrers, sources, and campaigns. It does this while helping users stay compliant and avoid cookies.

#### Key features

- **Simple web analytics:** Fathom provides a simple, GA-like experience to monitor your website and get an overview of your traffic.
- **Cookieless:** Avoid cookie banners and track your site without using cookies.
- **Marketing metrics:** Track the sources, devices, browsers, locations, UTMs, and more for your traffic.
- **Conversions:** Track custom events and analyze them as conversions towards a goal.
- **Google integrations:** Imports your Google Analytics 4 history and, since February 2026, shows Search Console data in the dashboard.

### How does Fathom compare to Plausible?

Feature-wise, Plausible and Fathom are nearly identical. Looking closer, however, Fathom is missing some of the compliance features Plausible offers, like [open source](/compare/best-open-source-analytics-tools) code.

<summary>Main differences between Fathom and Plausible</summary>

- Plausible is open source (AGPL license) with a self-hostable Community Edition; Fathom is closed-source and cloud-only.
- Plausible is fully EU-hosted on European-owned infrastructure; Fathom is a Canadian company that processes EU visitors' data on EU servers, then stores the anonymized data in the US.
- Plausible offers conversion funnels on its Business plan; Fathom doesn't have native funnel analysis.
- Fathom starts at $15/month for 100k pageviews across 50 sites; Plausible's Starter plan is $9/month for 10k pageviews and $19/month for 100k, on one site.
- Plausible's trial is 30 days with no card; Fathom's is 7 days and needs a card.

- Both support custom events, UTM campaign tracking, and conversion goals.
- Both offer email reports and show Google Search Console data in the dashboard.
- Both can import your Google Analytics 4 history.
- Neither offers session replay, A/B testing, feature flags, or product analytics.

</details>

> 💡 **Good to know:** Fathom isn't entirely EU hosted, but instead attempts to stay compliant with European regulation by:
>
> 1. Being a Canadian company. The European Commission determined that Canada has an adequate level of data protection.
> 2. Having EU isolation so visitor data is routed and stored on EU servers.
> 3. Having a data processing agreement (DPA) available.

## 3. Matomo

- **Founded:** 2007
- **Who it's for:** Content, marketing, and e-commerce teams who want a Google Analytics-style tool they can self-host.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that want Plausible's simplicity, or self-hosters who want funnels, cohorts, and session recordings without paying for plugins.
- **Cookieless tracking:** Optional (first-party cookies by default)
- **Open source:** Yes (GPLv3; premium plugins are proprietary)

![Matomo](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/matomo_8d74c7fc2c.png)

### What is Matomo?

[Matomo](/compare/posthog-vs-matomo) is a fully-featured, privacy-focused Google Analytics alternative. It contains basic web analytics along with more advanced product and behavioral analytics features. This includes features like funnels, cohorts, session recordings, and heatmaps, which are included in Matomo Cloud and sold as plugins if you self-host.

#### Key features

- **Web analytics:** Get an overview of your traffic, popular pages, sources, referrers, and more.

- **Custom reports:** Get detailed reports on product usage, cohorts, funnels, and conversion goals.

- **Marketing analysis:** Combine traffic insights with channel attribution, keyword data, ad performance, and more.

- **Ecommerce:** Integrate with popular ecommerce platforms and track sales, products, and conversions.

- **Agent access:** An MCP server for Matomo Cloud and self-hosted Matomo, released in May 2026.

### How does Matomo compare to Plausible?

Matomo is more of a like-for-like alternative to Google Analytics, meaning it has many more features than Plausible. Both are also privacy-focused and open source, but their cloud products only have trials, not free tiers.

## 4. Heap

- **Founded:** 2013
- **Who it's for:** Product and marketing teams who want autocaptured product analytics, with session replay and heatmaps as add-ons.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that want simple, cookieless web analytics, open source code, or public pricing.
- **Cookieless tracking:** No (first-party cookies or local storage)
- **Open source:** No


![Heap](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/v1710055416/posthog.com/contents/images/blog/best-amplitude-alternatives/heap.png)

### What is Heap?

[Heap](/compare/posthog-vs-heap) describes itself as a digital insights platform. This means it offers both product analytics and session replay and supports marketing use cases with multi-touch attribution. 

[Contentsquare](/compare/best-contentsquare-alternatives), a marketing and ecommerce analytics firm, agreed to acquire Heap in September 2023 and completed the deal that December. Heap is still sold on its own as Heap by Contentsquare, and its features are also part of Contentsquare's Product Analytics.

#### Key features

- **Event autocapture:** Product teams don't need to rely on engineers to instrument all events. Heap has a visual editor for teams to tag events directly on-page for analysis.

- **Session replay:** Get qualitative insights about user behavior by replaying their session - although this lacks the debugging tools typical of most replay tools.

- **Heatmaps:** See where people click, what point they scroll to, and the areas that get the most attention.

- **Analysis suggestions**: Advanced data science capabilities discover hidden interactions, friction points, and knowledge about key paths.

- **Managed ETL:** Connect to data warehouses, so you can combine your analytics with other sources and get a fuller picture of the entire user journey.

### How does Heap compare to Plausible?

Heap has much more of a product focus than Plausible. This means it lacks the easy-to-use web analytics features, but makes up for it with product analytics, session replay, and more. It also isn't open source or cookieless.

## 5. Piwik Pro

- **Founded:** 2013
- **Who it's for:** Enterprise teams in regulated industries who need analytics, consent management, and a choice of where their data is hosted.
- **Who it isn't for:** Small teams that want a free plan or self-hosting; it has neither.
- **Cookieless tracking:** Optional
- **Open source:** No

![Piwik Pro](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/Clean_Shot_2024_10_03_at_10_24_54_2x_b0a26db1cb.png)

### What is Piwik Pro?

Piwik PRO is a suite of privacy respecting analytics tools. These include web, mobile, and product analytics as well as tag and consent management. It is targeted at users in a strict regulatory environment.
 
Piwik PRO launched in 2013, offering enterprise support and premium features on top of the open source Piwik project (now Matomo). The two went their separate ways in 2016. Since then, it has added compliance-focused features as well as a range of integrations with tools like Data Studio (formerly Looker Studio), Google Search Console, and Google Ads.

#### Key features

- **Analytics:** Create custom dashboards with trends, charts, flows, funnels, and more to analyze your analytics.

- **Tag manager:** Manage tags, pixels, and JavaScript snippets from a range of platforms.

- **Consent manager:** Collect and manage consent to track and use personal information. Customize and tailor banners for different situations.

- **Data Activation:** Piwik PRO's customer data platform. Integrate with other tools and capture data to have it all in one place, then send it from Piwik PRO to other tools.

- **Agent access:** An MCP server you run locally, in beta since May 2026.

### How does Piwik Pro compare to Plausible?

Piwik Pro takes a more heavyweight approach to compliance with its consent and tag management features.

## 6. Umami

- **Founded:** 2020
- **Who it's for:** Developers and small teams who want a Plausible-style dashboard they can self-host for free, or a free hosted plan for one site.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that want session replay or heatmaps on a low-cost hosted plan; on Umami Cloud they're on the $200/month Business plan.

#### Key features

- **Natural language search:** Ask specific questions, like "MoM growth in users on iOS", rather than searching existing reports. Ask Advisor, a Gemini-powered assistant, is in beta for English-language properties.
