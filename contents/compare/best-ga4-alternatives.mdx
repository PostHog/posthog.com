[Google Analytics 4](/blog/posthog-vs-ga4) is one of the most widely deployed analytics tools in the world – and one of the most complained about too.

GA4's event-based model is more powerful than its predecessor, Universal Analytics (UA), in theory, but in practice, many teams found it harder to use, harder to query, and harder to trust. 

Add in data sampling on large exploration queries, a 14-month limit on event-level data, [privacy concerns under GDPR](/compare/best-gdpr-compliant-analytics-tools), [opaque pricing](/blog/google-analytics-cost) once you outgrow the free version, and a UI that frustrates marketers and developers alike – and it's easy to understand why so many teams are looking for alternatives.

Whether you're leaving GA4 for privacy reasons, because you don't like it, or because you simply need more than web analytics, this guide covers the best GA4 alternatives available today.

<details>
<summary>Why do people dislike GA4?</summary>

If event-based tracking is more powerful, why do so many people complain about GA4? Users tend to fall into one of three camps:

1. **Users who miss pre-defined reports** – GA4 launched without many of the reports teams relied on. Google has added some back, including annotations in 2025 and dashboards in 2026, but things are still harder to find.

2. **Teams without data science support** – GA4 caters more to large enterprises and app developers. Its reliance on Data Studio (formerly Looker Studio) and BigQuery exports is a barrier for small business and marketing teams who don't have analytics engineers.

3. **App developers who need more** – Despite courting app developers, GA4 still falls short of alternatives that pulled users away from Universal Analytics in the first place. For many, it's too little, too late.

Some teams also avoid GA4 for privacy reasons. GA4 is not GDPR-compliant by default – it requires additional configuration including consent banners and Consent Mode v2. The 2023 EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework gave those data transfers a legal basis. The EU General Court upheld it in September 2025, though an appeal was filed the following month.

</details>

## 1. PostHog

- **Founded:** 2020
- **Who it's for:** Startups, engineers, and product teams who want web analytics connected to product analytics, session replay, experiments, and error tracking.
- **Who it isn't for:** Marketing teams that depend on GA4's built-in Google Ads attribution and Search Console reporting; our marketing analytics is still in beta.
- **GA4 data import:** Partial (GA4 report data syncs into the [data warehouse](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/google-analytics))
- **GDPR compliant:** [Yes](/docs/privacy/gdpr-compliance)

![posthog](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/v1710055416/posthog.com/contents/images/screenshots/hogflix-dashboard.png)

[PostHog](/) (that's us 👋) is your product's context layer. It combines [web analytics](/web-analytics) with [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [experiments](/experiments), [user surveys](/surveys), and [other developer tools](/products), all built on the same events, users, and sessions. 

A [data warehouse](/warehouse) and [data pipelines](/data-pipelines) connect it all to the business data you keep elsewhere, like Stripe, HubSpot, or your GA4 reports. You can query everything with [SQL](/docs/sql), or hand it to an agent from [Slack](/slack), the [CLI](/docs/cli), or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp).

### Key features

- **Web analytics:** [Pageviews, sessions, bounce rate, channels, and UTMs](/docs/web-analytics), plus conversion goals and [web vitals](/docs/web-analytics/web-vitals).
- **Product analytics:** [Funnels](/docs/product-analytics/funnels), [retention](/docs/product-analytics/retention), [user paths](/docs/product-analytics/paths), and [SQL access](/docs/sql) on the same events.
- **Session replay:** [Watch what visitors did](/docs/session-replay), with console logs and network activity, on web and mobile.
- **Heatmaps:** [Clicks, rage clicks, scroll depth, and mouse movement](/docs/toolbar/heatmaps). Heatmap data is free.
- **Feature flags and experiments:** Roll out changes with [feature flags](/docs/feature-flags) and A/B test them with [experiments](/docs/experiments), without another tool.
- **Marketing analytics (beta):** [Syncs spend and clicks](/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics) from Google, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok, and other ad platforms.
- **Data warehouse and CDP:** Sync GA4 reports, Stripe, HubSpot, and other sources into the [data warehouse](/warehouse) and query them alongside your events.
- **Agent access:** The [MCP server](/docs/web-analytics/surfaces/mcp) lets an AI agent query your web analytics from your editor.

### How does PostHog compare to GA4?

PostHog covers the core [web analytics](/docs/web-analytics) most teams use in GA4 – traffic sources, pageviews, sessions, bounce rate, UTMs, [conversions](/docs/web-analytics/conversion-goals), and [web vitals](/docs/web-analytics/web-vitals) – and adds far more on top. It doesn't yet match GA4's advanced attribution or its built-in Google Ads reporting, though [marketing analytics](/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics) is in beta.

Where GA4 is a marketing analytics tool, PostHog is a full product development platform. This means that, in addition to tracking user behavior, you can also use PostHog to run [A/B tests](/docs/experiments), set up [feature flags](/docs/feature-flags), [record user sessions](/docs/session-replay), and a lot more.

GA4 doesn't have any of these features built-in, so you'd need to integrate with other tools to get the same functionality.

Read our [PostHog and Google Analytics comparison](/blog/posthog-vs-ga4) for an in-depth look at the differences.

## 2. Matomo

- **Founded:** 2007
- **Who it's for:** Content, marketing, and e-commerce teams who want a Universal Analytics-style tool they can self-host, with their GA4 history imported.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams tracking apps more than websites, or self-hosters who want funnels, cohorts, and session recordings without paying for plugins.
- **GA4 data import:** Yes (aggregated reports, not raw events)
- **GDPR compliant:** Yes

![Matomo - open source analytics tools](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/v1710055416/posthog.com/contents/images/blog/open-source-analytics-tools/matomo-screenshot.png)

[Matomo](/compare/posthog-vs-matomo) is one of the most popular Google Analytics alternatives around. Like UA, it's based on a session-based tracking model, and it even allows new users to import data from an existing Google Analytics account.

It also shares some weaknesses inherent in Universal Analytics. While [Matomo](/compare/best-matomo-alternatives) has added more advanced analytics features, it better suits traditional website analytics than tracking desktop and mobile apps. It's open source, so can be self-hosted if you prefer.

### Key features

- **Web analytics:** Visits, referrers, goals, and e-commerce reports, with no data sampling on any plan.
- **Google Analytics import:** A free importer brings in your GA4 report data, on Cloud and self-hosted.
- **Privacy:** Can be configured for cookieless tracking, and Matomo Cloud stores data in Frankfurt.
- **Premium features:** Funnels, cohorts, heatmaps and session recording, and A/B testing are included in Matomo Cloud and sold as plugins for self-hosted Matomo.
- **Mobile SDKs:** Official SDKs for iOS and Android.
- **Agent access:** An MCP server for Cloud and On-Premise, released in 2026.

### How does Matomo compare to GA4?

Matomo is session-based like Universal Analytics, making it a more natural migration path for teams who preferred the old Google Analytics model. It gives you full data ownership and GDPR compliance without the complexity of GA4.

## 3. Kissmetrics

- **Founded:** 2008
- **Who it's for:** Marketing and e-commerce teams who want to tie campaigns and behavior to revenue for each customer.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that want traffic metrics like bounce rate, EU data hosting, or session replay.
- **GA4 data import:** No
- **GDPR compliant:** Yes

![kissmetrics](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/q_auto,f_auto/pasted_image_2026_11_19_T18_01_26_061_Z_257c2f5bd5.png)

**Kissmetrics** is all about tracking marketing ROI. As a result, it doesn't track what Kissmetrics deems vanity metrics like bounce rate, time on page, exits, etc. It does, however, track most essential website metrics, and makes it easy to understand the impact of organic and paid marketing activity.

One of its key features is the Populations report, which groups users into key cohorts, such as those who have recently activated trials, or are at risk of churning. You can also create custom funnel reports, and track user paths through your website.

### Key features

- **Person-based tracking:** Every event is tied to a person, so you can see everything one customer did.
- **Populations:** Groups defined by behavior, like trials at risk or recently churned customers, that keep themselves up to date.
- **Funnels and paths:** Funnels and activity reports are on the free plan; revenue, paths, and A/B test reports are on Growth.
- **Campaign reporting:** Performance by campaign, source, and medium, with a UTM builder.

### How does Kissmetrics compare to GA4?

Kissmetrics and GA4 serve different masters. GA4 is built for traffic and marketing attribution. Kissmetrics is built for understanding customers and driving revenue – it intentionally excludes "vanity metrics" like bounce rate and time on page, focusing instead on what actually drives growth.

## 4. TelemetryDeck

- **Founded:** 2021
- **Who it's for:** Indie developers and app teams who want privacy-safe analytics for iOS, Android, and Flutter apps.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that need user profiles, ad attribution, or website-first analytics.
- **GA4 data import:** No
- **GDPR compliant:** Yes

![telemetrydeck](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/q_auto,f_auto/pasted_image_2026_11_19_T19_20_44_169_Z_4b2ac82b01.png)

While it can be used on websites, **TelemetryDeck** is primarily a privacy-minded analytics tool for mobile apps. As such, it uses an event-based tracking model – TelemetryDeck calls them signals. It has first-party SDKs for Swift (iOS, macOS etc. apps), Kotlin (Android and Java apps), Flutter, and JavaScript (Node and web apps).

TelemetryDeck makes it easy for app developers to track things like active users, OS version, app version, and basic user metadata like user location. It also supports basic retention and conversion funnel insights. TelemetryDeck doesn't collect any personally identifiable metadata, so you don't need tracking consent banners.

### Key features
 
- **SDKs:** Swift, Kotlin, Flutter, and JavaScript, plus a web SDK and partner-maintained React Native support.
- **Privacy:** Collects no personally identifiable information, and runs on servers in Germany and Finland.
- **Retention and funnels:** See how many users come back after day 1, 7, and 30, and where they drop off.
- **Purchase tracking:** Automatic purchase tracking and a RevenueCat integration.

### How does TelemetryDeck compare to GA4?

TelemetryDeck does far less than GA4 by design. It's intentionally lean – no user profiles, no ad attribution, and only basic purchase tracking. What it offers is privacy-safe app analytics with zero compliance overhead, which is a compelling advantage for many indie developers and privacy-focused teams.

## 5. Plausible

- **Founded:** 2018
- **Who it's for:** Content and marketing teams who want simple, cookieless website analytics they can read at a glance.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that need user-level product analytics, session replay, or a free hosted plan.
- **GA4 data import:** Yes (historical stats)
- **GDPR compliant:** Yes

![Plausible Analytics - open source analytics tools](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/v1710055416/posthog.com/contents/images/blog/open-source-analytics-tools/plausible-screenshot.png)

[Plausible](/compare/posthog-vs-plausible) is a leader in the trend of lightweight, privacy-orientated analytics tools. It's easy to use and doesn't collect any personally identifiable information. This makes it ideal for complying with GDPR, but this comes at the cost of functionality. 
 
[Plausible](/compare/best-plausible-alternatives) focuses on website metrics like pageviews, session duration, and referrers, plus custom events and goals. Funnels and revenue attribution are on its Business plan. It has no user-level product analytics, so it's a poor fit for apps, and it's significantly less powerful than Google Analytics and other alternatives in this list.
 
But, if you just want basic website analytics, it gets the job done, and won't adversely impact the performance of your website thanks to its lightweight tracking script.

### Key features
 
- **Lightweight script:** 2.5KB with no cookies.
- **Goals and custom events:** On every plan; funnels, revenue attribution, and custom properties are on Business.
- **Google integrations:** Imports your GA4 history and connects to Search Console.
- **Events API:** For server-side and mobile app tracking.
- **Self-hosting:** A Community Edition under AGPLv3, without funnels and revenue goals.

### How does Plausible compare to GA4?

Plausible covers the basics of web analytics without the complexity. It's not a like-for-like GA4 replacement – it lacks user profiles, advanced segmentation, and the depth of reporting GA4 offers. But for teams who just want simple, trustworthy traffic data without GDPR headaches, it's hard to beat.
