PostHog and [Google Analytics 4 (GA4)](/compare/best-ga4-alternatives) are both popular tools for understanding how users interact with your website or app – but they're built with very different goals.

1. **GA4** is designed to track traffic, campaigns, and user behavior across websites and apps, with tight integration into Google's advertising ecosystem. It's one of the most widely deployed analytics tools in the world, and the default choice for teams who want marketing and attribution data in a familiar interface. It's built for marketers and growth teams.

2. **PostHog** is your product's context layer. On top of [web analytics](/web-analytics), it includes [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [experiments](/experiments), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), [AI observability](/ai-observability), [surveys](/surveys), an [AI assistant](/ai), and more. A [data warehouse](/warehouse) and [data pipelines](/data-pipelines) tie it all together and unify that context into one source agents can read across. You can work with all of it from [Slack](/slack), the [CLI](/docs/cli), or your own editor via [the MCP](/mcp). It's built for engineers, AI builders, and product teams.

If you're looking for a [GA4 alternative](/compare/best-ga4-alternatives) – whether for privacy reasons, because you need more than web analytics, or because you just don't like GA4 – this comparison will help you decide if PostHog is the right fit.

## How is PostHog different?

### 1. We're an all-in-one platform

PostHog combines [product analytics](/product-analytics) and [web analytics](/web-analytics) with [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [experiments](/experiments), [surveys](/surveys), a baked-in [data warehouse](/docs/data-warehouse), and more into one tightly integrated platform. Everything you need from a single app with a single contract. A _genuine_ single source of truth for your product and customer data.

GA4 is a powerful web analytics tool, but it doesn't include session replay, feature flags, A/B testing, error tracking, or surveys. For those features, you'd need separate tools like [FullStory](/compare/posthog-vs-fullstory), [LaunchDarkly](/compare/posthog-vs-launchdarkly), and [Optimizely](/compare/posthog-vs-optimizely), and a way to stitch the data together.

### 2. It's built for developers

This means you get support from the engineers who _actually build the product_, [extensively documented APIs](/docs/api), and a [SQL query builder](/docs/data-warehouse/sql) so you can analyze data how you want. Your agents get the same access you do, through a free [MCP server](/docs/model-context-protocol), a [Slack app](/slack), and a [CLI](/docs/cli). 

We're open source, so you can inspect [our source code](https://github.com/PostHog). And we ship fast – check out [the weekly changelog](/changelog) to see what's new.

GA4 is primarily designed for marketers and growth teams. It integrates tightly with Google Ads and the broader Google Marketing Platform, but offers limited developer-facing tooling.

### 3. Transparent and cheap pricing (forever)

We pride ourselves on [having a generous free tier](/pricing). Web analytics is billed with product analytics, so your first 1 million events every month are free across both, and 97% of companies use PostHog for free. 

Beyond the free tier, our pricing is usage-based and transparent. Anonymous events, which is what most website traffic is, start at $0.00005 each and get cheaper as your volume grows. There's no minimum spend, no per-seat charge, and no annual contract, and you can set [billing limits](/docs/billing/limits-alerts) so you're never surprised. 

Want to know how much we'll charge? See our [pricing calculator](/pricing).

GA4's core product is free, but once you hit the limits of the free tier you'll need to evaluate GA360, which starts at approximately [$50,000/year according to third party sources](/blog/google-analytics-cost) (Google doesn't publish pricing).

Both are free to start, cover the core web analytics reports, export to BigQuery, and have an MCP server for your agents. You can also run them side by side, with GA4 for ad reporting and PostHog for product.

### AI and MCP
 
Both tools now have built-in AI and an MCP server, so the question is what your agents can see in each.
 
- **GA4** has Ask Advisor, a Gemini-powered assistant in beta for English-language properties, AI overviews that summarize what changed on the Home page and in reports, Analytics Intelligence for automated insights and anomaly detection, and predictive metrics for properties with enough purchase or churn data. Its [MCP server](https://github.com/googleanalytics/google-analytics-mcp) is open source and left experimental status in November 2026. You run it yourself, and it's read-only by default.

- **PostHog** has [PostHog AI](/ai) for questions, SQL, and fixes (500 credits a month free), a free [MCP server](/mcp) with a read-only mode, a [Slack app](/slack) you can tag in a thread, a [CLI](/docs/cli), [Replay Vision](/replay-vision) for scanning recordings in bulk, and [Scouts](/blog/what-is-a-scout) that watch your data and send reports to an [Inbox](/docs/self-driving/inbox) where agents open PRs.

The difference is what's behind them. GA4's AI and MCP server work from your traffic and conversion reports. PostHog's work from those plus your replays, errors, logs, flags, and external data in the warehouse.

- Want one platform that goes beyond [web analytics](/web-analytics) with [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [A/B testing](/experiments), and [error tracking](/error-tracking), plus a [data warehouse](/warehouse) for your business data and agents that work from [Slack](/slack) or [the MCP](/mcp)? Go with **PostHog**.

### Recommendations by team type

**For engineering-led product teams**
- **PostHog** – [SQL access](/docs/data-warehouse/sql), an [MCP server](/mcp), open-source codebase, [error tracking](/error-tracking), and [AI observability](/ai-observability). Everything a developer needs to understand users, ship features, and debug problems without leaving the platform.

**For marketing and growth teams**
- **GA4** – Best-in-class integration with Google Ads, Search Console, and Google's attribution ecosystem. If your team lives in Google's marketing stack, GA4 is the natural fit.

**For product managers and UX teams**
- **PostHog** – [Funnels](/docs/product-analytics/funnels), [retention](/docs/product-analytics/retention), [session replay](/session-replay), and [surveys](/surveys) in one place means PMs can answer behavioral questions and gather user feedback without stitching together multiple tools.

**For B2B SaaS companies**
- **PostHog** – [Group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) enables account-level analysis, so you can understand behavior at the company level, not just the individual user level. 

**For privacy-conscious and regulated organizations**

**For content and media sites**
- **GA4** – If your primary need is understanding content performance, audience demographics, and ad revenue attribution across a high-traffic publishing site, GA4's native integrations with Google AdSense, Ad Manager, and Search Console are hard to beat. 

**For early-stage startups**
- **PostHog** – A single platform that covers analytics, replays, feature flags, and more from day one. The [generous free tier](/pricing) includes 1 million events, 5,000 web and 2,500 mobile session replays, and 1 million feature flag requests per month. [Startups can also qualify for $50k in free credits](/startups).

**For enterprise teams**
- **Tied** – GA360 raises the sampling threshold in explorations from 10 million to 1 billion events, adds unsampled explorations, and keeps event data for up to 50 months, with deep Google Marketing Platform integration for large marketing organizations. PostHog offers [SSO enforcement](/docs/settings/authentication/sso), a [BAA for HIPAA compliance](/docs/privacy/hipaa-compliance), priority support, and a complete product development stack with transparent pricing.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What does it mean for PostHog to be your product's context layer?</summary>

GA4 can tell you where your visitors came from and which pages they viewed; PostHog knows that too, as well as what they did in your product, what they saw, what broke, which flags they were in, and what they pay you. That's because [web analytics](/web-analytics), [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [logs](/logs), and [AI observability](/ai-observability) all share one model of users and sessions.
 
The [data warehouse](/warehouse) and [data pipelines](/data-pipelines) bring in the business data that lives elsewhere, like Stripe, HubSpot, Postgres, or Google Ads, and push data back out, so your agents can read across all of it. They can work from [Slack](/slack), the [web app](/ai), or your editor via [the MCP](/mcp) and your whole product, not just your traffic.
 
</details>

<details>
<summary>PostHog says it makes your product "self-driving" – what does that mean?</summary>

It means PostHog digs through your product data, finds what's worth fixing, and has agents do the work.

It starts with context. A full suite of developer tools – [AI observability](/ai-observability), [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [experiments](/experiments), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), and more – captures everything happening in your product, and a [data warehouse](/warehouse) unifies it into one source agents can read across.

From there, [Scouts](/blog/what-is-a-scout) read across all of it and sort what's worth knowing from what's just noise. What clears the bar becomes a report in your inbox: an agent picks it up, roots out the cause, and opens a PR. You review and merge.

You can steer it from [Slack](/slack), the [web app](/ai), or your own editor via [the MCP](/mcp) or [CLI](/docs/cli).

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the main differences between PostHog and Google Analytics?</summary>

- **Scope:** GA4 covers web and app analytics for marketing; PostHog adds session replay, feature flags, experiments, error tracking, surveys, logs, AI observability, and a data warehouse
- **Pricing:** GA4 is free with limits on sampling, retention, and exports, and Analytics 360 is sold through sales; PostHog has a free tier and then published usage-based pricing
- **Ads and attribution:** GA4 links natively to Google Ads, Search Console, AdSense, and Ad Manager; PostHog's [marketing analytics](/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics) is in beta
- **Data access:** PostHog lets you query raw events with SQL in the app; GA4 needs the BigQuery export for that
- **Privacy and compliance:** PostHog offers EU hosting in Frankfurt and a BAA on its Boost, Scale, and Enterprise packages; GA4 has no EU-only data residency and no BAA
- **Agents:** PostHog's MCP server is hosted and can read and write across its products; Google's is self-run and read-only by default
- **Open source:** PostHog is open source (MIT, open core); GA4 is closed source

</details>

After the free allowance, you pay only for what you use, with pricing that scales down at volume. You can set per-product billing caps to avoid surprises.
