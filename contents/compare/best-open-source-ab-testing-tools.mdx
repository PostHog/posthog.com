Also known as split testing or [multivariate testing](/product-engineers/what-is-multivariate-testing-examples), A/B testing is the practice of splitting your audience to test variations of a product design, new feature, call to action, landing page – anything you can imagine, really.

There are dozens of A/B testing tools available, but what they offer varies widely. Many lean toward marketing use cases (i.e. testing website landing pages) and aren't useful for product feature testing, or only support specific platforms like Shopify or WordPress.

In this guide, we're looking at the best free and open-source A/B testing tools. Open-source tools are mainly built for developers, so we recommend marketers read our guide to the best [alternatives to VWO](/compare/best-vwo-alternatives).

<details>
<summary>What features do you need in an open-source A/B testing tool?</summary>

A good open-source A/B testing tool lets you experiment with changes to your product and measure their impact on real users without locking you into a proprietary platform. Most solid tools include:

- A/B and multivariate testing for comparing different variations
- Statistical analysis to determine when results are significant
- Feature flags for safely rolling out changes to specific user segments
- SDKs for your stack (JavaScript, Python, React, mobile, etc.)
- Self-hosting options so you keep full control of your data

More advanced tools go further with:

- **Bayesian and Frequentist statistics** so you can choose the approach that fits your team's workflow
- **Multi-armed bandit experiments** that automatically allocate traffic to the winning variation
- **Holdout groups** to measure the long-term cumulative impact of your experiments
- **Visual editors** for creating variations without code changes
- **Warehouse-native architecture** that connects directly to your existing data infrastructure
- **Product analytics integration** so you can analyze experiment results alongside broader user behavior

</details>

## 2. GrowthBook

- **Who it's for:** Data and engineering teams that already have a data warehouse and want experiments analyzed on top of it.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that want CUPED, sequential testing, or bandits without paying (they're on Pro and Enterprise), or more than one project on the free plans.
- **License:** MIT (enterprise features are licensed separately)

![GrowthBook - best open source ab testing tools](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/v1710055416/posthog.com/contents/images/blog/open-source-testing-tools/growthbook.png)

[GrowthBook](/compare/posthog-vs-growthbook) is an [open-source platform for feature flags](/compare/best-open-source-feature-flag-tools) and A/B tests that helps teams to deploy code efficiently and analyze experiments. Built by engineers who wanted better insights on new releases, it is a modular solution that promotes feature flagging as an essential step in the development process. This means it can be used as a full experimentation platform, for feature flags only, or for experiment analysis only.

In 2026, [GrowthBook](/compare/best-growthbook-alternatives) made product analytics generally available and rebuilt its visual editor as an AI Visual Editor.

### What does GrowthBook include?
 
- **Warehouse-native analysis:** Connects to BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift, Databricks, ClickHouse, Postgres, and more with read-only access, so raw user data stays in your warehouse. A managed warehouse is available if you don't have one.
- **Statistics:** Bayesian and frequentist engines on every plan. CUPED variance reduction and sequential testing are on Pro and Enterprise.
- **Experiment types:** Feature flag experiments on every plan, plus an AI Visual Editor for no-code web tests, multi-armed bandits, and safe rollouts on Pro and Enterprise. Holdouts are Enterprise-only.
- **Product analytics:** Generally available since version 5.0 in July 2026, with the explorer on all plans.
- **SDKs:** Lightweight SDKs, including Swift, Kotlin, Flutter, and React Native for mobile.
- **Agent access:** An MCP server on all plans, hosted on Cloud or run locally when you self-host.

### How much does GrowthBook cost?
 
GrowthBook open source is free to self-host with unlimited users and experiments, limited to one project. Their cloud Starter plan is free for up to 3 users and one project, also with unlimited experiments. 

The Pro cloud plan costs $40 per seat per month for up to 30 users and adds advanced statistics (CUPED, sequential testing), the AI Visual Editor, and multi-arm bandits. If you self-host, those paid features need an Enterprise license.

> #### Bottom line
>
> GrowthBook is the best choice for data and engineering teams that already have a data warehouse and want to run experiments on top of their existing infrastructure. Its warehouse-native approach means your raw user data can stay in your own warehouse.

## 3. Unleash

- **Who it's for:** Engineering teams that mainly need feature flags and controlled rollouts, want to self-host, and already have an analytics tool for reading experiment results.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that want experiment results and statistics built in, a free hosted plan, or more than one project and two environments without paying.
- **License:** AGPLv3 since June 2026 (Apache 2.0 before)

![Unleash - best open source ab testing tools](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/v1710055416/posthog.com/contents/images/blog/open-source-testing-tools/img2_Unleash.png)

**Unleash** is a feature management platform that empowers engineering teams to implement A/B tests via feature toggles and offers accurate user targeting. It appeals to privacy-conscious organizations because flags can be evaluated inside your own infrastructure, so you don't have to share user data with third parties.

You can use Unleash to define the rules of your experiments, but the platform doesn't provide all the tools you need to manage A/B tests end-to-end. Instead, you'll need to connect your experiment with an analytics platform like [PostHog](/product-analytics), [Google Analytics](/compare/posthog-vs-ga4), or [Mixpanel](/compare/posthog-vs-mixpanel) to visualize results.

Unleash moved its server from Apache 2.0 to AGPLv3 with version 8 in June 2026. Its SDKs keep their permissive licenses.

### What does Unleash include?
 
- **Flags and variants:** Feature toggles with variants and user targeting, which you use to split traffic for an A/B test.
- **No built-in analysis:** Unleash's SDKs emit impression events that your code forwards to an analytics tool of your choice, and that's where you read results. Its impact metrics, an Enterprise feature, are for monitoring rollouts, not analyzing experiments.
- **SDKs:** 17 official SDKs, eight for backend and nine for frontend and mobile, plus OpenFeature providers, most of them in beta.
- **Privacy:** Backend SDKs evaluate flags locally, so user data stays inside your application.
- **Integrations:** Slack, Microsoft Teams, Datadog, and webhooks, with Jira Cloud on Enterprise.
- **Agent access:** An MCP server that can set up an experiment, though it doesn't analyze the results.

### How much does Unleash cost?
 
Unleash's open source edition is free to self-host, with one project and two environments. There's no free hosted plan, only a 14-day trial. Enterprise is pay-as-you-go at $75 per seat per month, hosted by Unleash or self-hosted (with a 5-seat minimum when self-hosted). Custom Enterprise pricing is available for teams that want annual contracts and dedicated support.

> #### Bottom line
>
> Unleash is the best open-source option for teams that primarily need feature flag management and want to add basic A/B testing on top.

## 4. Flagsmith

- **Who it's for:** Engineering teams that want feature flags and remote config they can self-host, with experimentation in the same tool.
- **Who it isn't for:** Teams that need a mature experimentation suite today (its native experiments are in beta and Bayesian only), or more than one team member on the free cloud plan.
- **License:** BSD 3-Clause (enterprise features are licensed separately)

![Flagsmith](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/q_auto,f_auto/pasted_image_2026_11_17_T17_32_53_304_Z_e498e0017f.png)

[Flagsmith](/compare/best-flagsmith-alternatives) is an open-source feature flag and remote configuration service that lets you manage features across mobile, web and server-side applications.

Until 2026, A/B testing with Flagsmith meant using its multivariate flags as a bucketing engine and sending the data to a third-party analytics tool. 

It now has native experimentation, in beta: multivariate flags serve the variants, events flow into a warehouse, and a built-in Bayesian statistics engine reports the results. It works in the open source build as well as on Flagsmith's cloud, though self-hosters need their own warehouse and event pipeline to run it in production. The older bring-your-own-analytics route is still supported.
 
### What does Flagsmith include?
 
- **Flags and remote config:** Feature flags and typed remote config values across web, mobile, and server-side apps, with segment rules for targeting.
- **Experimentation (beta):** Multivariate flags serve the variants, and a built-in Bayesian engine reports lift, a 95% credible interval, win probability, and a sample ratio mismatch check. Events go to Flagsmith's managed warehouse on Cloud, or your own ClickHouse, Databricks, or PostgreSQL (on Cloud, connecting your own needs the Scale-Up plan or above).
- **Integrations:** Amplitude, Mixpanel, Segment, Heap, and RudderStack, if you'd rather analyze experiments in your own analytics tool.
- **Deployment:** Cloud, private cloud, or fully on-premises. The open source build is free to self-host with no API request limits.
- **Agent access:** An MCP server, hosted or self-hosted, and OpenFeature providers.

### How much does Flagsmith cost?
 
Flagsmith is open source under a BSD 3-clause license and free to self-host. A supported Enterprise edition for on-premises or private cloud hosting is priced on request. 

Their cloud free plan includes up to 50,000 requests per month for 1 project with 1 team member with unlimited flags, environments, and segments. The Start-Up cloud plan is $45 per month ($40 if billed yearly) for up to 1 million requests and 3 team members, and Scale-Up is $300 per month ($250 if billed yearly) for 5 million or more.

> #### Bottom line
>
> Flagsmith is the best fit for teams that want self-hosted feature flags and remote config first, with experiments in the same tool. Its built-in analysis is still in beta and Bayesian only, so if you need mature statistics today, PostHog or GrowthBook is the safer pick.

## Other open source tools worth knowing
 
These didn't make the list, either because they're newer and less widely used or because they don't analyze results:
 
- **Bucketeer**: An Apache 2.0 feature flag and experimentation platform from CyberAgent. It has built-in Bayesian analysis with a sample ratio mismatch check, and you self-host it with Docker Compose or Kubernetes. No free hosted plan is documented.
- **FeatBit**: An MIT-licensed, open core feature flag platform with Bayesian A/B/n analysis and guardrail metrics. It's free to self-host with no seat limits, and its cloud has a free plan for up to 1,000 monthly active users.
- **GO Feature Flag**: A fully MIT-licensed flag tool built around OpenFeature. It can split traffic for an experiment and export the data, but it doesn't analyze results.

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="A note on Mojito" type="fyi">
Mojito was on earlier versions of this list. It's an open source (BSD 3-clause) split testing stack from Mint Metrics that lets you build, launch, and analyze experiments through Git and CI. Its JavaScript library still gets a few maintenance commits a year, but the storage and reporting components haven't been updated since 2021 and 2020. It can still suit a team that wants a tiny, fully source-controlled setup, but it's no longer one we'd recommend starting with.
 
</CalloutBox>

<details>
  <summary>What's the difference between feature flags and A/B testing?</summary>

[Feature flags](/docs/feature-flags) let you toggle features on or off for specific users without deploying new code. [A/B testing](/docs/experiments) uses feature flags to randomly split users into groups (variants) and then measures which variant performs better against a goal metric. 

In practice, most A/B testing tools on this list – **PostHog**, **GrowthBook**, **Unleash**, **Flagsmith** – are built on top of a feature flagging engine. The distinction matters because **Unleash** is primarily a feature flag tool that can be _used_ for A/B testing, while **PostHog** and **GrowthBook** provide end-to-end experimentation with built-in statistical analysis. **Flagsmith** sits in between: it's a feature flag tool that added native experiment analysis, in beta, in 2026.

</details>

The cloud-hosted Starter plan is free for up to 3 users and one project. If you need advanced features like CUPED variance reduction, the AI Visual Editor, or multi-arm bandits, you'll need the Pro plan at $40 per seat per month on Cloud, or an Enterprise license if you self-host.

For fully no-code A/B testing, you'll likely want a marketing-focused tool like [VWO](/compare/best-vwo-alternatives) or [Optimizely](/compare/best-optimizely-alternatives) instead, though these are neither free nor open source.

It depends on the tool.

<details>
  <summary>What's the difference between Bayesian and Frequentist A/B testing?</summary>

These are two statistical approaches for analyzing experiment results. 

- **Frequentist** testing uses p-values and confidence intervals – you define a sample size upfront, run the test, and check if results are statistically significant at the end. 
- **Bayesian** testing calculates the probability that one variant is better than another and updates continuously as data comes in, making it easier to interpret mid-experiment.

**PostHog** and **GrowthBook** support both approaches. **Flagsmith**'s native experiments are Bayesian only, and **Unleash** doesn't include statistical analysis at all – you'll need to calculate significance yourself or use a separate tool.

</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can I self-host an A/B testing tool?</summary>

Yes – that's one of the main advantages of open-source A/B testing tools. **GrowthBook** offers both cloud and self-hosted options, though its paid statistics features need an Enterprise license when you self-host. **Unleash** and **Flagsmith** can be self-hosted for free with their open-source editions. 

**PostHog** can be self-hosted too, but the deployment is unsupported and we recommend Cloud.

</details>

<details>
  <summary>Are older A/B testing frameworks like Wasabi, PlanOut, and Sixpack still maintained?</summary>

Mostly not. Intuit's Wasabi says it's no longer under active development, Facebook's PlanOut was archived in 2021, Sixpack hasn't had a commit since 2020, and Vanity was archived in 2024. The Split gem for Ruby is the exception and is still maintained.

They still turn up in lists of open-source A/B testing frameworks, but for a new project you're better off with one of the tools above.

</details>
