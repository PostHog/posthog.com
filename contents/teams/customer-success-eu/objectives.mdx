### Make enablement part of shipping <TeamMember name="Andre de Vries" photo />
The GTM team doesn't have a structured way of keeping up to date and trained on net new features and products, or big updates to existing ones. Today we mostly pick it up on Slack or at the company all hands demo. In Q4 I want to build a repeatable process so that product engineers, PMs and PMMs know that when a product or feature ships, enabling the field is part of that. Happy and trained staff leads to better customer conversations and better adoption of new features. I'm not the one delivering these sessions, that should be a collaboration between the product engineers, PMs and PMMs. My job is building the process and making it easy to follow.

**We'll know we're successful when**:
One end-to-end enablement has been scheduled and delivered via the new process, with documentation, a handbook update and a recording of the session.


### Are customers using the data they capture? <TeamMember name="Ella Cullen" photo />
I want to find out whether our customers are actually using what they capture today in PostHog. The plan is to define what good usage looks like for each of our core products as three tiers, from basic use to repeatable, embedded use, drawing on our docs, user interviews and what we see across customers. I'll test the tiers against my own book and adjust as needed. I'll also turn the changes that are reliable into signals: based on the criteria, a customer could go from tier 0 to tier 1, or the other way around, and a scout will check for these changes and alert on them.

**We'll know we're successful when**:
Core products have written tier definitions, I've mapped my book to them, and at least one behavioral signal is running as a scout and has led to a real customer conversation. Stretch: a playbook of specific recommendations for moving a customer from tier 0 to tier 2.
