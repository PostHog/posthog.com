### Stakeholder Engagement Strategies <TeamMember name="Phil DelGobbo" photo />
Here in CS, we do a really terrific being valuable to our users. Engineers, Product Managers, day-to-day users who engage with our platform leverage us to get the most out of PostHog, and it works great. For smaller orgs and start-ups, this is perfect, because the users are often the decision makers. At more enterprise-sized companies, we can end up disconnected to the people who decide if they use PostHog or go somewhere else. We need to improve our ability to connect with customer leadership, and demonstrate our value at the top of the org.

**We'll know we're successful when**:
- A handbook page around stakeholder engagement, including:
  - Identifying the right stakeholders
  - Outreach and maintained engagement strategies for executives
  - Demonstrating value to someone who doesn't use PostHog

### Build a setup wizard that tells customers what to cut, keep, and add <TeamMember name="Jack Tomei" photo />

A guided wizard, shipped through Christophe's concierge mode, that helps customers fit their PostHog setup to what they're actually trying to do. The customer picks their role (founder, product engineer, product manager, data engineer, or CTO to start, with more to follow) and states their goals. The wizard then asks a short set of questions: are they trying to cut spend, replace other tools in their stack with PostHog, or get more out of what they already pay for? It finishes with recommendations based on their own project: savings where they're overpaying, and expansion where PostHog can replace something they pay for elsewhere. An upgrade to (or replacement for) the current wizard audit: the same idea, but tailored to the customer's role and goals instead of one review for everyone.

**We'll know we're successful when**:
- The wizard is live in concierge mode with all 5 role paths, and adding a new role is a config change, not a rebuild
- Customers can find the wizard in the PostHog UI and run it themselves, without a CSM having to point them to it

### Make Claude a reliable investigation partner for every CSM <TeamMember name="Ryland Bowles" photo />
Right now Claude is basically guessing which skills to use. There are ~390 skills in the project 2 skill store and Claude Code only keeps descriptions for so many of them in context (about 1% of the context window), the rest it only sees by name. We end up pointing it at the right skill, pasting doc links in, and correcting it mid-investigation. Lots of the cs skills also overlap (we have 3 different weekly retro skills). I want to build a certified set of cs skills for the core stuff we do every day (account audit, account explorer, usage/spike investigation, handover). Each one gets a per product docs map that Claude only reads when an investigation goes into that product, so it pulls the right docs without bloating everyone's context.

**We'll know we're successful when**:
- A certified list of ~10 cs skills lives in the handbook, each with an owner and last verified date, and the overlapping ones are merged or archived
- Testing on 5 past real investigations to see if Claude gets to the right product docs without anyone pasting links, compared to a baseline from the start of the quarter
- Certified skills get used more in q1 2027 (after the list is published) than in q4 2026, measured by skill usage across the cs team
