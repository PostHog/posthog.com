### Q3 2026

The big shift this quarter is the switchover from Zendesk to Conversations, so every goal below assumes a Conversations-native world. A migration this big makes SLA attainment harder to measure and easy to let slip, so our overriding aim is to protect the customer experience through the transition: hold response times and SLA attainment as steady as we can, and keep CSAT high. Success this quarter looks like customers barely noticing the switchover.

#### 1. Improve the proactive and in-app support customers get

##### 1.1 Surface why a replay won't play

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Christian Rafferty" photo />

**Objective:** A continuation of last quarter's replay work. Last quarter we surfaced "why is my replay missing?"; this quarter we'll tackle our other most common question, "why is my replay not playing / infinitely buffering?". Our events already carry the debug information to answer this — the challenge is presenting it clearly enough that customers can self-solve without raising a ticket. We'll know this is working when fewer "replay won't play" tickets reach the queue.

##### 1.2 Troubleshooting skills and tools for common issues

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Phillip Ramirez" photo />

**Objective:** Build troubleshooting skills and tools for the issues we see again and again, starting with Experiments. The aim is to turn common cause-and-fix patterns into reusable skills, likely alongside the docs team. We'll know this is working when we've shipped at least one troubleshooting skill and can point to the recurring tickets it's designed to eliminate.

##### 1.3 Measure and improve support via PostHog AI

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Christiaan Hendriksen" photo />

**Objective:** A two-parter. First, research what PostHog AI is doing in a support context and how well it's doing it, before trying to fix anything. Then, based on what we find, improve the experience of getting support via PostHog AI - including how customers raise tickets through AI chats. We'll know this is working when we have a data-backed picture of AI support quality and at least one concrete improvement shipped.

##### 1.4 Define the future of proactive support
 
**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Luke Belton" photo />
 
**Objective:** Define what the future of proactive support should look like for us — using signals in a customer's own data (churn risk, sharp changes in usage, signs something isn't set up correctly) to spot people who need help and reach out before they come to us. The output this quarter is that framing, plus one or two signals we could reliably use to raise support tickets from. We'll know this is working when we have a shared view of where proactive support is heading and a shortlist of signals worth acting on.

##### 1.5 Surface useful context and grow the health overview

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Kyle Swank" photo />

**Objective:** A continuation of last quarter's work, now built around Conversations and the health overview rather than Zendesk. Start by enriching the context captured on Conversations tickets to speed up investigations, then look longer term at the health overview — including how we might surface this diagnostic information to customers so they can help themselves. We'll know this is working when tickets arrive with richer context and we have a clear direction for the health overview.

#### 2. Make support faster and smarter in Conversations

##### 2.1 Bring valuable HogDesk features into Conversations

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Xander Jones" photo />

**Objective:** Conversations is our way forward, and as a team using it every day we're well placed to make it better. Evaluate the features we find most valuable in HogDesk and bring them across into Conversations - getting it right for ourselves as much as for our customers. We'll know this is working when the HogDesk functionality we rely on is available in Conversations.

##### 2.2 Improve HogDesk features and workflows
 
**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Ben Lea" photo />
 
**Objective:** Keep building out HogDesk — improving the features we use most and streamlining our day-to-day support workflows so the team can work faster. This runs alongside the work to bring the best of HogDesk into Conversations, and helps us learn what's worth carrying across. We'll know this is working when engineers can do more of their support work in HogDesk with less friction.

##### 2.3 Improve the customer support experience and sidebar

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Ben Lea" photo />

**Objective:** Focus on the Conversations experience from the customer side, starting with the support sidebar — currently a long, unwieldy scroll of tickets that needs fixing. We'll know this is working when customers have a clearer, faster experience raising and following up on tickets.

##### 2.4 Feedback recordings in the Conversations ticket flow

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Luke Belton" photo />

**Objective:** Get Loom-style feedback recordings into the Conversations ticket-raising flow, building on the work started in Toronto — making it easier for customers to raise a ticket and giving us richer context up front. We'll know this is working when customers can attach a feedback recording when raising a ticket in Conversations.

##### 2.5 Improve account impersonation

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Steven Shults" photo />

**With:** <TeamMember name="Xander Jones" photo /> on switching QoL work

**Objective:** Make impersonation faster and less clunky. The main new piece is adding MCP impersonation to HogDesk, so we can investigate complex tickets faster; if time allows, we'll extend it to PostHog Code and Conversations. Alongside this, Xander will finish work on switching between impersonated users in the same org. We'll know this is working when engineers can get into an impersonated MCP session without the current clunky re-auth, and switching users is quick.

#### 3. Billing and legal self-serve

##### 3.1 Streamline the BAA process for startup-plan customers

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Eleftheria Trivyzaki" photo />

**Objective:** Make the BAA process self-serve for startup-plan customers. BAAs need the Boost add-on, which startup credits don't cover, and there's currently no defined path — so handling is ad hoc. We'll stop customers from signing a BAA they aren't covered by, and ship a self-serve flow so they can add Boost and get set up properly without needing to talk to sales, plus a documented process for these requests. We'll know we're successful when startup-plan customers can resolve this themselves and we can report on BAA coverage across the startup plan.

#### 4. Raise the bar: migration, offering, and team clarity

**Owner:** <TeamMember name="Abigail Richardson" photo />

##### 4.1 Complete the move to Conversations and retire Zendesk

**Objective:** Finish the migration from Zendesk to Conversations and turn Zendesk off — handbook updates, workflows and notifications in place, and reporting and CSAT working again. We'll know this is working when support runs entirely in Conversations, Zendesk is retired, and reporting and CSAT are back to full health.

##### 4.2 Define our support offering and packaging

**Objective:** Look at how we structure and package our support offering, focused on improving response times and coverage for high-paying customers. Build on the Q2 work defining when it makes sense to share a ticket across timezones vs. take care of it yourself. We'll know this is working when we have an agreed, documented support offering and response times for managed and high-paying customers are improving.

##### 4.3 Clarify the boundary between Sales/CS-owned and support-owned tickets

**Objective:** Create clarity around which tickets support picks up vs. which are managed by Sales/CS — a continuation of the Q2 boundary work. We'll know this is done when these tickets are handled more efficiently and fewer remain breaching-but-answered in the queue.
