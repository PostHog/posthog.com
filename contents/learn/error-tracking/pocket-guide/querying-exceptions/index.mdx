# Querying exceptions

Now that the engineers capture high quality exceptions, they can query them for analysis.

Exceptions are just noise if the team can't accurately understand their impact and urgency. To justify fixing one before another, a team needs context from its other PostHog data, like who the issue impacts and what they pay.

Each check below has a prompt to give an agent on [Learn Error Tracking with your agent](/learn/error-tracking/agent-prompts-and-mcp).

## Joining exceptions to other PostHog events

PostHog keeps Twig's events in one [context warehouse](/docs/self-driving/context) with persons, groups, sessions, and the tables an app can [sync](/docs/data-pipelines/sources) from tools like [Stripe](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/stripe) or [Zendesk](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/zendesk). [HogQL](/docs/data-warehouse/sql) queries all of it together, so anything sent to PostHog can be joined to Twig's exceptions.

Filter, break down, or join exceptions on these properties:

| To answer | Use | Set up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which error is it? | The message in `$exception_list`, or the issue in `$exception_issue_id` | The message is set automatically by every SDK. PostHog assigns the issue |
| Which page did it happen on? | `$current_url` or `$pathname` | Set automatically by the web SDK |
| Is it a crash? | `$exception_level` set to `fatal` | Set automatically for crashes by the <img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/Android_robot_bec2fb7318.svg" alt="" className="inline-block size-4 align-text-bottom" /> [Android](/docs/error-tracking/installation/android), <img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/posthog.com/contents/images/docs/integrate/ios.svg" alt="" className="inline-block size-4 align-text-bottom dark:invert" /> [iOS](/docs/error-tracking/installation/ios), and <img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/posthog.com/contents/images/docs/integrate/react.svg" alt="" className="inline-block size-4 align-text-bottom" /> [React Native](/docs/error-tracking/installation/react-native) SDKs |
| Which SDK or app sent it? | `$lib` or `$app_namespace` | `$lib` is set automatically by every SDK, and `$app_namespace` by the mobile SDKs |
| Who hit it? | `distinct_id` and person properties, like email | [Identify your users](/docs/getting-started/identify-users) |
| Which account hit it, and what does it pay? | `$groups` and the account's group properties | [Set up group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) |
| Which flag variant did they see? | `$feature/<flag-key>` | [Send flags with your events](/docs/feature-flags/adding-feature-flag-code), on the server too |
| Which session was it in, and what's in its replay? | `$session_id` | Set automatically by the web and mobile SDKs. On the server, [pass the session ID](/docs/data/sessions#server-sdks-and-sessions) |
| Which LLM trace was it in? | `$ai_trace_id` | [Link exceptions to traces](/docs/ai-observability/link-error-tracking) |
| Which part of the product threw it? | Custom properties | [Add them at capture](/docs/error-tracking/capture#customizing-exception-properties) |
| What do other tools say, like support tickets or billing? | Any property above, joined to a synced table | [Sync a source](/docs/data-pipelines/sources) |

[Issues and exceptions](/docs/error-tracking/issues-and-exceptions) lists every property PostHog sets.

## Revenue at risk

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Group analytics to relate users to the company they belong to. [Configure group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#set-up-group-analytics)
- An `mrr` property from a warehouse connection with [Stripe](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/stripe). [Connect your payment data](/docs/data-warehouse/warehouse-properties#set-up-a-warehouse-property)

</Callout>

Rank issues by the revenue of the accounts that hit them. If an issue hits accounts with high MRR, it might be more important, even with fewer occurrences <SeeFig n={1} />.

Combine this with the checks below to judge how severe the impact is: whether the error [makes people leave](#churn), and what they saw in [their sessions](#session-replays).

This joins exceptions to accounts on `$groups`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `$exception` | The accounts each issue hit |
| `groups` | What each account pays, from its `mrr` property |

To build a version of this, give an agent the [revenue skill](/learn/error-tracking/agent-prompts-and-mcp#skills).

The [Errors by account value](/learn/error-tracking/pocket-guide/who-it-breaks) scout runs a related check every day. It reports issues concentrated in paying accounts.

## Churn

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Session tracking to see what people did after an error. The web and mobile SDKs do this automatically. [Track sessions on your server](/docs/data/sessions#server-sdks-and-sessions)

</Callout>

Revenue shows an error's potential impact. Next, confirm whether it actually makes people leave:

- **Funnels.** The engineers build a <Term name="funnel" /> of booking a stay on Twig, with its graph type set to [historical trends](/docs/product-analytics/funnels#graph-type), to see whether conversion fell when the error appeared <SeeFig n={2} />. They [break it down](/docs/product-analytics/funnels#breakdowns) by the issue's cohort, created from the issue page, to compare people who hit the error with people who didn't.
- **Retention.** They filter a [retention insight](/docs/product-analytics/retention) to the issue's cohort and compare it with all users. If people who hit the error come back less, the error is driving churn.
- **Session drop-off.** They check whether people did anything within a minute of each error. Some sessions end anyway, so they compare each issue with the overall drop-off.

The session drop-off check joins exceptions to the events that followed on `$session_id`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `$exception` | When the error fired |
| `$pageview`, `$autocapture` | Whether the person kept going |

## Session replays

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Session replay to watch what people saw when the error happened. [Set up session replay](/docs/session-replay/installation)
- Recordings of every session with an error, so the right one exists. [Record sessions with errors](/docs/session-replay/how-to-control-which-sessions-you-record#event-triggers)

</Callout>

When an error makes people leave, the engineers watch their sessions to see what happened.

This joins exceptions to recordings on `$session_id`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `$exception` | The error and when it fired |
| `session_replay_events` | What the person saw and did |

## A customer's errors

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Group analytics to relate users to the company they belong to. [Configure group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#set-up-group-analytics)

</Callout>

In a product with paying accounts, triage can also start from a customer. When one reports a problem, rank every issue their account hit by how many of their users the issue impacts <SeeFig n={3} />. In the issue list, filter to their account and sort by **Users**.

Checkout hit 186 of their users, so fix it first.

This joins exceptions to accounts on `$groups`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `$exception` | Each issue and how many users it impacts |
| `groups` | Which customer those users belong to |

## Support tickets

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- A support tool connected to PostHog, like [Zendesk](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/zendesk) or [Intercom](/docs/data-pipelines/sources/intercom). [Connect your support data](/docs/data-pipelines/sources)
- Replay links in tickets, so each ticket points to a session. [Add replay links to tickets](/docs/session-replay/sharing#method-two-automatically-share-replays-to-intercom-or-crisp)

</Callout>

Support tickets work the same way. A ticket with an error behind it points at a bug the team can fix.

This joins tickets to exceptions on the session ID in each ticket's replay link:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| A tickets table, like `zendesk.tickets` | What the customer reported |
| `$exception` | The error behind it |

## People who hit the error

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Identified users, so it's clear who to contact. [Identify your users](/docs/getting-started/identify-users)

</Callout>

Once the engineers fix an issue, they might want to tell the people the issue impacts, or survey them. They create a <Term name="cohort" /> from the issue page, then [duplicate it as a static cohort](/docs/data/cohorts#can-you-use-a-dynamic-cohort-as-a-feature-flag-target) to target them with a survey or flag.

This joins exceptions to people on `distinct_id`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `$exception` | The issue each person hit |
| `persons` | Who they are, so the team can reach them |

## Experiments

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Feature flags sent with events, so it's clear which variant each person saw. [Send flags with your events](/docs/feature-flags/adding-feature-flag-code)

</Callout>

When the next change ships as an <Term name="experiment" />, check that it doesn't add errors. Add `$exception` as a [secondary metric](/docs/experiments/metrics#primary-and-secondary-metrics) to compare errors across variants.

To see when the errors started, chart each variant's error rate <SeeFig n={4} />. When the traffic split isn't even, compare a ratio, like exceptions per pageview, rather than raw counts. A variant on 5% of traffic then compares fairly with one on 95%. The Feature Flags pocket guide covers [running the experiment](/learn/feature-flags/pocket-guide/flag-to-experiment).

This joins exceptions to variants on `$feature/<flag-key>`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `$exception` | Errors in each variant |
| `$pageview` | Traffic in each variant, for the rate |

## Crash-free sessions

<Callout title="Prerequisites">

- Crash reporting in an <img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/Android_robot_bec2fb7318.svg" alt="" className="inline-block size-4 align-text-bottom" /> [Android](/docs/error-tracking/installation/android), <img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/posthog.com/contents/images/docs/integrate/ios.svg" alt="" className="inline-block size-4 align-text-bottom dark:invert" /> [iOS](/docs/error-tracking/installation/ios), or <img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/posthog.com/contents/images/docs/integrate/react.svg" alt="" className="inline-block size-4 align-text-bottom" /> [React Native](/docs/error-tracking/installation/react-native) app.

</Callout>

To watch an app's stability across all these changes, track its crash-free rate. It's the share of sessions without a fatal exception.

This joins sessions to fatal exceptions on `$session_id`:

| Source | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `sessions` | Every session |
| `$exception` with a `fatal` level | Which sessions crashed |
