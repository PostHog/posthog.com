# Find the errors your best accounts hit

Error Tracking can sort <Term name="issue">issues</Term> by occurrences, users, or sessions. None of those says which accounts pay you, so an issue that four of your paying accounts keep hitting can rank below a free account's retry loop.

Each <Term name="exception" /> is a `$exception` <Term name="event" />, stored in PostHog with the rest of your product and business data. When your events carry the account that sent them through [group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics), a query can join an issue to the accounts that hit it and check which of them pay you. [Measure impact with your other data](/learn/error-tracking/pocket-guide/measure-impact) shows how to write that join in SQL.

This <Term name="scout" /> runs the join on a schedule and reports the issues that concentrate in your paying accounts.

## What the scout looks at

`TypeError: Cannot read properties of null` fired 2,340 times this week across 31 accounts, and it isn't in your top ten issues by occurrences. Four of those accounts pay you, and together they send 9% of your traffic. Which is it?

- If the four accounts carry about 9% of the issue, it's spread evenly across your accounts. The scout stays _quiet_.
- If they carry 71% of it, the issue is concentrated in accounts that pay you. The scout _**reports**_.

The scout looks for the second case <SeeFig n={1} />.

The scout measures each account's share of the issue and share of your traffic over the last seven days, and it only reports an issue that fired in the last 24 hours. It also stays quiet when one account is the whole issue because a single client keeps retrying. PostHog's error tracking scout watches for stuck loops like that, and for broad regressions spread evenly across your users. See [Catch error spikes and stuck loops](/learn/error-tracking/pocket-guide/errors-as-signals).

## The report in your inbox

When an issue concentrates in paying accounts, the scout files a <Term name="report" /> in your <Term name="inbox" />. <SeeFig n={2} /> breaks the card down part by part.

The report names the issue and the accounts, with their share of the issue next to their share of your traffic. It also says what the scout can't measure. Whether an account pays you is a stand-in for impact, so a paying account that hits an error on a page nobody uses still counts.

If the issue keeps firing, the next run updates the same report instead of filing a new one.

## The pull request you merge

An agent investigates each report. When there's a clear code change to make, the report is actionable and the agent opens a pull request with the report and its evidence as context. You review and merge it like any other pull request.

Reports are free. Pull requests are **$15 each**, and your first three each month are **free**. See self-driving [pricing](/docs/self-driving/pricing).

## The scout itself

A scout is a file of instructions that an agent follows on a schedule. This one is a custom scout, and its file is in the [self-driving pocket guide](/docs/self-driving/pocket-guide/errors-your-best-accounts-hit).

It reads three kinds of data in PostHog:

- **Error Tracking** for every exception from the last seven days, grouped by issue and by the account that hit it.
- **Your product events** for every event each account sent over the same seven days, which gives that account's share of your traffic.
- **Customer analytics** for your accounts, matched to your events by group key. An account with a Stripe customer ID counts as paying.

## The schedule it runs on

The scout runs **daily**. You can change that to any interval from every 30 minutes to every 30 days, or set a cron expression. It only writes when a live issue concentrates in paying accounts, so most days it files nothing.

## Add this to your scout troop

The scout needs exceptions reaching PostHog and [group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) set up, so each exception carries the account that hit it. It also needs your accounts in Customer analytics, keyed by the same group key your events carry, with a Stripe customer ID on each account that pays you.

The **Add this scout** button in the self-driving pocket guide opens PostHog with the scout prefilled for you to review and create.
