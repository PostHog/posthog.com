**"Do more weird"** is a stated PostHog company value.<Ref id="2" /> It is also a documented practice with its own handbook page, which the handbook opens by conceding that for some, and possibly all, of the company it is "not just a benign corporate value" but "a way of life. A craft, to be honed."<Ref id="1" />

## The thesis

The handbook states the reasoning plainly: PostHog's competition for attention is not other business software companies, but the internet as a whole – memes, TikTok, Hacker News. The company's response is to bring a consumer mindset to a business context.<Ref id="1" />

Two clauses in that statement do most of the work. The practice aims at awareness of the brand among product engineers at high-growth companies, and the handbook says explicitly that it is *not* trying to make people sign up.<Ref id="1" /> A marketing activity with no conversion target is unusual enough to be worth recording.

## Rules

The handbook sets out the conditions under which weirdness is permitted.

- **It is a numbers game.** About 95 per cent of ideas are expected to go nowhere, and of the ones attempted only some will succeed.<Ref id="1" />
- **It must be genuinely entertaining.** The stated test is whether the author would enthusiastically share it with a friend.<Ref id="1" />
- **Feedback must address the idea, not the feasibility.** The handbook states that weird ideas are fragile, so the useful question is "do I think this is a good idea" and not "can we do it".<Ref id="1" />

## Recorded examples

The handbook lists past output. A selection:

- **[DeskHog](/deskhog)**, an open-source developer toy.<Ref id="3" />
- A hot dog cart taken to a conference.
- Bus advertising in San Francisco.
- Action figures of a co-founder, with an accompanying advertisement.
- A fake boardroom, with a fake board meeting, at a conference.
- The Hacker News feed placed inside the PostHog setup wizard.
- A [data processing agreement generator](/dpa) with fairy tale, pop-star, and Gen Z slang versions.<Ref id="5" />
- A feet pics page, which the handbook notes ranked on Google for "feet pics" for a period.
- **[Hedgehog Mode](/sparks-joy/hedgehog-mode)**, with an accompanying documentary.<Ref id="4" />

The handbook also records that PostHog Desktop was made to say "meep".<Ref id="1" />

## Controversy

The handbook devotes a section to what the practice is not, which amounts to a list of the ways it fails.<Ref id="1" />

**Corporate try-hard.** Described as "how do you do fellow kids" energy. The handbook states the company knows it when it sees it.

**"Marketing team do some work."** Having an idea for someone else to execute is permitted; expecting them to execute it is not, because other teams have their own weird ideas.

**In-jokes.** The handbook is specific that the output must be funny to a person who does not work at PostHog, and proposes a test: describe it to a friend, a partner, or a stranger, and see whether they laugh.

That last rule is the interesting one, because it disqualifies most of what a company finds funny about itself. This encyclopedia does not claim to pass it.
