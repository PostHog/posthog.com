The **history of PostHog** begins on 23 January 2020, when James Hawkins and Tim Glaser founded the company.<Ref id="1" /> Its primary source is the handbook page ["How we got here"](/handbook/story), which PostHog maintains in public.

## Before PostHog

The founders began working together on a startup in August 2019. Their first idea was a tool to help engineers manage technical debt. It did not succeed, but the handbook records two conclusions they drew from it: that growth can be treated as an engineering problem, and that many engineers have no way to see the effect of what they build.<Ref id="1" />

PostHog was their sixth idea. The handbook states that they had been pivoting roughly once a month for half a year.<Ref id="1" />

## Founding and first release

PostHog entered Y Combinator's W20 batch, and the handbook records that the founders realized within a couple of weeks of starting the batch that PostHog was what they should build.<Ref id="1" />

They released the first version on Hacker News four weeks after writing the first line of code. Within a couple of days there were more than 300 self-hosted deployments, and two weeks later the GitHub repository had passed 1,500 stars.<Ref id="1" />

The shape of that reception mattered to what the company became. The first users installed the software on their own servers, which is why the open-source distribution was not a marketing decision but the initial product.

## Funding

PostHog has raised six rounds.

| Round | Amount | Lead investor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seed | $3.025m | Y Combinator's Continuity Fund, 1984 Ventures |
| Series A | $9m | GV |
| Series B | $15m | Y Combinator's Continuity Fund |
| Series C | $10m | Y Combinator's Continuity Fund, GV |
| Series D | $70m | Stripe |
| Series E | $75m | Peak XV Partners |

The handbook gives the total raised since 2020 as approximately $194m as of October 2025, at a valuation of about $1.4bn.<Ref id="1" />

The handbook timeline narrates events as they happened and may date a round to its announcement; the press kit is the table PostHog gives to journalists.<Ref id="1" /><Ref id="2" /> For a specific date, read both.

## Growth

The handbook records the milestones the company chose to record, which is worth noting as a limitation: this is a company's account of itself.

- **April 2020.** The seed round closed and hiring began. The first engineers were Marius Andra, Eric Duong, and James Greenhill.<Ref id="3" /><Ref id="5" />
- **March 2023.** The company decided to build a data warehouse, which began its move from an analytics tool to a platform.<Ref id="1" />
- **December 2024.** It ended the year cashflow positive, which the handbook says was mostly unplanned.<Ref id="1" />
- **February 2025.** [AI observability](/hogpedia/ai-observability) was built to watch what PostHog AI was costing, then shipped as a product.<Ref id="1" />
- **June 2025.** Stripe led the Series D. The handbook records that the introduction began with a tweet from Patrick Collison about the PostHog website, about 18 months earlier.<Ref id="4" />
- **October 2025.** The Series E closed, and PostHog offered employees a secondary share sale.<Ref id="6" />
- **Early 2026.** PostHog released [PostHog Desktop](/desktop), which the handbook calls its biggest launch so far. Around the Series D the company stopped describing itself as an analytics company; its stated aim is now to make a product [self-driving](/self-driving).<Ref id="1" /><Ref id="7" />
