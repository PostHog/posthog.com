**PostHog** is an open-source platform for building software products. It combines [product analytics](/hogpedia/product-analytics), [session replay](/hogpedia/session-replay), [feature flags](/hogpedia/feature-flags), [experiments](/hogpedia/experiments), [error tracking](/hogpedia/error-tracking), surveys, web analytics, a data warehouse, and a customer data platform in a single tool.<Ref id="4" /> The company of the same name was founded by James Hawkins and Tim Glaser on 23 January 2020.<Ref id="1" />

PostHog is built for engineers who make product decisions themselves, a role the company calls a [product engineer](/hogpedia/product-engineering). Its core is open source and can be self-hosted, and it also sells a hosted cloud service with a free monthly allowance on every product.<Ref id="8" />

## Overview

A product team usually buys one tool to measure behaviour, a second to record sessions, a third to control feature releases, and a fourth to run tests. Each tool holds a separate copy of the user data, so a question that crosses two of them is hard to answer.

PostHog puts those tools on one event database. A single stream of events feeds every product, so the same user can be counted in a funnel, watched in a replay, targeted by a flag, and enrolled in an experiment without a data export. This is the company's main argument for itself, and it is the reason it describes its products as one platform rather than a suite.<Ref id="4" />

## History

PostHog began after its founders spent about half a year on other ideas. Their first idea was a tool to help engineers manage technical debt. PostHog was their sixth idea.<Ref id="1" />

The company entered Y Combinator's W20 batch and released its first version on Hacker News four weeks after the first line of code. The response produced more than 300 self-hosted deployments within days, and the GitHub repository passed 1,500 stars two weeks later.<Ref id="1" />

PostHog has raised money in six rounds. Investors include Y Combinator's Continuity Fund, GV, 1984 Ventures, Tapas Capital, Formus Capital, Stripe, and Peak XV Partners.<Ref id="1" /><Ref id="2" /> The handbook gives the total raised since 2020 as approximately $194m as of October 2025.<Ref id="1" />

The two PostHog sources give different dates and amounts for the early rounds. The handbook timeline dates the seed round to April 2020, the Series A to December 2020, and the Series B to June 2021.<Ref id="1" /> The press kit dates them to March 2020, July 2020, and March 2021, and it lists a $10m Series C that the timeline does not mention.<Ref id="2" /> This article does not resolve the difference. For a specific round, read both sources.

For the full timeline, see [History of PostHog](/hogpedia/history-of-posthog).

## Philosophy

PostHog states its mission as equipping every developer to build successful products.<Ref id="3" /> Three positions follow from it and are visible in how the company operates.

**The engineer makes the decision.** PostHog is designed for a person who writes the code and also decides what to build. This is why the tools sit together rather than in separate departments.

**Prices are published, and cut.** PostHog publishes its pricing and has reduced it more than once.<Ref id="8" />

**Write it down.** The company records decisions in public, in [the handbook](/hogpedia/the-posthog-handbook).<Ref id="5" />

## Open source

The PostHog application is open source and the repository is public.<Ref id="7" /> A team can run it on its own infrastructure, which was the original reason people adopted it: the first release attracted self-hosted deployments, not cloud sign-ups.<Ref id="1" />

The paid product is the hosted service. This is the usual arrangement for a commercial open-source company: the code is free, the operation of it is the product.

## Company culture

PostHog has been fully remote since it was founded, and it writes down decisions rather than hold meetings about them.<Ref id="5" /> Work is organised into small teams, which the handbook defines as strictly between two and six people, each owning a product area.<Ref id="6" />

The company also treats unusual behaviour as a value in its own right, under the name ["Do more weird"](/hogpedia/do-more-weird). For the internal jokes and traditions this produces, see [Category: Hog lore](/hogpedia/category/hog-lore).
