This page explains what covering the online community involves: what to watch, when to reply, and what to do when something goes wrong.

Almost nothing here is urgent. There is no response time target. If you are not sure about something, ask in <PrivateLink url="https://posthog.slack.com/archives/C0AB78YBCNA">#team-builder-relations</PrivateLink>.

> These processes are new, and we still change them often. Read this page as the current state, not as a settled policy.

## Community surfaces

Four spaces:

- **The forum**, at [posthog.com/forum](/forum)
- **Discord**
- **GitHub**, for pull requests from external contributors. See [external pull requests](#external-pull-requests).
- **Reddit**, at [r/posthog](https://www.reddit.com/r/posthog). See [Reddit](#reddit).

## Where to look

Slack shows everything for the forum and Discord. You do not need to open either one to see what is new. GitHub and Reddit are not in Slack, so check them directly.

## External pull requests

External contributors open real pull requests, and they deserve an answer. Our job is to make sure each one gets to a team that can review it. We do not review the code ourselves.

Look for open pull requests from external contributors that are not drafts, and that:

- Have no reviewer assigned, or
- Have had a reviewer assigned for more than a week with no reply

For now, ask Claude to find these for you. We're building a dashboard that will show them in one place.

For each one:

1. Find the team that owns the code it changes.
2. Assign a reviewer from that team, or ping the team in their Slack channel if you are not sure who.
3. If a reviewer has been assigned for more than a week, nudge them on the pull request or in Slack.

> All we ask of the reviewer is a reply: "useful, here's why", "not a fit, here's why", or "leaving it open to see if others want it".

## Reddit

Look at [r/posthog](https://www.reddit.com/r/posthog) when you check the other spaces. It's quiet, so this takes a minute. When you see a new post, acknowledge it or reply. If it needs a team, flag it to them in their Slack channel.

## How often to check

Two or three times each day is enough.

We aren't alone in these channels:

- Other PostHog product teams are in Discord (but you might need to ping in Slack to help them notice a specific post).
- Members of the Array (hand-picked community leaders) help. Ask them in the `#the-array` Discord channel, or mention the `@champion` role.
- Community members often answer each other.

If we answer nothing for two days, that's fine. We just don't want anything going a week without any interactions, especially if a user seems to be blocked on something.

## When to reply

Most of the Discord feed does not need an answer. Have fun with it – there's no PostHog line you need to toe. Be yourself, and talk with the other founders and builders there.

- **Chatter.** A person writes "morning hogs", or something like it. Join the conversation, or add a reaction. This is the community working, not a waste of your time.
- **A question you can answer.** Answer it.
- **A question you cannot answer.** See [if you do not know the answer](#if-you-do-not-know-the-answer).

## Where to send people

If it's troubleshooting or clarifying how something works, encourage them to ask PostHog AI, as it will have more context around their project. This is the same rule as in [community channels](/handbook/community/channels#pull-support-out).

On the forum, check that the tag is set to the team that owns the product mentioned in the post. The control is below the post, in the moderator tools. The tag puts the thread into that team's support Slack channel, so the team can see it. A tag does not make that team answer. If you want a reply, write in the team's Slack channel too.

## If you do not know the answer

Use the `answering-community-questions` skill in PostHog AI (in the builder relations space). Type `/answering` and select it, then give it the URL of the thread or the text of the question. It searches our public content, the person's own project data, GitHub, and Slack, and gives you material for your reply. Do not copy and paste a response. Use it to inform what you can say to them, offer troubleshooting advice and help them debug, or help you figure out which team can best help answer the question.

## Escalating to a team

Escalate and follow up when a person is truly stuck and cannot continue with their work.

1. Copy the link to the forum post or the Discord message.
2. Get the user's email (if on the forum, you can see it in their profile; if in Discord, ask them to DM you).
3. Create a new support ticket. You can follow a <PrivateLink url="https://us.posthog.com/project/2/support/tickets/75872">format like this ticket</PrivateLink>:

   > Hey there, we just created a ticket for you on behalf of your community post (LINK):
   >
   > [COPY THE TEXT OF THE POST, OR AT LEAST A QUICK SUMMARY]
   >
   > We'll take a look and respond here.

## Heated topics

Sometimes a group of people disagree with a decision, and the thread gets hot. An example: people want bring-your-own-key, and they are angry that we do not have it.

Do not defend the position yourself. Ask the product owner for that area to write the current thinking in the thread. They know what changed and what is planned. A first-hand answer ends the thread. A second-hand answer extends it.

A hot thread is not an incident. People who disagree with us are still welcome.

## Spam, abuse, and hateful content

This is the most urgent thing that tends to happen and needs to be addressed immediately.

### Act first, write it down after

If a message contains hateful content, targeted abuse, spam, or leaked credentials, remove it before you do anything else.

- **On the forum**, hide the reply or archive the thread. See [moderator tools](/handbook/community/moderator-tools).
- **In Discord**, delete the message. Use a timeout if the person is only heated. Use a ban if the person acts in bad faith.

Then say what you did in <PrivateLink url="https://posthog.slack.com/archives/C0AB78YBCNA">#team-builder-relations</PrivateLink>.

### Ban immediately, or use the ladder

Ban on sight for bad faith:

- Hateful content of any kind
- Abuse aimed at a person
- Spam and unsolicited promotion
- A person who says they abused a PostHog endpoint, account, or credit allowance

For anything else, use the four steps in the [code of conduct](/docs/contribute/code-of-conduct#enforcement-guidelines): correction, warning, temporary ban, permanent ban. A first mistake that looks careless starts at correction. A first mistake that looks hostile does not.

Always say why, and how to appeal. [Bans and grievances](/handbook/community/channels#bans-and-grievances) has the mechanics for Discord and for PostHog organizations.

## People who help you

Two Discord channels hold members that we selected by hand:

- `#the-array`
- `#forum-cleanup` (same members, just conversation focused around content in the forum)

You can rely on these members. They join questions and conversations when they can help.

## Routine maintenance

There is almost none, but we may dig into issues if something unexpected happens.

- Point redemptions are self-serve. You can monitor them in the `#community-rewards` channel. See [points & rewards](/handbook/community/points).
- Reports and digests run automatically. Nothing to pull.
- For sending merch, see the [merch store](/handbook/company/merch-store) page.
- If you see another PostHog teammate responding to a question or engaging on a post, give them a shoutout in `#community-kudos`.
