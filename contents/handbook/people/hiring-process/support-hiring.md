## Technical Support Engineer hiring at PostHog

Our [Support team](/teams/support) helps customers across all of PostHog's products. We work in the app and in shared Slack channels with our larger customers. Most of our customers are engineers, so the person who helps them must be technical too.

A Technical Support Engineer (TSE) owns their tickets from start to finish. A TSE also ships fixes to the PostHog code and docs, becomes the support expert for a product area, and helps improve our support tooling and workflows. Please check our [careers page](/careers) for our open roles, and read more about [how the Support team works](/handbook/support/support-team).

### What we are looking for in Technical Support Engineer hires

Outside of the skills listed in the job description, we are generally looking for:

*   Engineering ability - you are an engineer who does support. You can work across code, SQL, APIs, and systems.
*   Strong debugging skills - you can investigate a problem you have never seen before, in a careful way, and check that your fix works.
*   Fast learning - you pick up new tools and products quickly, and you are comfortable when nobody knows the answer yet.
*   Care for the customer - you care about how a customer feels when the conversation ends, not only that the ticket is closed.
*   Clear writing - you explain a complex fix in plain words and do not bury the customer in options.
*   Good judgment - you know when to keep digging and when to ask for help. You prioritize well across a busy queue.
*   A genuine wish to do support - you want this type of work, and you want to make it better.
*   Humility – you take feedback well, say when you don’t know something yet, and learn from anyone on the team.

### How we evaluate candidates

We care more about attitude and aptitude than your previous job title. The hardest thing to teach is the “I can figure this out” mindset. We want people who are curious, unafraid of technology they haven’t seen before, and are quick to pick it up.

You don’t need to know every language. We look for a technical background that transfers, whether that’s from a development role, side projects, a bootcamp, or somewhere else, and for someone who can explain what they find clearly. People on our team came to support from a range of backgrounds.

We also think every support ticket means something failed somewhere. The app wasn’t clear, the docs were missing something, or there was a bug. So we look for people who want to fix the cause as well as answer the ticket.

### Technical Support Engineer hiring process

#### Culture interview

This is our usual first-round interview with a member of the Talent team.

#### Technical interview

The technical interview with [Abigail](/community/profiles/31138) (team lead) usually lasts 45 minutes. There is no live coding.

The interview checks how you work as a technical person in a customer-facing role. We talk about how you investigate problems, how you learn new things, how you decide when to ask for help, how you prioritize, and how you use AI. We also want to understand why you want a support role.

#### Culture and motivation interview

In this 30-minute interview, you will meet one of our execs. They will try to answer: "Are they a good cultural fit for PostHog?".

#### Technical Support Engineer SuperDay

The final stage of our interview process is what we call a PostHog [SuperDay](/handbook/people/hiring-process#4-posthog-superday). This is a paid full day of work (1,000 USD), which we can flexibly arrange around your schedule.

If you've been invited to a PostHog [SuperDay](/handbook/people/hiring-process#4-posthog-superday), here's what to expect and how to set yourself up for success.

##### What the SuperDay looks like

You get the challenge at the start of your day and send your work at the end. It’s a mix of what a TSE does here: support tickets, technical work, and some bigger questions about how we run support and where it goes next. There’s more than anyone could finish in a day, on purpose. Choose what’s most valuable to work on and take each thing as far as you can.

The brief won’t tell you everything, so you’ll need to ask questions. You won’t be penalized for asking.

During the day, you will also have:

*   A kick-off session (30 min) - a call to go over the challenge for the day.
*   A peer team interview (30 min) - a chat with members of the team. You can ask them as many questions as you want.

You’ll have a Slack channel with the team. Use it like a normal team channel for questions, progress, and blockers. Please don’t send direct messages.

##### How to prepare for the day

*   Dig into the product. You will be expected to know PostHog well when you start, so install it and use it before the day.
*   Create some test data and try the main products, such as product analytics, session replay, feature flags, and experiments.
*   Read the [Support section of the handbook](/handbook/support/support-team) and our [docs](/docs).

##### During the day

*   Communicate early. Share your progress and your blockers in Slack, and ask questions when something is not clear.
*   Divide your time across the whole challenge.
*   Work as you would on a normal day with real customers.

> In line with our [values](/handbook/values) and [culture](/handbook/company/culture), you might get short replies like "step on toes" or "bias for action".

##### A note on AI tools

You can use AI tools during the day. We know this is how many TSEs work. But we want to see real, human communication. We also want to see how well you use AI, and where you choose not to.

##### What comes next

After the SuperDay, everyone involved will leave their feedback. We aim to give you a decision within a few days at most.
