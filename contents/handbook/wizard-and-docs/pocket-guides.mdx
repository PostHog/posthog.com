Pocket guides are our **learn with a story** format. They're for people who'd rather follow a team making product decisions than read a list of features.

The [Product Analytics Learn page](/docs/product-analytics/learn) and its [pocket guide](/docs/product-analytics/learn/introduction) show what we're aiming for. The Learn page offers a few ways in: learn with an agent, read the story, or try things in Twig. The guide opens inside the product's Learn tab, with chapters in the docs navigation. Readers don't have to leave that experience to keep going.

## What makes it a pocket guide?

In the Product Analytics guide, Twig's engineers start with a small question: which destinations do visitors choose? They add an event, connect activity to people and sessions, and use trends and funnels to understand what happens next. Each chapter picks up where the last one left off.

That's the model for future guides:

- **Follow a product question.** Show what the team wants to know, what they can see in PostHog, and what they decide to do with that evidence.
- **Teach concepts when the story needs them.** Explain an event, session, or insight as it becomes useful. Give readers enough context to stay in the guide, with links to docs when they want to go deeper.
- **Let readers see the evidence.** Put Twig views, PostHog figures, and small interactions beside the moment they're discussed. The Product Analytics examples use local teaching data, not live bookings or events sent to a PostHog project.
- **Make it easy to keep reading.** Use the cover, chapter navigation, and next-chapter links in the shared Learn reader. A guide can have a different number of chapters, but it should feel like one connected story.

The Product Analytics guide is the reference for the premise and design. A guide about another product will have different questions, figures, Twig components, and actions for readers to take. It doesn't need to copy a fixed "101, then use cases" outline or end every chapter with the same kind of button.

## Plan the story before writing chapters

Start with what you want someone to understand or do by the end. Then ask: what product question would make them care about learning this?

1. **Pick an example product and a question.** Twig gives us a shared product to follow, so readers don't have to imagine the app or its users. Look for a natural action in Twig that the PostHog product can help explain.
2. **Sketch the journey.** List the questions the team asks, the evidence they inspect, and the decisions they make. Give each chapter one step in that journey. The Product Analytics guide moves from recording an action to connecting it to a person, then counting what people do.
3. **Choose what readers will see.** Use a focused Twig view or PostHog figure where it helps someone understand the evidence. Make any simulated data or behavior clear. The figure should answer a question in the prose, not just decorate the page.
4. **Choose the next step.** A chapter might lead to the next question, offer a prompt or link, or give someone an action to try. Let the lesson decide the call to action. Ask the relevant product team to check that the PostHog concepts and examples are right.

## Put the guide together

The story lives in `contents/pocket-guides/<volume>/`. Each chapter has an `index.mdx` file. A short block at the top sets its title and reading order. Use the Product Analytics [introduction](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog.com/blob/master/contents/pocket-guides/product-analytics/index.mdx) and [Events and properties chapter](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog.com/blob/master/contents/pocket-guides/product-analytics/101/index.mdx) as examples of the story's shape.

For component syntax and MDX details, use the [`PocketGuides` README](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog.com/blob/master/src/components/PocketGuides/README.md). Scout guides have extra rules in the [`SelfDrivingInbox` README](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog.com/blob/master/src/components/SelfDrivingInbox/README.md).

Keep the work in the right place:

| Repo | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| `posthog.com` | The guide's story, teaching examples, and PostHog figures |
| [`twig-components`](https://github.com/PostHog/twig-components) | Reusable Twig views, styles, and local lab UI |
| [`twig.com`](https://github.com/PostHog/twig.com) | The Twig site, its interactions, and real PostHog instrumentation |

You can use Twig in a guide without adding new instrumentation to Twig.com. If you need a reusable Twig view, build it in `twig-components` so the guide and Twig can share it. The [Twig contribution guide](/handbook/wizard-and-docs/twig) explains that split in more detail.

When the guide is ready, read it from the Learn page through the last chapter. Check the story, links, and figures in a narrow and wide window, in light and dark mode. Try each interaction and make sure the example still makes sense without it.
