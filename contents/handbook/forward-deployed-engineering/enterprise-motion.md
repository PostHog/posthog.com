FDE can already help close some deals through our standard paid packages, such as [Managed Migrations and PostHog Foundations 0-1](/handbook/forward-deployed-engineering/how-to-get-fde-involved#self-serve-and-price-our-standard-packages). Enterprise deals go further: we work alongside Technical Account Executives (TAEs) before the contract is signed, on technical work that isn't a good fit for them.

For FDE, an enterprise account is a company with approximately 1,500 or more employees. For every other account, the usual [ICP](/handbook/forward-deployed-engineering/who-we-work-with) and [intake path](/handbook/forward-deployed-engineering/how-to-get-fde-involved) apply.

FDEs are a limited resource, so we only take on work where nobody else on the deal can help. We're not sales engineers, and we don't replace them.

## Where FDE adds value

- **Architecture reviews and deployment scoping.** The prospect has a hard technical question that decides whether PostHog fits, such as how to deploy PostHog consistently and implement identity resolution across multiple products. We work through it with their engineers and come back with a recommended setup.
- **Supporting a POC from the start.** Bringing us in at the start of a POC stops avoidable setup mistakes from dragging it out, and gives the prospect a robust production setup they can expand from.
- **Managed migrations.** The prospect is replacing another tool, often against a renewal deadline. We own the migration so the switch lands on time, and the deal doesn't depend on their engineering bandwidth. It also gives the TAE a concrete offer to put in the contract.
- **Implementation audits.** After the sale, we review how PostHog is set up and fix what will cause problems later. This also applies to customers who started on self-serve and grew into an enterprise account.
- **Bridging the business buyer and the technical team.** The person who buys PostHog is often not the person who implements it. We translate what the buyer wants into a plan their engineers can deliver. If most of the stakeholders are non-technical, such as a marketing-led team, a small advisory and implementation package gets them live.

## Where FDE doesn't add value

- **Demos and first discovery calls.** Sales handles these well without us.
- **Standard pre-sales work.** If a TAE can answer the question, they should.

A good test: if the prospect's question needs someone to read their code, design their setup, or do the work, it's FDE-shaped. If it needs someone to explain what PostHog does, it isn't.

## How to bring us in early

The earlier we are involved in an enterprise deal, the more we can help close it. Don't wait until the technical questions block the deal.

- **Loop us into the deal thread.** Share the account in a dedicated channel or thread with the FDE, so we can follow along and iterate together.
- **Add us as a technical contact.** When a demo call surfaces a deep technical question, copy an FDE into the follow-up email instead of answering it secondhand.
- **Bring one or two specific use cases.** "FDE could help with X" lands better with a prospect than a general offer of help.

## How we charge for pre-sale work

Scoping is [free](/handbook/forward-deployed-engineering/how-we-work#what-it-costs), but the work itself is paid, including before the sale. There are three common ways to structure it:

- **A paid technical assessment during the POC.** A short engagement that covers an architecture review, scoping, and setup recommendations. We can also do hands-on delivery of the setup where required.
- **Bundled into the enterprise contract.** FDE fees sit alongside the platform spend in the same deal.
- **Paid with prepaid credits.** Customers with a large credit balance can use some of it for FDE time. See [what it costs](/handbook/forward-deployed-engineering/how-we-work#what-it-costs) for the approval rule.

Pricing for each of these lives in the <PrivateLink url="https://fde-engagement-menu.hosthog.dev/">FDE Menu of Services</PrivateLink>. The TAE still owns the commercial relationship, as with every [FDE engagement](/handbook/forward-deployed-engineering/working-with-sales-and-cs).
