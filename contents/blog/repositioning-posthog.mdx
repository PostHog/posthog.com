As PostHog enters its [next chapter](https://posthog.com/blog/posthogs-next-chapter), we have spent the last 6 weeks going all in on repositioning PostHog as the company that makes your product self-driving. This is how we are specifically doing it, and how it's going. If you are trying to position or reposition your product, hopefully this will help you!

**‘Repositioning’ sounds like a marketing buzzword - what does it actually mean?**

‘Positioning’ is when you set the context in which you want your product to be seen through the eyes of customers. You’re trying to get across [how your product is the best in the world](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/an-introduction-to-positioning) at doing something that your ICP actually cares about.

‘Repositioning’ is when you change your positioning (woah). It’s very normal to regularly review your positioning as the world around you changes. 

**What is PostHog doing?**

We originally positioned PostHog as open source product analytics for developers back in 2020. Over time, we’ve expanded to position ourselves as a suite of tools that product engineers use to build better products. Over the years we've used terms like ‘Product OS’ and 'customer data infrastructure' that tried to capture this. 

Despite this, most people still see PostHog as ‘analytics++’ today even though we make money from a lot of different products. In part this was because we did our repositioning incrementally, but mostly it’s because repositioning is just _hard_, especially if you don't go all in. 

**So what’s the deal with self-driving?**

Self-driving is now how we are positioning ourselves. We are going all in on this in an _uncomfortable_ way - this means putting it front and center on our website, but also focusing on it in [content](https://newsletter.posthog.com/p/why-were-bullish-on-loops), [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PfWVVI9ALd8), [influencers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0eHyEkZV2wY), [events](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:ugcPost:7479840334856843265/) etc.

> We will probably confuse some people for a bit. This might hurt signups. We are willing to tolerate some short term pain, because we believe our long term success (and revenue!) depends on convincing people that you build _with_ PostHog, not add it on afterwards. 

**What are the specific words PostHog is using to describe stuff?**

You can see more in [our public handbook](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/foundations#the-standard-description), but here’s the tl;dr:

- **PostHog** makes your product self-driving.   
- **Products** are the surfaces a customer adopts to access self-driving. Today that's **PostHog Web**, **PostHog Slack**, **PostHog MCP**, **PostHog CLI**, and **PostHog Desktop**. **PostHog Mobile** is coming.   
- **Tools** are the capabilities you access through the products: product analytics, session replay, feature flags, experiments, error tracking, surveys, web analytics, and so on. Tools can be as granular as annotations or comments even.  
- **Context** is the data that feeds the self-driving loop: events, recordings, errors, and logs from PostHog, plus other business data (Slack, code, Notion, support tickets etc.)  
- **Context warehouse** is the managed data warehouse plus the full context-ingestion pipeline (modelling, data pipelines, batch exports, and so on).

**Why are you inventing new concepts like ‘self-driving’ and ‘context warehouse’? Doesn't that make your job harder?**

When a term has been widely adopted in the industry, we will use that term - we would not try and redefine ‘MCP’ as something else for example. 

However, ‘self-driving’ and ‘context warehouse’ describe things that don’t really exist today, or where there isn’t consensus yet. You may have heard the concept of ‘self-healing’ or ‘self-improving’ software for example, but these are very far from industry standards. 

We have to work really hard to educate people about these new terms, to the point of feeling like we’re repeating ourselves too much. We're confident we can succeed because we have some prior experience helping to popularize the concept of ‘product engineer’. We didn’t invent the term, but were one of the earliest people to hire product engineers, [wrote about it](https://posthog.com/product-engineer/what-is-a-product-engineer) a bunch (first page on Google when you search the term), and now it feels pretty standard. 

**So what market is PostHog playing in now if it’s not analytics? Who do we see as our competition?**

We’re now positioning ourselves as part of the market for autonomous AI systems. We don’t have a perfect competitor in terms of overlap (some are _reasonably_ close if you squint), but the *truest* competitor for our ICP that we should care about is the old way of working. Triaging errors, watching replays back, writing the PR yourself, re-prompting an agent for every change, connecting your warehouse to your analytics to your coding tool - these are the habits that we are offering an alternative to.

**Are you open to changing your minds about this?**

Maybe one day! But not right now - internally, we’ve had plenty of rounds of feedback and we’ve all had to disagree and commit to some extent to get this out there. Debating product names is the perfect way to distract your entire team for hours!

Part of repositioning is that you _really_ have to commit to it for several months to see if it’s landing. And of course, we’ll change course if things look like they’re going wrong. Early signs are promising from some of our best customers, and people are starting to talk about it on places like X. But it’s only been a few weeks, and the majority of mentions still talk about us in the context of analytics. We'll get there!

As has always been the case, we will know whether we need to change course by talking to our users and [making something they want](https://paulgraham.com/good.html). 

**This was mildly useful, but I'd appreciate a book recommendation.**

There are a million books about positioning. They are all compromised in some way. I speed ran April Dunford's process in a week from [Obviously Awesome](https://www.aprildunford.com/books), and it's worked well enough for us!
