Fivetran moves data from your business tools into a warehouse you already own. PostHog moves it into a warehouse that comes with the product, and lets you query it next to the events your app already sends.

That difference decides most of this comparison. If you have Snowflake, dbt, and a data team, Fivetran slots into a stack you have already built. If you are trying to answer "which customers actually convert" without assembling that stack first, PostHog is a shorter path.

## Connectors

PostHog has over 1,300 sources. Fivetran has around 700.

The raw number flatters PostHog more than it should. A few dozen of ours are hand-built and documented, including the ones most teams connect first: Stripe, HubSpot, Salesforce, Postgres, and the ad platforms. The rest are REST connectors covering the long tail of tools that never make a vendor catalog. They work, but they have not had the years of production traffic Fivetran's have.

If you need a specific connector to be rock solid on day one, check whether it is one of ours with [its own docs page](/docs/data-warehouse/sources). If it is not in either catalog, both products let you build your own.

## Pricing

Fivetran prices on monthly active rows. A row counts once per month however many times it changed, which sounds generous until a table with a lot of churn moves you into a higher tier you did not plan for. Teams routinely describe the bill as hard to predict.

PostHog prices per row synced. The first million each month are free, and the first seven days of historical backfill on a new source are free, so connecting a large table does not land on your first invoice.

Writing rows onward to your own warehouse is billed separately from syncing them in. [ETL pricing](/docs/etl/pricing) has the worked example.

## What you get once the data lands

This is the real split.

Fivetran delivers rows to your warehouse and stops. That is the job, and it does it well. You supply Snowflake or BigQuery, something to model the data, and something to visualize it.

PostHog includes the warehouse. Once a table is synced you can query it in SQL, join it to your product analytics events, and put it on a dashboard without buying anything else. You can also write the same rows onward to your own Postgres, Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Databricks, S3, or Azure Blob, so choosing PostHog does not trap the data here.

## Where Fivetran is the better answer

We would rather say this than have you find out later.

- **Your warehouse is the centre of your stack.** If dbt, an orchestrator, and a BI tool are already running, PostHog is one more source to you, not a replacement for the pipeline.
- **You need a connector we do not have, this quarter.** Fivetran's catalog is smaller but older, and their support for an individual connector is deeper.
- **You have data engineers who want lineage, schema drift alerts, and transformation orchestration.** We do not compete on that.

## Where PostHog is the better answer

- **You want business data next to product data.** Revenue against activation is one query here and a pipeline somewhere else with Fivetran.
- **You want a bill you can predict.** Per row synced, with a free million, beats monthly active rows for most teams under serious volume.
- **You do not want to buy a warehouse to use a pipeline.** PostHog includes one.
- **You want to know when a sync breaks.** The [ETL page](/docs/etl/monitoring) opens on what stopped, with the error and a link to the source.
