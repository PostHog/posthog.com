'Do more weird' is an actual PostHog [company value](/handbook/values#do-more-weird), and we have a discretionary $45k/mo budget that anyone can use to bring weird ideas to life. 'Operationalizing weirdness' sounds like an oxymoron that some soul-sucking MBA came up with (hey), but there are a ton of ways you can practically inject more weirdness into your company. 

Let's get weird. 

## Why we do more weird in the first place

There are two answers to this. 

The first is tactical - if you work at a startup, you are probably competing with much larger, better-known companies who can easily outspend you. Even if you are an extremely well-funded company, when you're going up against a publicly traded giant, your resources are probably minuscule in comparison. Sure you need to do all the regular growth playbook stuff, but to actually have an edge and stand out, you need to get weird. Take the example of a good old-fashioned marketing blog. Your competition here isn't your competitor's (presumably lame) blog, your competition is everything else that occupies your audience's attention on the internet - TikTok, online shopping, every good independent writer on Substack etc. Weirdness helps you cut through the noise. 

The second answer is more philosophical - the average person is just really weird! Don't believe me? Imagine if you just said every passing thought that was in your head out loud in an office - you probably wouldn't stick around for very long. Given people have so much weirdness swirling around in their brains anyway, why not harness that energy and make the places we work a little bit more fun? Sorry, I mean 'why not harness that energy and cynically exploit it in the service of shareholder value'.

## Remember weirdness is relative

Sometimes the thing you want to do just isn't _that_ weird, but that may still be ok depending on the context! For example, transparent pricing is weird in the context of how we bill customers, but it wouldn't make sense to do something truly weird like bartering grain for PostHog credit or something.

On the other hand, the bar for weirdness in a marketing campaign is extremely high, because the world is full of marketing teams trying to do the same thing. A wry smile in response to the idea will not cut it. 

Perhaps something in the middle would be a small easter egg in the settings page of your app. Weirdness exists on a spectrum. 

## How to do weird wrong

Before we do weird right, first we must avoid doing it wrong:

1. Don't make weirdness your marketing team's responsibility. They may be the _primary_ facilitators, but if weirdness is delegated to your marketing team, you'll eventually gravitate to a bunch of corporate try hard. 
2. Make the weirdness coherent with your brand. If you have a brand that feels very premium and high quality, weirdness may be an unusual spin on your sales process that delights customers, or a unique take on an event. 
3. In jokes are funny internally. They are not funny to your users. If you're sure, ask a friend externally who matches your ICP - 'I hate it' is ok, but 'I don't get it' is not. 
4. Don't forget that it's a numbers game. If you pitch a few ideas or ship something that doesn't land, don't give up! What you see at PostHog is just a tiny fraction of the many terrible ideas we've had internally. 

## How to operationalize weird

Assuming you are bought in and excited to do weird stuff, there are a few specific things we do at PostHog to help weird ideas become a real, shipped thing. 

We have a dedicated `#do-more-weird` Slack channel where anyone can propose any idea, no matter how ludicrous. If nobody engages with your idea, chances are it wasn't a good one - and that's ok!

Once a month, I have an automated skill scrape the last 30 days of ideas and pitch which ones should get support and why. It takes into account how much they are likely to cost, the effort, and how enthusiastic the response from the team was. It uses [this handbook page](/handbook/company/do-more-weird) as context for what makes a good candidate. A small (2-3 person) group then reviews the pitches and decides how to spend the money. 

Of course, many weird things don't actually require money - in those cases, we just encourage the person who pitched it to do the thing! There's nothing like basking in the warm glow of your team mates for shipping something like [DeskHog](/deskhog) or [Hedgehog Mode](/sparks-joy/hedgehog-mode). 

## What to do first

Start a `#do-more-weird` Slack channel. Add a diverse group of people to it who are interested in this stuff. Then copy our [playbook](/handbook/company/do-more-weird). That's it.
