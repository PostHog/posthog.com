If you're a Helicone user, you've probably seen the news: [Mintlify acquired it](https://www.helicone.ai/blog/joining-mintlify) in March 2026, and the platform is now in maintenance mode. It keeps running, it gets security updates and new model support, but the roadmap is frozen.

That's not an emergency – nothing is breaking tomorrow. But "no new features, ever" is a significant limitation in a space where the tools reinvent themselves every six months. Agent tracing, evals, OTel support: whatever the next must-have is, Helicone won't have it.

So whether you're planning a migration or just starting a new project, here are the best Helicone alternatives available today – what each does well, how it compares, and who it's actually for.

<details>
<summary>What does Helicone's maintenance mode mean for you?</summary>

For Helicone, maintenance mode means the platform still works and will keep getting security updates, bug fixes, and support for new models, but no new features.

You don't have to move right away. If Helicone does what you need, you can keep using it for now. Mintlify has said it will help existing customers move to other platforms if they choose to. The choice matters more if you're starting something new or planning for the next year or two.

If you do decide to move, look at how each tool fits into your workflow. Helicone uses a proxy-based approach, while many alternatives rely on SDKs and code-level instrumentation. Also consider the features you need the most, whether that's tracing, cost tracking, prompt management, evaluations, or something else.

</details>

## 1. PostHog
- **Founded:** 2020
- **Similar to:** Langfuse, LangSmith
- **Typical users:** Engineers and product teams
- **Typical customers:** Startups and SaaS companies

### What is PostHog?

**[PostHog](/)** (that's us 👋) is the leading platform for self-driving products. You can use our desktop ([Code](/code)), [web](/ai), [Slack](/slack), and [MCP](/mcp) products to leverage tools like [AI observability](/ai-observability), [product analytics](/product-analytics), [session replay](/session-replay), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [experiments](/experiments), [error tracking](/error-tracking), [logs](/logs), and more.

Companies like [Y Combinator](/customers/ycombinator), [Supabase](/customers/supabase), [Lovable](/customers/lovable), and Mistral AI use PostHog to understand how people use their products.

That means it is not just a Helicone alternative. Many teams also use it instead of separate tools for product analytics, feature management, and experimentation, so data from their AI features can be analyzed alongside the rest of their product data instead of in a separate dashboard.

### Key features

- **AI observability:** Track traces, token usage, costs, and latency across your AI applications so you can understand how they perform in production.
- **Product analytics:** See how people use your AI features, including adoption, engagement, retention, and conversion.
- **Session replay:** Watch user sessions to understand what happened before, during, and after an AI interaction.
- **Feature flags and experiments:** Roll out new models, prompts, and AI features gradually, then measure their impact before making them available to everyone.
- **Prompt management:** Create, version, and manage prompts without hardcoding them into your application.
- **Data warehouse:** Bring AI and product data together in one place so you can query and analyze it without moving data between tools.

### How does PostHog compare to Helicone?

PostHog covers the same core observability features as Helicone, including tracing, cost tracking, prompt management, and evaluations. The difference is that PostHog combines those features with product analytics, session replay, feature flags, and experiments.
Helicone uses a proxy-based approach, which usually means less setup and fewer code changes. PostHog is SDK-based, so migrating typically requires more work. PostHog also gives you a wider view of how AI features affect the rest of your product.

## 2. Langfuse

- **Founded:** 2023
- **Similar to:** LangSmith, Arize Phoenix
- **Typical users:** AI and ML engineers
- **Typical customers:** Startups to large enterprises

### What is Langfuse?
Langfuse is an open-source platform for building, monitoring, and improving LLM applications. It helps teams track traces, costs, and evaluations, and manage prompts. It can be used as a managed cloud service or self-hosted.
Langfuse is focused on LLM observability rather than a broader developer platform. In January 2026, it was acquired by ClickHouse and continues to be developed as an open-source project.

### Key features
- **LLM observability and tracing:** Track traces, costs, latency, token usage, and model outputs across your LLM applications.
- **Prompt management:** Create, version, and manage prompts outside your application code.
- **Evaluations:** Test and score model outputs to measure quality and catch issues before they reach users.
- **Datasets and experiments:** Run experiments against datasets to compare prompts, models, and changes over time.
- **Playground:** Test prompts and models in an interactive environment before deploying them to production.

### How does Langfuse compare to Helicone?
Both companies were acquired in 2026, but while Helicone moved into maintenance mode, Langfuse remains actively developed under ClickHouse and provides an [official migration guide for Helicone users](https://langfuse.com/faq/all/migrate-from-helicone).
| Feature | Langfuse | Helicone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracing | ✅ | ✅ |
| Cost tracking | ✅ | ✅ |
| Evaluations | ✅ | ⚠️ Limited |
| Prompt management | ✅ | ✅ |
| Open source | ✅ | ✅ |
| Self-hosting | ✅ | ✅ |
| AI gateway / proxy | ❌ SDK-based | ✅ |
| Actively developed | ✅ | ⚠️ Maintenance mode |

<details>
<summary>Main differences between Langfuse and Helicone</summary>
- Helicone works as a proxy you route requests through; Langfuse uses an SDK you add to your code, so migrating takes more setup.
- Langfuse can trace each step of a multi-step agent run, while Helicone mainly logs individual requests at the gateway level.
- Langfuse runs on a ClickHouse backend designed for high-volume trace data.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Main similarities between Langfuse and Helicone</summary>
- Both are built for LLM observability, with tracing and monitoring of LLM requests.
- Both provide cost tracking.
- Both offer prompt management features.
- Both are open source and support self-hosting.
</details>

### Why do companies use Langfuse?
Based on user reviews on [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/langfuse/reviews) and [Gartner Peer Insights](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/langfuse-658437652), teams choose Langfuse for a few reasons:

2. **The tracing is detailed:** Users point to the depth of its tracing and latency data, and say the visualizations make debugging easier.
3. **It moves fast:** Reviewers note that the open-source project ships frequent updates and continues to add new capabilities.

Arize Phoenix is an open-source platform for tracing, evaluating, and improving LLM applications and AI agents. It can be run locally, self-hosted, or used as a hosted cloud service.
Phoenix is built by Arize AI and works with many AI frameworks and model providers. It is built on open standards such as OpenTelemetry and OpenInference, and includes tools for evaluating AI systems, including retrieval and RAG evaluation.

### Key features

- **RAG and retrieval evaluation:** Measure retrieval quality, document relevance, and how well your RAG pipeline supports model responses.
- **OpenTelemetry and OpenInference support:** Use open standards for tracing and observability instead of proprietary instrumentation and trace formats.

### Key features

- **Guardrails:** Apply safety checks, validation rules, and policies to AI inputs and outputs.
- **Prompt management:** Store, version, and manage prompts across teams and environments.
- **AI governance:** Manage access controls, audit logs, and organizational policies for AI applications.

<details>
<summary>Can PostHog replace Helicone?</summary>
Yes, for many teams. **PostHog** includes AI observability, tracing, evaluations, cost tracking, prompt management, and self-hosting. Unlike **Helicone**, it also connects AI observability with product analytics, session replay, feature flags, and experiments in the same platform.
The tradeoff is that **PostHog** uses SDK-based instrumentation rather than a proxy, so migration typically involves more setup. Its AI observability tooling is also newer than some dedicated LLM observability platforms.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is there a free or open-source Helicone alternative?</summary>
Yes. **PostHog**, **Langfuse**, and **Arize Phoenix** are open-source alternatives to **Helicone.** All three offer free ways to get started, whether through self-hosting, free tiers, or both. The best one depends on what you're looking for: **PostHog** combines observability with product analytics, **Langfuse** is the closest dedicated observability alternative, **Phoenix** is strong for evaluations and RAG workflows.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the difference between a proxy-based and an SDK-based observability tool?</summary>
A proxy-based tool sits between your application and the model provider, so you can capture requests and responses by routing traffic through it. An SDK-based tool requires you to add instrumentation to your application code, but it can provide deeper visibility into workflows, tool calls, and application logic.
**Helicone** and **Portkey** use a proxy-based approach. **PostHog**, **Langfuse**, **LangSmith**, **Arize Phoenix**, **Braintrust**, and **Weave** rely primarily on SDK-based instrumentation.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Will my Helicone data export to a new tool?</summary>
Usually, yes. **Helicone** provides a data export tool that lets you export logs in JSON, JSONL, or CSV format, so your data isn't locked in.
The migration process depends on the tool you're moving to. Some platforms provide migration resources. For example, **Langfuse** publishes a [migration guide for moving from **Helicone**](https://langfuse.com/faq/all/migrate-from-helicone), and [**Helicone** integrates with **PostHog**](https://posthog.com/docs/ai-observability/integrations/helicone-posthog) to export events for analysis. For other tools, check the platform's migration and import documentation before making the switch.
</details>
