Loki is famously cheap to run because it barely indexes anything. Users accept that searching for one specific value means scanning the whole haystack for a needle. Most days, that's not an issue. During an incident, with a request ID in hand and LogQL churning slowly through compressed chunks, it's the only thing you can see.

Grafana Labs knows. At GrafanaCON 2026 they shipped a major Loki rearchitecture and acquired Logline specifically to make finding request IDs and user IDs faster; but if you just want an easier setup, a managed option, or a life outside the Grafana ecosystem, there are strong options out there.

This guide covers the best Grafana Loki alternatives available today – from [error tracking and monitoring tools](/blog/best-error-tracking-tools) to [full observability platforms](/blog/best-ai-observability-tools), so you can find what actually works for your stack.

### What is PostHog?

[PostHog](/) (that's us 👋) is a developer platform that helps engineers build self-driving products and iterate faster by combining [logs](/logs) with [error tracking](/error-tracking), [session replay](/session-replay), [product analytics](/product-analytics), [feature flags](/feature-flags), [A/B testing](/experiments), [AI observability](/ai-observability), and more in one place.

So it's not just a Grafana Loki alternative – it's also an alternative to tools like [Datadog](/blog/best-datadog-alternatives), [Sentry](/blog/posthog-vs-sentry), and [Amplitude](/blog/posthog-vs-amplitude).

PostHog Logs went into [beta](/blog/logs-beta) in December 2025 and [GA](/blog/logs-ga) in January 2026. It's newer than every other tool on this list and built specifically for teams that want logs where the rest of their debugging already lives.

What PostHog does differently is treat logs as product data: every log is tied to a user, a plan, a feature flag variant. You can query your logs from Claude, Cursor, or any MCP-compatible AI tool via the [MCP server](/docs/model-context-protocol) without opening a dashboard.

Usually it comes down to one of a few things. You need to search log fields you didn't define as labels upfront. You want someone else to run the infrastructure. <summary>Is Grafana Loki still worth using in 2026?</summary>

Yes. Grafana Labs shipped a major rearchitecture at GrafanaCON 2026 and acquired Logline to close the gap on full-text search. If the Grafana ecosystem already fits your stack, there's no compelling reason to leave.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the difference between Loki's label indexing and full-text search?</summary>

With Loki, you decide what to index before logs come in. Anything you didn't label is slow to query because Loki has to scan the raw content to find it. Full-text search tools like **Elastic Stack**, **OpenSearch**, and **VictoriaLogs** index every field automatically. You can search anything, any time, without planning ahead. The downside is it costs more to store and run; full indexing is heavier than Loki's label-only approach.

</details>

For most engineering and product teams, yes. PostHog puts logs next to error tracking, session replay, feature flags, and product analytics in one platform.
