I used to tell people that I send emails for a living. Truthfully, that's what it felt like working in post-sales the past few years. If I wasn't actually sending emails, I was sitting in back-to-back-to-back meetings that only contributed to my growing existential dread and backlog of Jira support tickets that I had to beg people to look at. Even when I started playing defense on my calendar, it didn't feel like I was going to win a championship (little sports talk for my ball-knowers).

## Why would anyone talk to their CSM?

At PostHog, I am often spending a lot of time getting customers to talk to *me*. It was a little challenging to grasp at first, but now this makes sense - by the time a customer makes it to my book, the expectation is that they are in a steady state. When the product is set up and working, a direct ping from some employee of a software vendor might as well have the urgency of a glacier. Why should they talk to me at all? Why is this guy who only types Slack messages in lowercase letters accosting me in a very kind manner?

## Weapons grade prose

A note for the reader: I am handsome and charming. While that gets me nine-tenths of the way there, it falls short in traditional communication channels. I decided that if I was going to be competing for attention, the outreach needed to be short, attention grabbing, and valuable. If somebody laughs, 2x strength multiplier. I was already armed with a powerful weapon - the written word crafted via the neurotic casino that is my mind. If punching keys with my fingers or furiously tapping a screen to create words that people would read had been a viable and lucrative career, I would have pursued it. Instead, I opted to learn how to program and took to honing my penchant for writing on social media sites (mainly Twitter, somehow amassing excess of 30,000 followers for no reason) before retiring my sword.

In the past few months, I have dusted off the sword and added a secondary weapon: the merch code. Fact: people love free stuff. Fact: people love PostHog. Ground truth: people absolutely love free stuff from PostHog. So what does that look like in practice?

## The messages that worked

These drove meaningful quick calls, or interactions that led to continued engagement:

<div className={quoteStyle}>

hi there [customer employee name]. trade proposal - 30 minutes of your time for some posthog merch. hit me with a 🟩 or ⛔

</div>

<div className={quoteStyle}>

hi [customer employee name] remember me? i have returned with a dashboard for you. re: mobile app, sorry i am late. take it for a spin and let me know what you think. also i impersonated you to create this dashboard, if you hate it it looks like you created it and if you love it then it was me.

</div>

<div className={quoteStyle}>

completely (well semi-related) unrelated ping here but wanted to let you know [customer employee name] that you are in the top 10 of _literally all posthog users_ when it comes to using the posthog mcp. i'd love to get a little time with you to ask about how you're using the mcp. in exchange for your time, i'd love to give you a code for some merch.

</div>

<div className={quoteStyle}>

hey folks happy friday! I know it's been quiet but wanted to check in and see if there was anything that you were working on that you needed help with or any feedback you might have for the platform. One of the things that I've been enjoying is seeing how many users are using the platform and what their number of sessions looks like. handing out a gold medal 🥇 to [customer employee name] as top user. i sent you a code for a t-shirt via [posthog.com/merch](/merch)

</div>

My take is that these types of outreach messages had a high success rate because they deviate so severely from the traditional corporate speak bullshit that drives people nuts. I am on calls wearing a TUMMY ACHE SURVIVOR graphic tee with raccoons on it making it rain merch codes for valuable feedback, you are sending twice-polished emails proposing our execs talk to each other. We are not the same.

## The messages that didn't

Not everything you throw at the wall sticks:

<div className={quoteStyle}>

does next monday work for me to drop by the office? fair warning it will hurt my feelings if you say no. great question, no, you cannot have another csm assigned to this account

</div>

A short collection of email subject lines that also did not work:

</div>

do you want $50,000 in credits (i am a posthog human)

</div>

</div>
