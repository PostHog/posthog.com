ETL means extract, transform, load: PostHog pulls tables out of the tools your business runs on, and loads them somewhere you can use them. That is one product with two halves.

- **Sources** are where the data comes from. Over 1,300 of them, including Stripe, HubSpot, Salesforce, and your own Postgres.
- **Destinations** are where the rows land. Your PostHog warehouse by default, and your own Postgres, Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Databricks, S3, or Azure Blob if you want them there too.

## Connect your first source

[Linking a source](/docs/data-warehouse/sources) covers the whole flow: credentials, picking tables, sync frequency, and the IP addresses to allow. Every connector has its own page for the credentials it needs.

If your tool is not in the list, the [custom REST source](/docs/data-warehouse/sources/custom-rest-source) can usually reach it.

## Choose where the rows land

By default a source writes to your PostHog warehouse and you can query it straight away.

To send the same rows to your own database or object store, set a [destination](/docs/etl/destinations). Read that page first if the source has already synced: adding a destination starts a full resync, and every destination is billed for the rows it receives.

## Query what you synced

Synced tables sit in the same query engine as the events your product sends, so you can join them. Start with [querying the warehouse](/docs/data-warehouse/query) and [joins](/docs/data-warehouse/join).

## Keep an eye on it

Pipelines usually fail quietly, and you find out when a dashboard looks wrong. The [ETL page](/docs/etl/monitoring) opens on what stopped, then shows rows written per destination, tables syncing, and runs in flight.

When something does break, [warehouse troubleshooting](/docs/data-warehouse/troubleshooting) covers the common sync failures.

## What it costs

Rows you sync in are billed on the managed warehouse product, and the first million each month are free. Rows you write to an external destination are billed separately. [ETL pricing](/docs/etl/pricing) has the worked example.
