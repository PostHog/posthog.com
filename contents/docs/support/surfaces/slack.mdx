SupportHog is how a support team works in Slack. Customers raise a ticket with `@SupportHog` or a `:ticket:` reaction. Your team replies in the same thread, and that reply lands on the ticket as a message from your team. You do not have to open the web app for a short reply.

This page is Slack as the place you do the work. To turn a channel into a ticket source, [connect Slack](/docs/support/slack).

## Before you start

1. **Set up Support** – [enable conversations and connect a channel](/docs/support/start-here) so tickets arrive in your inbox.
2. **Install SupportHog** – [add the bot to your workspace](/docs/support/slack) and invite it to the channels you use: `/invite @SupportHog`.

## Raise a ticket

There are four ways a Slack message becomes a ticket. The [connect Slack](/docs/support/slack) page has the full rules.

- **Support channel** – every top-level message in a channel you marked as a support channel becomes a ticket. Thread replies sync as ticket messages.
- **Mention** – type `@SupportHog` in any channel the bot is in. A mention inside an existing ticket thread adds a comment instead of a new ticket.
- **Emoji** – react with the ticket emoji (default `:ticket:`) on any message.
- **Nudge** – outside a support channel, SupportHog can ask in the thread whether to open a ticket. The prompt has **Open ticket** and **No thanks** buttons.

## Reply from the thread

When a teammate replies in a SupportHog ticket thread, PostHog stores that reply on the ticket as a team message. The customer sees it on the channel the ticket came from. A reply you send from the [inbox](/docs/support/inbox) is posted back into the same Slack thread, with your name on it.

**Private notes** never go to Slack. Use them for internal context.

## Why Slack

- **Shared channels** – a Slack Connect channel with a customer is a support queue. PostHog runs its own enterprise support this way. A message in the shared channel becomes a ticket, including while you are offline.
- **One thread, two places** – the Slack thread and the PostHog ticket stay in sync. Images move with the messages.
- **Nudges catch the rest** – customers do not have to remember the emoji. SupportHog asks when a message looks like a request.

## Ask @PostHog about your tickets

<CalloutBox icon="IconFlask" title="Open beta" type="action">

The `@PostHog` Slack app is in beta, so some behaviors may still change.

</CalloutBox>

`@PostHog` is a different app from SupportHog. Mention it when you want an agent to query support data or to work on a [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving) report. It does not create tickets. [Install `@PostHog`](/docs/slack/setup) separately, and make sure your Slack email matches your PostHog account.

In a thread, you can:

- Ask about your support data, like `summarize my open tickets` or `what's the oldest unresolved one?`
- Review the [Self-driving reports](/docs/self-driving/reports) built from your conversations, and approve, steer, or decline them.
- Ask it to plan a fix. It runs checks and opens a draft pull request, replying in-thread as it goes.

For everything else the app can do, see the [Slack app docs](/docs/slack).
