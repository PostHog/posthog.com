Claude supports [enterprise-managed authorization](/docs/model-context-protocol/enterprise-managed-authorization) for the PostHog connector. Claude calls it **managed authorization**. An admin connects PostHog once, and members can use the PostHog MCP server in Claude without signing in to PostHog themselves.

<CalloutBox icon="IconStar" title="Scale and Enterprise add-ons" type="action">

Enterprise-managed authorization is available to organizations on the Scale or Enterprise [platform add-on](/platform-packages).

</CalloutBox>

## Before you start

- **In Claude:** a Team or Enterprise plan, and the **Owner** role in your Claude organization. Only owners can set up managed authorization for a connector.
- **In PostHog:** the Scale or Enterprise platform add-on, and admin access to your PostHog organization.
- **Matching email addresses.** PostHog connects each Claude member to the PostHog user with the same email address. A member works only if they already have a PostHog account in your organization under the email they use in Claude. PostHog does not create accounts, so [invite members](/docs/settings/organizations#adding-new-members) or provision them with SSO or [SCIM](/docs/settings/authentication/scim) first.

If the PostHog connector in Claude has no **Managed authorization** option, it isn't available for PostHog in your Claude organization yet. [Contact us](/talk-to-a-human).

## Choose an identity source

Claude offers two identity sources. If your organization signs in to Claude through Okta, both work.

| | [Claude-issued](#claude-issued-identity) | [IdP-issued through Okta](#idp-issued-identity-through-okta) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who vouches for the user** | Claude, under an issuer URL unique to your Claude organization | Your Okta org |
| **What you need** | No identity provider or SSO | Okta with Cross App Access, and Claude sign-in through Okta |
| **Where access is controlled** | Claude roles, and PostHog membership | Okta assignments and scopes, and PostHog membership |
| **Setup effort** | A few minutes, in Claude and PostHog | Longer, in Okta, Claude, and PostHog |

## Claude-issued identity

With Claude-issued identity, Claude tells PostHog which member of your Claude organization is signed in. PostHog must trust your Claude organization's issuer URL. You can set this up in two ways:

- **Automatic setup:** you approve Claude on a PostHog consent screen, and Claude registers its issuer URL for you.
- **Manual setup:** you copy the issuer URL from Claude and paste it into PostHog.

### Automatic setup

Claude may not offer automatic setup for PostHog in your Claude organization yet. If it doesn't, use [manual setup](#manual-setup).

To approve Claude, you must be an admin of the PostHog organization. The organization needs the Scale or Enterprise add-on and at least one verified domain. If it only admits emails on its verified domains, your PostHog email must be on one of them.

1. In Claude, go to **Organization settings → Connectors** and select the **PostHog** connector.
2. On the **Configuration** tab, click **Set up** next to **Managed authorization**, and choose **Claude-issued** under **Identity source**.
3. Choose to connect PostHog automatically. Claude opens the PostHog consent screen. PostHog asks you to sign in again if your last sign-in isn't recent.
4. Under **Organization**, select your PostHog organization. If you can't select it, open the list to see why.
5. Read what approving turns on, then click **Trust Claude**.
6. Back in Claude, click **Test connection**, then **Enable and continue**, and choose which roles get the PostHog connector.

The configuration is read-only in PostHog. Click **View** to see it. To change it, run the automatic setup again from Claude, which replaces the issuer. To stop trusting Claude, delete the configuration.

### Manual setup

#### 1. Copy the issuer URL from Claude

1. In Claude, go to **Organization settings → Connectors** and select the **PostHog** connector.
2. On the **Configuration** tab, click **Set up** next to **Managed authorization**.
3. Under **Identity source**, choose **Claude-issued**.
4. Copy the **Issuer URL**. It looks like `https://identity.anthropic.com/issuers/<id>` and is unique to your Claude organization.

Leave this screen open. You come back to it in step 3.

#### 2. Trust the issuer in PostHog

1. In PostHog, go to [**Organization settings → Authentication**](https://app.posthog.com/settings/organization-authentication).
2. Under **Authentication domains**, add and verify your email domain if it isn't verified yet. See the [SSO docs](/docs/settings/authentication/sso) for the steps.
3. Find **XAA authentication** and click **Configure**.
4. Give the configuration a name, such as `Claude MCP connector`.
5. Paste the issuer URL from Claude into **Identity provider issuer URL**.
6. Under **Domains**, choose **Selected domains** and pick the domain your Claude members use.
7. Click **Save configuration**.

You only need to fill in the issuer URL and the domains. Leave **JWKS URL** and **Allowed client IDs** empty. PostHog finds Claude's signing keys from the issuer URL.

#### 3. Test and enable in Claude

1. Back in Claude, you don't need to set anything on the **Tenant ID** screen. Leave it empty. PostHog picks your organization from the issuer URL and the member's email domain.
2. Click **Test connection**. Claude shows which step failed, if any, and what PostHog returned.
3. Click **Enable and continue**.
4. Choose which roles get the PostHog connector. All roles get it by default.

If Claude lets you choose scopes for the connection, see [choosing scopes](#choosing-scopes).

## IdP-issued identity through Okta

With IdP-issued identity, your Okta org decides which members can use the PostHog MCP server in Claude, and with which scopes. Okta uses Cross App Access (XAA) for this. Your members must sign in to Claude through Okta.

PostHog isn't in the Okta Integration Network yet, so you use the PostHog app you created in Okta for SSO. Before you start, you need:

- **In Okta:** an org with SSO and Cross App Access, and the super admin role.
- **A PostHog app in Okta for SSO.** Cross App Access builds on SSO, so set up [SAML](/docs/settings/authentication/saml) or [OIDC](/docs/settings/authentication/oidc) with Okta first.

### 1. Turn on Cross App Access for your PostHog app in Okta

1. In the Okta Admin Console, go to **Applications → Applications** and select your PostHog app.
2. Open the **Machine Assignments** tab. In the **Callers** tile, click **Edit** next to **Cross App Access (XAA)**.
3. Select **Enable**, then fill in:
   - **Issuer URL:** `https://oauth.posthog.com`
   - **Audience/tenant ID:** `https://oauth.posthog.com`
   - **Scopes:** the PostHog scopes members may use. See [choosing scopes](#choosing-scopes).
4. Click **Save**.

### 2. Register Claude as an AI agent in Okta

Start Claude's setup first, because it gives you the values Okta needs:

1. In Claude, go to **Organization settings → Connectors**, select the **PostHog** connector, and click **Set up** next to **Managed authorization** on the **Configuration** tab.
2. Under **Identity source**, choose **IdP-issued**, confirm your Okta connection, and follow Claude's setup guide.

In Okta, Claude's guide has you go to **Directory → AI agents**, click **Register AI agent**, link it to the app your members use to sign in to Claude, and enter the client ID Claude shows on the **Client registration** tab. Activate the agent when you're done.

### 3. Connect Claude to PostHog in Okta

1. In **Directory → AI agents**, select the Claude agent and open **Resource connections**.
2. Click **Add resource connection**, then set:
   - **Application instance:** your PostHog app.
   - **Claude client ID registered in PostHog:** the client ID Claude uses with PostHog, which Claude's setup guide shows.
   - **Resource identifier:** `https://mcp.posthog.com/mcp`, or `https://mcp-eu.posthog.com/mcp` if you pin the EU region.
   - **Scopes:** **Allow specific scopes**, and select the scopes from step 1. See [choosing scopes](#choosing-scopes).
3. Click **Add**.
4. On the agent's **User access** tab, assign the users or groups who should get PostHog in Claude.

### 4. Trust your Okta org in PostHog

1. In PostHog, go to [**Organization settings → Authentication**](https://app.posthog.com/settings/organization-authentication).
2. Under **Authentication domains**, make sure your email domain is verified.
3. Find **XAA authentication** and click **Configure**.
4. Set **Identity provider issuer URL** to your Okta org URL, for example `https://acme.okta.com`. Use the org authorization server, not a custom one such as `/oauth2/default`, because Okta issues these assertions from the org server.
5. Under **Domains**, choose your email domain, then click **Save configuration**.

As with Claude-issued identity, you only need to fill in the issuer URL and the domains. Leave **JWKS URL** and **Allowed client IDs** empty.

### 5. Test and enable in Claude

1. Back in Claude's setup, you don't need to set anything on the **Tenant ID** screen. Leave it empty.
2. Click **Run test**. Claude shows which step failed, if any, with what Okta and PostHog returned.
3. Enable the connector and choose which roles get it.

### Okta limits

Okta limits SSO-based Cross App Access to 250 ID-JAGs per user, per resource app, per month. Claude requests a new ID-JAG each time its PostHog access token expires, which is every 2 hours, so heavy daily use can reach that limit. Okta for AI Agents raises it.

## Choosing scopes

Start with the [recommended scopes](/docs/settings/authentication/id-jag#which-scopes-should-i-grant). They're the ones the PostHog MCP server grants by default when someone connects with OAuth. [API scopes](/docs/api/scopes) explains what each one covers.

With Okta, enter the scopes on the PostHog app in step 1, and allow them in the resource connection in step 3. The access token gets only the scopes that both Okta granted and Claude asked for. To add a scope later, add it in both places. Members get it with their next token, within 2 hours.

<CalloutBox icon="IconWarning" title="Okta allows 100 scopes per app" type="action">

PostHog offers more than 100 scopes, so you can't list all of them in Okta. The recommended scopes are fewer than 100.

</CalloutBox>

## What members see

The next time a member uses PostHog in Claude, Claude connects for them. They don't see a PostHog sign-in prompt. Each connection acts as that member in PostHog, with their own permissions, limited to the granted scopes.

## Revoking access

- **In Claude:** removing a member from your Claude organization stops new tokens.
- **In Okta:** unassign a user from the agent, or remove the resource connection. Claude can't get new tokens for them.
- **In PostHog:** removing a member from your PostHog organization, or deactivating them, stops their access on the next request.
- **For everyone:** in Claude, choose **Disable** under the connector's **Managed authorization**, or delete the XAA configuration in PostHog. With automatic setup, delete the configuration tagged **Set up from Claude**.

A token that Claude already has stays valid for up to 2 hours, unless you remove the member in PostHog.

## Troubleshooting

| Error | Where | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `invalid_target` | Okta | The resource identifier Claude requested doesn't match the resource connection. |
| `unauthorized_client` | Okta | A scope Claude requested isn't allowed in the resource connection. |
| `invalid_grant` (`ID-JAG could not be verified`) | PostHog | The issuer URL in PostHog doesn't match the Claude issuer URL or your Okta org URL, or the member's email domain isn't selected under **Domains**. |
| `invalid_grant` (`ID-JAG sub is not an active member…`) | PostHog | The member's email in Claude or Okta doesn't match a member of your PostHog organization. |
| `invalid_grant` (`ID-JAG client_id doesn't match the authenticating client`) | PostHog | With Okta, the **Claude client ID registered in PostHog** in the resource connection isn't the client ID Claude uses with PostHog. |
| `invalid_client` | PostHog | Claude couldn't authenticate to PostHog. [Contact us](/talk-to-a-human). |
| `access_denied` | PostHog | Your PostHog organization doesn't have the Scale or Enterprise add-on. |
| `issuer_in_use` | PostHog, during automatic setup | Your organization already trusts this issuer in another XAA configuration, for example from an earlier manual setup. Delete that configuration in PostHog, then try again. |
| `insufficient_scope` | PostHog MCP server | The connection is missing `user:read`, `organization:read`, `project:read`, or a scope a tool needs. |

For every PostHog error code and what PostHog checks, see the [ID-JAG reference](/docs/settings/authentication/id-jag).
