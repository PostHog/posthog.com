A broadcast goes to people in your PostHog project, so you can target anything you track about them: their plan, their signup date, what they did, or a cohort they're in.

## Filter people

On the **Recipients** step, click **Add condition** and filter by:

- **[Person properties](/docs/data/persons):** such as `plan` equals `pro`.
- **[Cohorts](/docs/data/cohorts):** such as people who used a feature in the last 30 days.
- **`distinct_id`:** to target specific people by their ID.

A person must match every condition. With no conditions, the broadcast goes to everyone in the project.

**This broadcast will reach** shows about how many people match. Click **View list** to see them. The list is checked again at send time, so people who match by then get it too.

## Upload a list

Click **Upload a list**, drop in a CSV, give the list a name, and click **Add to audience**. PostHog saves the list as a static cohort, so you can reuse it in other broadcasts.

- The file needs an `email` column. To match people by the ID your app uses, add a `distinct_id` column.
- Every other column is saved as a person property, with the column name as the property name. Use it in the email, such as `{{ person.properties.plan }}` for a `plan` column.
- People who aren't in PostHog yet are created. A blank cell leaves the existing value as it is.
- Each row is sent as one event, so it counts toward your event usage.

If you also have filters, a person must be on the list and match the filters.

## Pick a message category

Under **Message category**, pick the kind of email this is, such as a newsletter. People who unsubscribed from that category don't get the broadcast. A transactional category goes to everyone, including people who unsubscribed, so use it only for email people need. See [opt-outs](/docs/workflows-next/email/opt-outs).

## Let PostHog suggest one

The **Broadcasts** tab can show **Audiences you could send to**: up to three audiences that AI suggests from your events, such as people who signed up but never uploaded a file. Each card shows about how many people have an email address, why the audience is worth a send, and its rule.

Click **Draft the email**. PostHog saves the audience as a static cohort, opens a new broadcast with it, and AI writes the first draft in the style of your last broadcast or a saved template. You check it before it sends.

Suggestions are in beta. They need your organization to approve AI data processing, and they're hidden from people who can't see email addresses. To send one, you need a [verified sender on your own domain](/docs/workflows-next/domain).

## Who doesn't get it

PostHog skips some people in the audience when it sends:

- **Shared addresses:** people who share an email address get one email.
- **No email address:** people without a usable address are skipped.
- **Opted out:** people who unsubscribed from the broadcast's message category, or from all marketing email. See [opt-outs](/docs/workflows-next/email/opt-outs).
- **Suppressed:** addresses that bounced or marked your email as spam. See the [suppression list](/docs/workflows-next/email/suppression).
- **Over the frequency cap:** people who already got their share of marketing email. If your cap has a hold time, they wait up to that long, then they're skipped. To send to them anyway, turn on **Send even if people reached your frequency cap** on the **Schedule** step. See the [frequency cap](/docs/workflows-next/email/frequency-cap).

After the send, the broadcast counts each group, so you can see why someone didn't get it.

## When the audience is too big

Each project has a [sending allowance](/docs/workflows-next/broadcasts/large-audiences) that limits how many people one email broadcast can reach. When the audience is bigger, the **Recipients** step tells you. Add filters to narrow it, or limit the sending rate.
