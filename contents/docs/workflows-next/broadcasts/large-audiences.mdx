Mailbox providers trust senders that grow steadily. So each project has a sending allowance, which grows as you send good email, and a broadcast can only reach an audience that fits it.

## Your sending allowance

| Tier | Emails per day | Largest audience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 100 | 100 |
| 1 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| 2 | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| 3 | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| 4 | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 5 | 100,000 | 100,000 |
| 6 | 300,000 | 300,000 |
| 7 | 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 |

A new project starts at tier 0. Once a day, PostHog moves it up a tier if it held its tier for a few days, sent at least half its daily cap on 2 days, and kept bounces under 2% and spam complaints under 0.1%. A full climb takes about five weeks. The **Reputation** tab shows your tier. See [sending health](/docs/workflows-next/metrics#sending-health).

The allowance refills over the day, not at midnight. Email over it waits in a queue and goes out as it refills. Nothing is dropped.

## When the audience is too big

If an audience is bigger than your tier allows, PostHog won't launch the broadcast, and shows your limit. You can:

- **Narrow it:** add [filters](/docs/workflows-next/broadcasts/audiences).
- **Slow it down:** limit the sending rate, below.
- **Wait:** your tier grows as you send clean email.

## Limit the sending rate

On the **Schedule** step, turn on **Limit sending rate**, and set the most emails per minute or per hour. The broadcast spreads its sends out, and the rest wait their turn.

A slow rate also lets a broadcast reach more people than your tier's largest audience: up to what that rate sends in 7 days, at most about 3.5 times your daily allowance. For example, at tier 3, 200 emails an hour can reach 33,600 people. When an audience is too big, PostHog suggests a rate that fits.

You can't change the rate while a slowed broadcast is still sending.

## Transactional email first

Transactional emails count toward the allowance, but never wait for it, and go out ahead of a broadcast. A big broadcast never holds up a password reset.
