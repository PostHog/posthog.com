The suppression list holds addresses PostHog stops emailing, because email to them bounces or gets reported as spam. It protects your [deliverability](/docs/workflows-next/email/deliverability), so you don't have to clean your lists by hand.

An opt-out is a person's choice. A suppression is about the address: it can't, or shouldn't, get email.

## How addresses get on it

PostHog adds them for you:

- **Hard bounce:** the address doesn't exist, or refuses mail. Added after the first one.
- **Soft bounces:** the mailbox is full or down. Added after several in a row with no delivery between them.
- **Spam complaint:** the person marked an email as spam. Added after the first one.

You can also click **Add address**, for example for someone who asked you by email to stop.

Each entry shows the address, how it got there, the reason, and when.

## What it blocks

- **Marketing email:** any entry blocks it.
- **Transactional email:** only a hard bounce blocks it, because only a hard bounce proves the address is dead. Someone who reported your newsletter still gets their password reset.

A transactional send to a hard-bounced address fails with `recipient_suppressed`. Don't retry it.

## Remove an address

Remove an entry if a bounce was a one-off, such as a full mailbox that's been emptied. Don't remove a real hard bounce: it bounces again and costs you twice.

## From your own systems

The [messaging suppressions API](/docs/api/messaging-suppressions) lists, adds, and removes entries, one at a time or in bulk. A bulk add takes a `reason` for each address:

| `reason` | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| `bounce` | All email, transactional too |
| `complaint` | Marketing email |
| `manual` | Marketing email |

Moving from another provider? Import its bounces as `bounce` before your first send. See [move from another provider](/docs/workflows-next/migrate).
