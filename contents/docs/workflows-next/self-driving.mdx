PostHog watches how each automation performs, such as how many people open and click each email. When a step underperforms, it suggests one change, such as a new subject line. You approve it. Nothing reaches anyone until you do.

## Turn it on

Self-driving is in beta. Open an automation, go to **Self-driving**, and turn on **Suggest improvements**. Your organization needs [AI data processing](/docs/posthog-ai/allow-access) turned on.

PostHog starts suggesting once the automation:

- Has been live for 2 days.
- Has sent enough emails from its busiest email step in the last 7 days. The tab shows how close it is.

Then PostHog checks it once a day. Transactional emails, such as receipts, aren't reviewed.

Set a [conversion goal](/docs/workflows-next/automations#add-steps), and suggestions aim at conversions, not just opens and clicks.

## Review a suggestion

Each suggestion shows:

- **The change:** what it changes, such as an email's subject or content. **Compare emails** puts the old and new email side by side.
- **The evidence:** the numbers behind it, and how many sends they came from. With too little data, it says so.

Click **Approve as draft** to stage it, then publish the automation when you're ready. Or click **Reject**, and say why, such as "this is the last email in the series, so clicks aren't its goal". Rejected suggestions stay in the history, and nothing changes.

## See if it worked

Once you publish a suggestion, PostHog measures the step before and after the change, version by version. If someone changes the step again later, it says so.

## Over MCP

Your coding agent can file suggestions for you to review, and read the ones PostHog filed. Ask "What has PostHog suggested for our onboarding automation?" over [MCP](/docs/workflows-next/mcp).
