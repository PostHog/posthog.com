PostHog imports your Postmark suppressions, message streams, and templates in one background import.

> The Postmark import is rolling out. If Postmark isn't in the list, your project doesn't have it yet.

## What moves

| From Postmark | In PostHog |
| --- | --- |
| Hard bounces | [Suppression list](/docs/workflows-next/sending-rules#suppression-list). Blocks all email. |
| Spam complaints and manual suppressions | Suppression list. Blocks marketing email. |
| Unsubscribes | Opted out of all marketing email |
| Custom broadcast streams | Marketing [categories](/docs/workflows-next/sending-rules#opt-outs) |
| Templates, with their layout | Templates, keyed by their Postmark alias |
| Domains and webhooks | A list, so you know what to set up |

Postmark has no automations or contact lists. Inbound streams, SMTP settings, send history, stats, and tokens stay in Postmark.

## Run the import

1. Copy a token from [Postmark](https://account.postmarkapp.com/api_tokens). A **server token** imports one server. The **account token** imports every live server, and lists your domains and senders. PostHog only reads, and deletes the token when the import ends.
2. In [Workflows](https://app.posthog.com/workflows), go to **Opt-outs** and click **Import from another tool**.
3. Pick **Postmark**, paste the token, pick what to import, and click **Start import**.

## Switch your sending

- **Transactional stream:** templates from it become [transactional emails](/docs/workflows-next/transactional), which only a hard bounce blocks.
- **SMTP:** PostHog has no SMTP service. An app that sends over SMTP changes to the [server SDK](/docs/workflows-next/installation) or the API.
- **Inbound:** PostHog can't receive email, so inbound stays in Postmark.

See [switch over safely](/docs/workflows-next/migrate#switch-over-safely) for the full checklist.
