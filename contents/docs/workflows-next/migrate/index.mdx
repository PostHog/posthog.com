PostHog imports what you've built with another email provider: your suppression list, unsubscribes, templates, and, for some providers, automations. Then you move your sending over one email at a time.

## Ask your agent

## Or import in PostHog

In [Workflows](https://app.posthog.com/workflows), go to **Opt-outs** and click **Import from another tool**. Pick your provider, paste an API key, pick what to import, and click **Start import**.

The import runs in the background. PostHog only reads from your account, and deletes the key when the import ends.

## Switch over safely

1. **[Set up your domain](/docs/workflows-next/domain).** PostHog needs its own DNS records, even for a domain that already sends. Keep your old provider's records while you move.
2. **Import.** Suppressions and unsubscribes come first, so nobody who opted out gets an email.
3. **Fix what the import flagged.** Each template and automation lists anything that didn't convert cleanly. Send yourself a test.
4. **Move your code one email at a time.** Start with a low-risk email, and send it with the [server SDK](/docs/workflows-next/installation).
5. **Turn on automations as you turn them off.** Imported automations are drafts, and never send until you turn them on. Turn off the old one at the same time, so nobody gets the same email twice.
6. **Ramp up slowly.** Mailbox providers slow down a sender whose volume jumps. Check your [sending allowance](/docs/workflows-next/metrics#sending-health) first.
7. **Clean up.** Once nothing sends through the old provider, remove its DNS records and delete the API key.

Importing again adds what's new. Anything you changed or deleted in PostHog stays as it is.
