Logs is an OpenTelemetry-native log store, so there are no PostHog-specific SDKs to adopt – point any OTLP client at PostHog and your log records start arriving. Search them by service, severity, and attribute, group similar lines into [patterns](/docs/logs/patterns) to see what changed, and open any record to have [PostHog AI](/docs/logs/explain-logs-ai) explain it.

Because your logs live next to the rest of your data, a log line is one click from the [session replay](/docs/session-replay), [person](/docs/data/persons), and [error](/docs/error-tracking) it belongs to. Log alerts also feed [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving), so a recurring failure becomes a report you can act on instead of a page you ignore.

## How Logs works with Self-driving

When a [log alert](/docs/logs/alerts) fires, it emits a signal into [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving). Recurring failures get grouped into a report with the logs behind them. PostHog also sweeps your logs for shifts nobody wrote an alert for – new message patterns, volume bursts, severity shifts, and services that go quiet – and writes those up as reports too.

The logs scout stops at the report. A log shift is usually an investigation rather than a one-line code change, so it doesn't open a pull request on its own – unlike [error tracking](/docs/error-tracking), where a stack trace often points at a specific file. You investigate in the [web app](/docs/logs/surfaces/web-app) and read the reports in the [Self-driving inbox](/docs/self-driving/inbox) or [PostHog Desktop](/docs/logs/surfaces/desktop). See the [Self-driving docs](/docs/self-driving) for the full picture.
