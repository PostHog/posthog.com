The data warehouse brings your business data into PostHog and puts it next to your product data. Sync Stripe, Postgres, Salesforce, HubSpot, and dozens of other [sources](/docs/data-warehouse/sources), then query them alongside your events, persons, and sessions in one SQL query – no ETL pipeline to build and no second analytics tool to keep in sync.

Once the data is in, you shape it with [views](/docs/data-warehouse/views) and [materialized views](/docs/data-warehouse/views/materialize), chart it in [insights](/docs/data-warehouse/insights), and reach it from your editor over [MCP](/docs/data-warehouse/surfaces/mcp). When a model breaks, it becomes a signal for [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving) instead of a dashboard that quietly goes stale.

## How the data warehouse works with Self-driving

Your models are monitored, not just stored. When a materialized view stops refreshing, the failure becomes a [signal](/docs/self-driving/inbox/sources) for [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving) rather than a dashboard that silently serves stale numbers.

Because a view's SQL lives in PostHog rather than your codebase, the fix usually happens in the [web app](/docs/data-warehouse/surfaces/web-app) – but you can diagnose it from [PostHog Desktop](/docs/data-warehouse/surfaces/desktop) or [MCP](/docs/data-warehouse/surfaces/mcp) first. See the [Self-driving docs](/docs/self-driving) for the full picture.
