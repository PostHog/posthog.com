AI Observability captures every call your AI product makes to an LLM – the prompt, the response, the tokens, the cost, the latency, and the tools it reached for along the way – and stitches them into traces you can actually read. When something goes wrong, you see the whole conversation instead of a single failed request.

Because it runs on the same events as the rest of PostHog, every trace comes with the user who triggered it, their [session replay](/docs/session-replay), and any [exceptions](/docs/error-tracking) the request threw. That same trace data is what [evaluations](/docs/ai-evals) score and what [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving) reads when it files a report about your AI product's quality.

## How AI Observability works with Self-driving

Your LLM traces are a [signal source](/docs/self-driving/inbox/sources) for [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving). A scout watches cost, latency, errors, volume, and evaluation performance, and when a slice regresses against its own baseline, it files an investigation report in the inbox.

A cost step or a latency band widening is rarely a one-line code fix – it's a question about a model, a prompt, or a change in what your users are asking for. So this scout stops at the report and routes it to a human reviewer. It does not open a draft pull request.

You read traces in the [PostHog web app](/docs/ai-observability/surfaces/web-app), and read the reports in the [Self-driving inbox](/docs/self-driving/inbox) or [PostHog Desktop](/docs/ai-observability/surfaces/desktop). Other signal sources, like [error tracking](/docs/error-tracking), do open pull requests – see the [Self-driving docs](/docs/self-driving) for the full picture.
