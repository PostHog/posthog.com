If one deployment of your product serves many separate customer accounts, you need a way to tell those accounts apart in PostHog. That covers a lot of products: B2B SaaS, agency and white-label dashboards, marketplace and platform apps installed across many merchant stores, multi-store retail and franchise systems, and consumer apps built around shared workspaces.

Whatever you call the unit – tenant, organization, account, workspace, store, or team – the setup is the same:

**Keep every tenant in one project, and separate them by attaching the tenant to your events.** Use [group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) when you need to aggregate by tenant rather than by user.

Installing PostHog works the same as for any other app, so start with the [installation docs](/docs/product-analytics/installation) if you haven't yet. Nothing on this page depends on your framework or hosting.

## Decide what a tenant is

Pick the entity your customers think of as "their account", because that's the boundary they'll ask you to report on. In most products it maps to a row in your database: an organization, a workspace, a store, or a company.

Some products need two levels. A retail platform might have an `organization` that owns several `store` entities, and both are worth analyzing. You can define up to 5 group types per project, so spend them on levels you'll actually report on rather than one per table in your schema.

Whichever you choose, key it on an immutable ID from your database, not a display name. Names duplicate and change.

## Use one project, not one project per tenant

A [project](/docs/settings/projects) is a silo of data: all queries are project-specific, and every project has its own token, settings, dashboards, feature flags, and insights. That makes a project the wrong unit for a tenant.

If you create a project per tenant, you can't answer questions that span your customer base, like how many organizations activated this month, which plan tier retains best, or how a feature rollout performed overall. There's no way to query across projects. You'd also duplicate every dashboard, flag, and setting for each new customer, and provision a new project and token on every signup.

Instead, keep all tenants in one production project, alongside your marketing site. Paid plans let you add separate projects for local development and staging so test data stays out of production. The free plan includes one project.

Reach for separate projects only when the [usual reasons](/docs/settings/projects#how-to-organize-projects) apply: entirely separate products with unlinked authentication systems, admin apps that aren't customer-facing, or internal services.

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Keep your marketing site in the same project" type="fyi">

Tracking your marketing site and app together lets you follow the whole journey, from first visit through signup to product usage. If tenants get their own subdomains, you can still slice by tenant with the `$host` property or by group type. See [using the same project for your website and app](/docs/product-analytics/best-practices#10-use-the-same-project-for-your-website-and-app).

</CalloutBox>

## Identify the user, group by the tenant

Users and tenants are separate layers. Each has its own identifier and its own properties.

| | Identifier | Set with | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **User** | `distinct_id`, your internal user ID | [`identify()`](/docs/product-analytics/identify) | Person properties like email, role, and signup date |
| **Tenant** | Group key, your tenant ID | [`group()`](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) | Group properties like plan, seat count, and contract value |

Call `identify()` as soon as you know who the user is, then attach the tenant they're working in:

<MultiLanguage>

```js-web
posthog.identify(user.id, {
    email: user.email,
    role: user.role,
})

// Call this after login, before you capture tenant-scoped events
posthog.group('organization', tenant.id, {
    name: tenant.name,
    plan: tenant.plan,
    seat_count: tenant.seatCount,
})
```

```node
posthog.groupIdentify({
    groupType: 'organization',
    groupKey: tenant.id,
    properties: {
        name: tenant.name,
        plan: tenant.plan,
        seat_count: tenant.seatCount,
    },
})

// Then attach the group to every event you capture
posthog.capture({
    distinctId: user.id,
    event: 'order_created',
    groups: { organization: tenant.id },
})
```

```python
posthog.group_identify('organization', tenant.id, {
    'name': tenant.name,
    'plan': tenant.plan,
    'seat_count': tenant.seat_count,
})

# Then attach the group to every event you capture
posthog.capture(
    'order_created',
    distinct_id=user.id,
    groups={'organization': tenant.id},
)
```

</MultiLanguage>

A few things to get right:

- **Use singular group type names**, so `organization` rather than `organizations`.
- **The web SDK remembers the group.** Call `posthog.group()` once after login and all subsequent events in that session link to that tenant. Call `posthog.reset()` on logout, and call `posthog.group()` again with the new key if a user switches tenant without logging out.
- **Other SDKs need the group on every event.** Server-side, pass `groups` on each `capture` call. There's no session to remember it for you.
- **Events must be [identified](/docs/data/anonymous-vs-identified-events) to link to a group.** If `$process_person_profile` is `false`, the event won't be associated with the tenant.
- **Keep tenant properties on the tenant.** A user's role belongs on the person, a plan tier belongs on the group. Putting the plan on every person means updating every user's profile when a tenant upgrades.

**Users who belong to several tenants** stay a single person, which is what lets you see their full activity. What changes is the tenant their events are attributed to, so re-call `group()` whenever they switch context. Use one stable `distinct_id` per human being, not one per tenant membership. See [identity resolution](/docs/product-analytics/identity-resolution) for the full picture.

<CalloutBox icon="IconWarning" title="Don't use the tenant ID as the distinct ID" type="caution">

It's tempting to send events with `distinct_id: tenant.id` so everything from a tenant lands together. Don't. The `distinct_id` identifies a *person*, so this collapses every user in a tenant into one person profile, which breaks per-user metrics, funnels, and cohorts.

It's also a scaling problem: a single distinct ID sending a high, sustained volume of events (roughly 5,000 per minute) crosses a [per-distinct-ID rate limit](/docs/how-posthog-works/ingestion-pipeline#rate-limiting-high-volume-distinct-ids). Its events still arrive, but they lose strict ordering and person profile updates until the spike subsides. A busy tenant on a shared distinct ID hits this long before your project does.

Identify the user, and pass the tenant as a group. For genuinely user-less tenant events like a nightly job or a webhook, see [capturing events for individual groups without a user](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#capture-events-for-individual-groups-without-a-user).

</CalloutBox>

### Without the group analytics add-on

You can get part of the way there with properties alone. Set the tenant ID as a [super property](/docs/libraries/js/usage#super-properties) on the frontend, or add it to every event server-side:

```js-web
posthog.register({ tenant_id: tenant.id })
```

Because it's attached to everything you capture, you can filter and break down insights, session replays, and error tracking issues by tenant. What you give up is aggregation *by* tenant, covered next.

## What modeling tenants gets you

Once each event carries its tenant, the rest of PostHog can work at the tenant level rather than the user level.

| What you want | How | Needs the add-on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filter insights, replays, and errors to one customer | Property filter or breakdown on your tenant property or group | No |
| Count unique tenants instead of unique users | Select **Unique** with your group type in [insights](/docs/product-analytics/insights) | Yes |
| Conversion measured per tenant, not per user | Set **Aggregating by** to your group type in [funnels](/docs/product-analytics/funnels) and [retention](/docs/product-analytics/retention) | Yes |
| Roll a feature out to one customer at a time | [Target feature flags at a group](/docs/feature-flags/user-and-group-targeting) so every user in a tenant gets the same variant | Yes |
| Run an experiment where the tenant is the unit | [Group-level experiment results](/docs/experiments) | Yes |
| A dashboard of accounts with usage and activity | [B2B mode in customer analytics](/docs/customer-analytics/b2b-mode) | Yes |

Two of these are worth calling out for multi-tenant apps specifically. Flag targeting by group means a rollout lands per customer instead of per user, so two people in the same account never see different versions of your product. And filtering [session replays](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings) and [error tracking issues](/docs/error-tracking/monitoring#finding-specific-issues) by tenant is usually the fastest way to answer "what is this customer seeing?" when they report a problem.

## Costs and limits

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Group analytics bills all identified events" type="fyi">

Group analytics is a paid add-on, and once you subscribe, billing applies to **all identified events** in your project, not only the events carrying group properties. Billing starts when you enable it on your [billing page](https://app.posthog.com/organization/billing), not when you add `posthog.group()` to your code. Check the [pricing calculator](/pricing) before you wire it up.

</CalloutBox>

The [limits on group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#limitations) that matter most when you're planning a multi-tenant setup:

- 5 group types per project. Deleted group types don't count toward it.
- You can delete a group type, but not an individual group.
- One event can't belong to two groups of the same type, so a user acting in two tenants at once needs two events.
- Group types aren't supported for lifecycle insights or user paths.

## Privacy, retention, and access control

In a multi-tenant app you're handling data on behalf of your customers, so the useful framing is: capture as little tenant PII as you can, then control who can reach what's left.

**Before data reaches PostHog**, use [privacy controls](/docs/product-analytics/privacy): the `before_send` config option to sanitize or drop events client-side, `ph-no-capture` to [keep sensitive elements out of autocapture](/docs/privacy/data-collection#hide-sensitive-information-with-autocapture), and [masking rules](/docs/session-replay/privacy) if you record sessions. You can also opt individual users out entirely.

**After it arrives**, [data storage controls](/docs/privacy/data-storage) cover the rest:

- **Region** – [PostHog Cloud EU](/eu) stores data in Frankfurt, which matters if your tenants have [GDPR](/docs/privacy/gdpr-compliance) requirements. Choose this at signup, because moving regions later isn't self-serve. A PostHog engineer has to run the migration for you.
- **Transformations** – use [realtime transformations](/docs/cdp/transformations) to hash PII, anonymize IP addresses, or [drop events](/docs/cdp/transformations/drop-events) before they're stored. You can also discard client IP addresses [per project](/docs/privacy/data-storage#discarding-ip-addresses-at-project-level).
- **Deletion** – when a tenant invokes the [right to be forgotten](/docs/privacy/data-storage#right-to-be-forgotten) for one of their users, delete that person and their events by API. Event data is cleared asynchronously during off-peak hours, so it isn't instant. [Session recordings](/docs/session-replay/recording-retention) have their own retention periods.
- **Access control** – [organization, project, and resource-level controls](/docs/settings/access-control) govern who on *your* team sees what, and [SSO, SAML, and SCIM](/docs/settings/sso) let you manage that from your identity provider.

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Showing tenants their own data" type="fyi">

PostHog's access controls apply to your team, not your customers. There's no way to scope a PostHog login to a single group. To surface per-tenant analytics inside your product, query PostHog server-side and render it yourself. See [embedded analytics](/tutorials/embedded-analytics) for the pattern. A [public share link](/docs/product-analytics/sharing) is viewable by anyone who has it, so it isn't a per-tenant boundary.

</CalloutBox>

## Bring tenant metadata in from your database

Some tenant attributes are awkward to send as group properties: contract value, account owner, billing region, anything that lives in your billing system rather than your app.

Link the database that holds them as a [data warehouse source](/docs/cdp/sources), then [join it](/docs/data-warehouse/join) in [SQL](/docs/sql) to break events down by columns you never captured as events. Sources exist for [Postgres](/docs/cdp/sources/postgres), [Supabase](/docs/cdp/sources/supabase), [MySQL](/docs/cdp/sources/mysql), Stripe, and others, and each source page has its connection requirements.
