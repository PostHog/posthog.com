If your product serves many separate customer accounts from one deployment, you need a way to tell those accounts apart in PostHog. B2B SaaS is the obvious case, but so is a marketplace app installed across many merchant stores, or anything built around shared workspaces.

Whatever you call the unit – tenant, organization, or workspace – the model is the same. Identify the user, then attach the tenant as a [group](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics).

Installing PostHog works the same as for any other app. Start with the [installation docs](/docs/product-analytics/installation) if you haven't yet.

## Use one project, not one project per tenant

A [project](/docs/settings/projects) is a silo of data. Queries can't cross projects, and every project has its own token, settings, dashboards, and feature flags.

With a project per tenant:

- You can't answer questions that span your customer base, like how many organizations activated this month or which plan tier retains best.
- You duplicate every dashboard, flag, and setting for each new customer.
- Every signup means provisioning a new project and token.

Keep all tenants in one production project, alongside your marketing site so you can follow the journey from first visit through signup to product usage.

Separate projects still make sense for the reasons that apply to any app: entirely separate products with unlinked authentication systems, admin apps that aren't customer-facing, or internal services. See [how to organize projects](/docs/settings/projects#how-to-organize-projects).

## Keep the tenant separate from the user

Model the tenant on whatever your customers call their own account, and key it on an immutable ID from your database.

[`identify()`](/docs/product-analytics/identify) sets the person and [`group()`](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) sets the tenant:

```js-web
posthog.identify(user.id, { email: user.email, role: user.role })
posthog.group('organization', tenant.id, { plan: tenant.plan })
```

Keep their properties separate too. A user's role belongs on the person, and a plan tier belongs on the tenant.

A few things to know:

- You can define up to 5 group types per project. Use two if you need levels, like an `organization` that owns several `store` entities.
- Users who belong to several tenants stay one person. Call `group()` again when they switch context rather than issuing them a second `distinct_id`.
- Server-side capture differs from the web SDK. See [setting up group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#set-up-group-analytics) for the details.

<CalloutBox icon="IconWarning" title="Don't use the tenant ID as the distinct ID" type="caution">

The `distinct_id` identifies a *person*. Setting it to the tenant ID collapses every user in a tenant into one person profile, which breaks per-user metrics, funnels, and cohorts.

A single distinct ID also hits a [rate limit](/docs/how-posthog-works/ingestion-pipeline#rate-limiting-high-volume-distinct-ids) at roughly 5,000 events per minute. Past that, events lose strict ordering and person profile updates.

Identify the user and pass the tenant as a group. For tenant events with no user behind them, like a nightly job or a webhook, see [capturing events for individual groups without a user](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#capture-events-for-individual-groups-without-a-user).

</CalloutBox>

## Filter and aggregate by tenant

Filtering by tenant needs no add-on. Send the tenant ID as a [super property](/docs/libraries/js/usage#super-properties) or an event property. You can then filter and break down insights, [session replays](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings), and [error tracking issues](/docs/error-tracking/monitoring#finding-specific-issues) by tenant.

Aggregating by tenant needs [group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics), a paid add-on. It covers:

- Counting unique organizations rather than users
- Funnels and [retention](/docs/product-analytics/retention) measured per tenant
- [Flag rollouts targeted at a whole tenant](/docs/feature-flags/user-and-group-targeting)
- Group-level [experiment](/docs/experiments) results
- [B2B mode in customer analytics](/docs/customer-analytics/b2b-mode)

Check the [billing scope](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#billing) before you subscribe. The add-on bills all identified events in your project, not only the events carrying group properties.

## Show tenants their own data

PostHog's [access controls](/docs/settings/access-control) apply to your team, not your customers. There's no way to scope a PostHog login to a single group, and anyone who has a [public share link](/docs/product-analytics/sharing) can view it.

To surface per-tenant analytics inside your product, query PostHog server-side and render it yourself. See [embedded analytics](/tutorials/embedded-analytics) for the pattern.

## Privacy and access controls

PostHog has controls for limiting what you capture from your tenants' users, and who can reach it once captured.

- [Privacy controls](/docs/product-analytics/privacy) redact data before capture.
- [Transformations](/docs/cdp/transformations) hash PII after capture.
- [Deletion](/docs/privacy/data-storage#right-to-be-forgotten) handles right to be forgotten requests per person.

See [data storage controls](/docs/privacy/data-storage) for the full set.

## Pull tenant metadata from your database

Attributes like contract value, account owner, or billing region often live in your billing system rather than your app. Link that database as a [data warehouse source](/docs/cdp/sources), then [join it](/docs/data-warehouse/join) in [SQL](/docs/sql) to break events down by columns you never captured as events.
