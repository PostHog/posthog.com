If one deployment of your product serves many separate customer accounts, you need a way to tell those accounts apart in PostHog. That covers a lot of products: B2B SaaS, white-label dashboards for agencies, marketplace apps installed across many merchant stores, multi-store retail systems, and consumer apps built around shared workspaces.

Whatever you call the unit – tenant, organization, account, workspace, store, or team – two decisions cover most of the setup, and this page is about those two. [Group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) has the implementation detail once you've made them.

Installing PostHog works the same as for any other app, so start with the [installation docs](/docs/product-analytics/installation) if you haven't yet.

## Use one project, not one project per tenant

A [project](/docs/settings/projects) is a silo of data: all queries are project-specific, and every project has its own token, settings, dashboards, feature flags, and insights. That makes a project the wrong unit for a tenant.

With a project per tenant, you can't answer questions that span your customer base, like how many organizations activated this month or which plan tier retains best. There's no way to query across projects. You'd also duplicate every dashboard, flag, and setting for each new customer, and provision a new project and token on every signup.

Keep all tenants in one production project, alongside your marketing site so you can follow the journey from first visit through signup to product usage. Paid plans let you add separate projects for local development and staging. The free plan includes one project.

Reach for separate projects only when the [usual reasons](/docs/settings/projects#how-to-organize-projects) apply: entirely separate products with unlinked authentication systems, admin apps that aren't customer-facing, or internal services.

## Keep the tenant separate from the user

Pick the entity your customers think of as "their account", because that's the boundary they'll ask you to report on. Key it on an immutable ID from your database. Some products need two levels, like an `organization` that owns several `store` entities, and you can define up to 5 group types per project.

The user and the tenant are separate layers. [`identify()`](/docs/product-analytics/identify) sets the person, and [`group()`](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics) sets the tenant:

```js-web
posthog.identify(user.id, { email: user.email, role: user.role })
posthog.group('organization', tenant.id, { plan: tenant.plan })
```

Keep their properties separate too. A user's role belongs on the person, a plan tier belongs on the tenant. Putting the plan on every person means updating every user's profile when a tenant upgrades.

Users who belong to several tenants stay one person, which is what lets you see their full activity. Re-call `group()` when they switch context rather than issuing them a second `distinct_id`. See [setting up group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#set-up-group-analytics) for the SDK details, including how server-side capture differs from the web SDK.

<CalloutBox icon="IconWarning" title="Don't use the tenant ID as the distinct ID" type="caution">

It's tempting to send events with `distinct_id: tenant.id` so everything from a tenant lands together. Don't. The `distinct_id` identifies a *person*, so this collapses every user in a tenant into one person profile, which breaks per-user metrics, funnels, and cohorts.

It's also a scaling problem: a single distinct ID sending a high, sustained volume of events (roughly 5,000 per minute) crosses a [per-distinct-ID rate limit](/docs/how-posthog-works/ingestion-pipeline#rate-limiting-high-volume-distinct-ids). Its events still arrive, but they lose strict ordering and person profile updates until the spike subsides. A busy tenant on a shared distinct ID hits this long before your project does.

Identify the user, and pass the tenant as a group. For genuinely user-less tenant events like a nightly job or a webhook, see [capturing events for individual groups without a user](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#capture-events-for-individual-groups-without-a-user).

</CalloutBox>

## What you can do once tenants are modeled

Filtering by tenant needs no add-on. Send the tenant ID as a [super property](/docs/libraries/js/usage#super-properties) or an event property, and you can filter and break down insights, [session replays](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings), and [error tracking issues](/docs/error-tracking/monitoring#finding-specific-issues) by tenant. That's usually the fastest way to answer "what is this customer seeing?" when they report a problem.

Aggregating by tenant needs [group analytics](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics), which is a paid add-on. It covers counting unique organizations rather than users, funnels and [retention](/docs/product-analytics/retention) measured per tenant, [flag rollouts targeted at a whole tenant](/docs/feature-flags/user-and-group-targeting) so two people in one account never see different versions of your product, group-level [experiment](/docs/experiments) results, and [B2B mode in customer analytics](/docs/customer-analytics/b2b-mode).

Check the [billing scope](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics#billing) before you subscribe. The add-on bills all identified events in your project, not only the events carrying group properties.

## Showing tenants their own data

PostHog's [access controls](/docs/settings/access-control) apply to your team, not your customers. There's no way to scope a PostHog login to a single group, and a [public share link](/docs/product-analytics/sharing) is viewable by anyone who has it, so it isn't a per-tenant boundary either.

To surface per-tenant analytics inside your product, query PostHog server-side and render it yourself. See [embedded analytics](/tutorials/embedded-analytics) for the pattern.

## Privacy and tenant data

You're handling data on behalf of your customers, so capture as little of their users' PII as you can, then control who can reach the rest. [Privacy controls](/docs/product-analytics/privacy) cover redaction before capture, and [data storage controls](/docs/privacy/data-storage) cover [transformations](/docs/cdp/transformations) that hash PII, per-person [deletion](/docs/privacy/data-storage#right-to-be-forgotten) for right to be forgotten requests, and access control.

## Tenant metadata from your database

Attributes like contract value, account owner, or billing region often live in your billing system rather than your app, which makes them awkward to send as group properties.

Link the database that holds them as a [data warehouse source](/docs/cdp/sources), then [join it](/docs/data-warehouse/join) in [SQL](/docs/sql) to break events down by columns you never captured as events.
