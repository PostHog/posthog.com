<QuestLogItem title="Install the SDK and turn on recording" subtitle="Where replays begin" icon="IconBook">

Add a PostHog SDK to your app and enable session recording. Web needs a snippet or the JavaScript SDK; mobile apps use the React Native, iOS, Android, or Flutter SDKs. Recording is a project setting, so you also have to switch it on in [project settings](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay) before anything is captured.

Web has the most complete feature support. Mobile has a few limits, like wireframe-only mode unless you turn on screenshot mode, so check your platform's page.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/session-replay/installation">Install session replay</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Decide which sessions to record" subtitle="Volume you control" icon="IconToggle">

Recording everything is rarely what you want, and it's the main driver of cost. [Recording rules](/docs/session-replay/how-to-control-which-sessions-you-record) let you sample a percentage of sessions, record only certain URLs or events, gate recording behind a [feature flag](/docs/feature-flags), or record only sessions from a specific cohort.

Set this up early. Rules apply at capture time, so they only affect sessions recorded after you change them.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/session-replay/how-to-control-which-sessions-you-record">Set recording rules</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Mask sensitive data" subtitle="Privacy before capture" icon="IconShield">

Masking happens on the device, before anything is sent to PostHog. By default inputs are masked; you can also mask all text, mask specific elements with `ph-no-capture`, or unmask elements you know are safe. Mobile SDKs have their own masking rules for screenshots and third-party screens.

Get this right before you record real traffic, because a replay captured unmasked stays unmasked.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/session-replay/privacy">Configure privacy controls</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Capture console logs and network requests" subtitle="A real debugging environment" icon="IconDashboard">

A replay is far more useful with its DevTools panel filled in. Turn on [console log recording](/docs/session-replay/console-log-recording) to see logs, warnings, and errors on the timeline, and [network performance capture](/docs/session-replay/network-recording) to get the request waterfall, including headers and bodies if you opt into them.

Apps that draw to a `<canvas>` element need [canvas recording](/docs/session-replay/canvas-recording), and apps that embed other PostHog-instrumented pages need [iframe recording](/docs/session-replay/iframes).

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/session-replay/network-recording">Capture performance data</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Watch, filter, and share replays" subtitle="Your team's playback" icon="IconRewindPlay">

Open **Replay** in PostHog and you get a filterable list of sessions plus a player with a synced activity timeline. Filter by rage clicks, exceptions, custom events, person properties, or feature flag variant, then save what you find as a collection and [share or embed](/docs/session-replay/sharing) it.

<QuestLogItem title="Let PostHog AI do the watching" subtitle="You can't watch them all" icon="IconMagic">

Nobody watches every recording. Ask [PostHog AI](/docs/posthog-ai) in the web app to [find replays](/docs/session-replay/find-replays-ai) in plain language – "users who rage clicked on the pricing page" – and to [summarize sessions](/docs/session-replay/session-summaries-ai) so you get the pattern instead of an afternoon of playback.

Prefer your editor? The [MCP server](/docs/session-replay/surfaces/mcp) exposes the same search from Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, and other MCP clients.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/session-replay/session-summaries-ai">Summarize sessions</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Let Self-driving watch them for you" subtitle="Replay feeds Self-driving" icon="IconMagic">

Session replay isn't just playback – it's a signal source. PostHog looks for the moments users get stuck, clusters them across sessions, and files a [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving) report with the recordings that prove it. The report goes to a human reviewer, not a coding agent, because a replay shows you the symptom and working out the cause takes judgment.

<QuestLogItem title="Know what's free" subtitle="Usage-based, no per-seat fee" icon="IconPiggyBank">

Session replay is free up to a monthly allowance of web and mobile recordings, then priced per recording with volume discounts. There are no per-seat charges, and you can set a billing limit so a traffic spike can't surprise you. Recording rules and [data retention](/docs/session-replay/recording-retention) are the two biggest levers on your bill.
