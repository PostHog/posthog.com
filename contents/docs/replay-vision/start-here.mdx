This walkthrough takes you from zero to your first observation in about five minutes. We'll have PostHog AI draft a scanner from a common starting point, run it on a recording you pick, and then query the result as a regular PostHog event.

You should already have [Session Replay](/docs/session-replay) recording sessions for your project. Replay Vision watches those recordings – without them there's nothing to scan.

## AI wizard

The fastest way to get set up is our wizard. Run it in your project directory and it reads your codebase, installs the PostHog SDK if you don't have it yet, turns Session Replay on, and creates three scanners written for your product: one that watches your key flow for breakage, one that watches for user frustration, and one that summarizes sessions. It also works for [LLM coding agents](/blog/envoy-wizard-llm-agent) like Cursor and Bolt:

<WizardCommand command="replay-vision" />

The scanners it creates are ordinary scanners. You can edit the prompt, filters, and sampling rate afterwards, or delete them and start over.

Prefer to create your first scanner by hand, or want to understand each piece? Follow the steps below.

## 1. Open Replay Vision and start a scanner

Open **Replay Vision** from the side menu, then click **New scanner**. Replay Vision sends recordings to an AI model, so your organization has to allow AI data processing first. If it hasn't yet, the button reads **Allow AI analysis and create scanner** for admins, or **Ask an admin to enable AI analysis** for everyone else.

<ProductScreenshot

  alt="The What should the scanner find out? page: a goal box, a Free or Set a budget choice, and common starting points"
  classes="rounded"

The **What should the scanner find out?** page gives you three ways to start:

- **Describe a goal** – type which sessions to watch and what you want to find out, and PostHog AI drafts the whole scanner for your project.
- **Common starting points** – one-click questions, such as "Where do people get stuck?", that PostHog AI drafts into a scanner the same way.
- **Create from scratch** – pick a type and write the prompt yourself.

Under **How much should this scanner use?**, leave **Free** selected if it's available. The scanner then uses only your organization's free monthly credits and never adds to your bill.

Click **Where do people get stuck?** to follow along. PostHog AI drafts a scanner fitted to your product. This takes a few seconds. Nothing is saved until you click **Create scanner**, and you can change anything before then.

## 2. Look over the scanner

<ProductScreenshot
  imageLight="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/q_auto,f_auto/rv_goal_draft_overview_f3a666ab8e.png"
  alt="A drafted scanner's overview: what PostHog AI understood, the name, what it will ask, eligible recordings, and sampling and budget, each with an Edit button"
  classes="rounded"
/>

The draft opens on an overview of what PostHog AI set up:

- **What it understood** – your goal in PostHog AI's words.
- **Name** – the scanner's name and a short description.
- **What it will ask** – the scanner type and the prompt, for example a monitor that describes what "stuck" looks like on your pages.
- **Eligible recordings** – the recording filters, chosen from your product's real pages and events, with a count of matching recordings from recent days.
- **Sampling and budget** – the model, the sampling rate, the session coverage, and the estimated cost.

Each section has an **Edit** button that opens the matching step of the scanner editor: **Details**, **Configure**, **Recordings**, or **Budget**. **Start over** throws the draft away.

The draft picks its own scanner type. The rest of this walkthrough assumes a monitor, so if the draft chose another type, the result in step 5 and the query in step 6 look different. See [scanner types](/docs/replay-vision/scanner-types) for what each one returns.

For a first run, leave the prompt and model alone. To keep volume low while you experiment, narrow the filters, for example to one page or a specific cohort, and lower the sampling rate.

<CalloutBox type="fyi" title="Estimate your usage first">

The overview and the editor's **Budget** step show an estimated monthly cost based on your project's recent recording volume, the filters you set, the session coverage, the sampling rate, and the model. Use that to size against your [budget](/docs/replay-vision/quota-and-limits) before saving.

</CalloutBox>

## 3. Save the scanner

Click **Create scanner**. The scanner is enabled by default. On its first sweep it scans a few matching recordings from the last 24 hours, so you see results quickly, and then it picks up new matching recordings every few minutes.

For this walkthrough we won't wait for the sweep – we'll trigger one observation by hand against a recording we already know about.

## 4. Run the scanner on one recording

Open the [replay](/docs/session-replay) for any session of yours, open the **Observations** tab in the player sidebar, and click **Scan this recording**.

<ProductScreenshot
  imageLight="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/q_auto,f_auto/rv_player_observations_tab_93b1c58fc9.png"
  alt="The replay player with the Observations sidebar open: the Scan this recording action, a chapter timeline, and the recording's observations"
  classes="rounded"
/>

Pick the scanner you just created. PostHog renders the recording, sends it to Gemini, and produces a result. This usually takes a couple of minutes.

You can also run a scanner from its **Run** tab, or programmatically – see [running scanners](/docs/replay-vision/running-scanners).

## 5. Open the observation

Once it finishes, the observation appears in two places: on the scanner's **Observations** tab, and in the **Observations** tab of the replay player for the recording you scanned. Open the observation to see the structured result.

<ProductScreenshot
  imageLight="https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/q_auto,f_auto/rv_observation_detail_monitor_ec8866d719.png"
  alt="An observation page for a monitor scanner: the question, a Yes verdict with confidence, reasoning with timestamp citations, and the rating control, next to the player"
  classes="rounded"
/>

For a monitor scanner you'll see:

- A **yes/no verdict** with a confidence score.
- The model's **reasoning**, with **clickable citations** that jump the embedded player to the relevant moment.
- The **prompt that produced this result** – the exact text Gemini saw, frozen even if you edit the scanner afterwards.

Spend a minute here. Watching the moments the model cited next to its reasoning is the fastest way to develop intuition for what your prompt actually catches. Then answer **Did the scanner get this right?** with a thumbs up or down, and add a note if it missed something. The scanner learns from your team's ratings on later scans – see [improving scanners with ratings](/docs/replay-vision/calibration).

## 6. Query it as an event

Every successful observation is also captured as a `$recording_observed` event in your project. Open a new [SQL insight](/docs/data-warehouse/sql) and run the query below, swapping in your scanner's name for `Dead-end pages`:

```sql
SELECT
    toStartOfDay(timestamp) AS day,
    count() AS flagged_sessions
FROM events
WHERE event = '$recording_observed'
  AND properties.scanner_name = 'Dead-end pages'
  AND properties.scanner_output_verdict = 'yes'
  AND timestamp > now() - INTERVAL 7 DAY
GROUP BY day
ORDER BY day
```

You'll see a count over time of sessions the scanner flagged. From here you can chart it, drop it on a dashboard, or wire an alert. See [observations](/docs/replay-vision/observations#querying-observations-as-events) for more SQL patterns.
