## Send email from your code

Send the subject and HTML with one call from the PostHog SDK on your server:

<MultiLanguage selector="tabs">

```js file=Node.js
import { PostHog } from "posthog-node"

const posthog = new PostHog("<ph_project_token>", {
    host: "<ph_client_api_host>",
    secretKey: process.env.POSTHOG_PROJECT_SECRET_API_KEY,
    enableLocalEvaluation: false,
})

await posthog.emails.send({
    to: "ada@example.com",
    templateKey: "welcome",
    subject: "Welcome aboard",
    html: "<p>Hi Ada, thanks for signing up.</p>",
    distinctId: "user_123",
})
```

The distinct ID links the email to the person, so its events land next to everything else they did. To let anyone change the email without a deploy, create it in PostHog and send it by [template ID](/docs/workflows/transactional/templates) instead. You can also send over the [API](/docs/workflows/installation/api), or render your [React Email](/docs/workflows/transactional/from-code#react-email) components. To set up your key, see [install the SDK](/docs/workflows/installation).

## Plan sends on the calendar

See your broadcasts and scheduled automations on one calendar. When a send would reach the same people too soon after another marketing email, PostHog warns you and suggests a better day. The spacing follows your [frequency cap](/docs/workflows/email/frequency-cap), or 2 days when you have none. See [scheduling and the calendar](/docs/workflows/broadcasts/scheduling).

## Build automations

An automation starts when someone does something, then waits, branches, and sends email or push notifications. It can also send a Slack message to your team. See [automations](/docs/workflows/automations).

With [self-driving](/docs/workflows/self-driving) turned on, PostHog watches how each step performs and suggests changes. Nothing changes until you approve it.

## Deliverability

- **No IP address to warm up.** Email goes out over shared Amazon SES IP addresses with a good reputation. See [deliverability](/docs/workflows/email/deliverability).
- **Transactional email never waits.** Password resets and receipts go out at once, even when your daily allowance for other email is used up.
- **Opt-out categories.** People can turn off your newsletter and still get their password reset. See [opt-outs](/docs/workflows/email/opt-outs).
- **Automatic suppression list.** A hard bounce blocks all email to the address. A spam complaint blocks marketing email. No automation step can skip this check. See [suppression list](/docs/workflows/email/suppression).
- **Frequency cap.** Set how many marketing messages one person can get in a window of days. See [frequency cap](/docs/workflows/email/frequency-cap).
