Workflows sends email and other messages to your users from the events and person data already in PostHog. It does three jobs: transactional email that your code sends to one person, broadcasts to an audience, and automations that react to what each person does. Every send, open, and click comes back as an event on the person who got it.

Because it runs on the same events and person properties you already send to PostHog, you act on real product behavior instead of a separate marketing dataset. Any [real-time destination](/docs/data-pipelines/destinations) in PostHog can be dropped into a workflow as a dispatch step, so one tool covers both messaging and automation.

## Which one do you need?

| You want to | Use | Start with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Send one email to one person when your code says so, such as a password reset, a sign-in code, a receipt, or an invite | Transactional email | [Send transactional emails](/docs/workflows/transactional-emails) |
| Send one email to many people, once or on a schedule, such as a product update, a changelog, or an event invite | Broadcasts | [Send a broadcast](/docs/workflows/broadcasts) |
| Send messages that react to what each person does, such as an onboarding sequence, a re-engagement email, or a Slack alert when someone upgrades | Automations | [Launch your first workflow](/docs/workflows/launch-workflow) |

Automations are built in the drag-and-drop builder. You decide when a workflow is triggered, who it reaches, and what happens at each step: triggers, delays, audience splits, message sends, and PostHog actions.

## Where you can use it

You build and launch workflows in the PostHog web app. MCP and the API are for scripting, reviewing, and automating that same work from outside the app.

## Where its data comes from

A workflow is only as good as what it can trigger on and who it can reach. Three things feed it: the events and person data already in your project, the channels you connect, and the templates and opt-out rules that decide what gets sent to whom.
