PostHog watches how each automation performs, such as how many people open and click each email. When a step underperforms, it suggests one change, such as a new subject line. Nothing changes until you approve the suggestion and publish it.

## Turn it on

Self-driving is in beta, and works only on automations that send email. Your organization needs [AI data processing](/docs/posthog-ai/allow-access) turned on.

Open an automation, go to the **Self-driving** tab, and turn on **Suggest improvements**. PostHog can start to suggest once the automation:

- Has been live for 2 days.
- Has sent enough emails from its busiest email step in the last 7 days. The tab shows how many more it needs.

After that, PostHog checks the automation on a schedule, and the tab shows when the next check is.

Set a [conversion goal](/docs/workflows/automations/triggers#conversion-goal-and-exit), and suggestions can aim at conversions, not only opens and clicks.

## Review a suggestion

Each suggestion shows:

- **The change:** what it changes, such as an email's subject or content. **Compare emails** shows the old and new email side by side.
- **The evidence:** the numbers behind it, and how many sends they came from. With too little data, it says so.

Click **Approve as draft** to stage the change, then publish the automation when you're ready. Or click **Reject**, and say why, such as "this is the last email in the series, so clicks aren't its goal". A rejected suggestion stays in the history, and nothing changes.

## See if it worked

After you publish a suggestion, PostHog compares the step before and after the change, version by version.

## Over MCP

Your coding agent can file suggestions for you to review, and list the ones that PostHog filed. Ask "What has PostHog suggested for our onboarding automation?" over [MCP](/docs/workflows/mcp).
