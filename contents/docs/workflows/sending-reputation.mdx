This page explains what you get when PostHog sends your email, transactional or marketing. In short:

- **No IP address to warm up.** Your email goes out over Amazon SES IP addresses that many senders share, so you don't need a dedicated IP address. See [shared sending infrastructure](#shared-sending-infrastructure).
- **Your domain keeps its reputation.** You send from your own domain, and you can set it up next to your old provider before you switch. See [move from another provider](#move-from-another-provider).
- **Transactional email never waits.** It counts toward your daily allowance, but it goes out at once even when the allowance is empty, so you can move it on day one. See [the allowance FAQ](#do-transactional-emails-count-against-the-allowance).
- **Marketing volume grows with your record.** A sending tier paces your other email, from 100 emails per day up to 1,000,000. If you move from another provider and need more sooner, [request a higher allowance](#can-i-request-a-higher-tier).

Every project holds a sending tier from 0 to 7. The tier caps how many emails your workflows send per day, and how large one batch audience can be. A new project starts at tier 0 and moves up one tier at a time as it sends with low bounce and spam complaint rates.

Why the caps? All workflow email leaves through PostHog's shared sending infrastructure. Mailbox providers judge that infrastructure on the bounce and complaint rates of everything it sends. One project with a bad list hurts deliverability for every project. The tiers keep an unproven project's volume small until its record shows it is safe.

You see both parts on the **Reputation** tab under Workflows in [PostHog](https://app.posthog.com/workflows/reputation). The sending health card shows your rates over the last 30 days with a per-workflow breakdown. The sending allowance card shows your tier, its caps, and how much of them you used.

## Sending health

The health card reports three numbers over a trailing 30-day window.

| Number | What it measures | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bounce rate | Hard bounces divided by emails sent | Stay under 2% |
| Spam complaint rate | Spam complaints divided by emails sent | Stay under 0.1% |
| Emails sent | Workflow emails sent in the window | |

Mailbox providers use the same thresholds. A project above either threshold cannot move up a tier, and PostHog can move it down. [Opt-outs and suppression](/docs/workflows/opt-outs) explains how PostHog keeps hard-bounced addresses off your lists. The [troubleshooting guide](/docs/workflows/troubleshooting#debugging-email-delivery-failures) shows how to find the invocations behind a bounce or complaint.

## Sending allowance tiers

| Tier | Emails per day | Largest batch audience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 100 | 100 |
| 1 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| 2 | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| 3 | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| 4 | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 5 | 100,000 | 100,000 |
| 6 | 300,000 | 300,000 |
| 7 | 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 |

The tiers pace workflow email and broadcasts. Transactional email counts toward the allowance but never waits for it, so you can move all of your transactional email on day one, even at tier 0. It still uses up allowance, which your other email then waits for.

The daily allowance refills continuously, not at midnight. A project with a full allowance can send its whole daily cap at once. After that, the allowance refills at one day's cap spread over 24 hours.

Tier 0 is a probation tier for a domain with no sending record. Its caps sit far below tier 1 on purpose. A first real send from an unknown domain is the send most likely to generate complaints, and a small cap keeps the damage small while complaint feedback catches up.

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Emails above the cap wait, they are not lost" type="fyi">

An email that does not fit in the current allowance stays queued. The worker sends it once the allowance refills. The workflow's invocations show the delay. This is different from the per-person [frequency cap](/docs/workflows/frequency-cap), which skips marketing messages over the cap unless you set a hold time.

A batch send is different. When the audience is larger than the tier allows, the trigger button blocks the launch and shows the current limit. Add filters to narrow the audience, set a sending rate limit, or wait for the next tier. This batch cap only applies to workflows with an email step.

A sending rate limit raises the batch cap. All of your workflows share the daily allowance, so a batch that sends slowly leaves room for the others. With a rate limit of at most half your daily allowance, the batch cap becomes the number of people that rate sends to in 7 days, when that is more than the tier's cap. That is up to about 3.5 times your daily allowance. For example, at tier 3, a rate of 200 emails per hour lets a batch reach 33,600 people. You can't change the rate limit while such a batch still sends.

</CalloutBox>

## How a project moves up a tier

PostHog reviews every project's tier once a day. A project moves up one tier when all four conditions hold.

1. **Time at the tier.** The project held its current tier for at least 3 days at tiers 0 to 2, 5 days at tiers 3 and 4, and 7 days from tier 5 up. A full climb from tier 0 to tier 7 takes about five weeks, which matches the warmup period mailbox providers expect from a new sender.
2. **Use of the tier.** On at least 2 separate days since it reached the tier, the project sent at least half of its daily cap. A project that sends nothing has no record to judge, so an unused tier does not earn the next one.
3. **Clean rates over the last 30 days.** The bounce rate stays under 2% and the spam complaint rate under 0.1%. PostHog only judges a rate once there is enough volume to measure it: 200 sends for the bounce rate and 1,000 sends for the complaint rate. Below those volumes, 3 or more complaints still block the promotion.
4. **No reputation flag from the sending provider.** The provider tracks complaint rates against its own feedback loops, which can show a problem your internal rates do not.

A project moves up one tier per review. Sending a large volume once does not skip a tier.

## How a project moves down a tier

A project moves down one tier when any of the following happens over the last 7 days.

- Its bounce rate or spam complaint rate goes over the thresholds above.
- The sending provider reports a high reputation impact for the project.

After such a demotion, PostHog waits 7 days before it demotes the project again for the same kind of reason. One incident costs one tier, not the whole ladder. The project can move up again once its 30-day rates are clean and it meets the other promotion conditions.

Two more events change a tier.

- **Inactivity.** A project above tier 0 that sends nothing for 30 days drops one tier, and one more for each further 30 days of silence. Mailbox providers keep about a month of reputation history, so an allowance the project stopped using no longer reflects a current record.
- **Sending suspended.** When PostHog staff or the sending provider suspend email sending for the project, the project returns to tier 0. It climbs again from there once sending resumes.

## Shared sending infrastructure

PostHog sends workflow email through Amazon SES, from one sending account per cloud region (US and EU). Each project is its own [SES tenant](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/tenants.html) in that account. Amazon tracks bounce and complaint rates per tenant, so it can pause one project's sending without stopping the others. All projects in a region still share the account's overall reputation, which is why the tiers above exist.

Your email goes out over IP addresses that Amazon SES shares between many senders. Mailbox providers also judge your sending domain on its own, and that reputation stays with the domain, whichever provider sends for it.

## Move from another provider

Your domain keeps its reputation when you move, because mailbox providers judge the domain, not only the provider that sends for it. They still notice when a domain starts to send from new infrastructure, so move gradually.

### Set up your domain next to your old provider

You can add PostHog's DNS records while your old provider still sends. The [Channels tab](/docs/workflows/configure-channels) shows each record for your domain.

- **DKIM.** PostHog gives you three CNAME records under `_domainkey`, with names that only PostHog uses. Your old provider's DKIM records stay as they are, and each provider signs its own email.
- **SPF.** PostHog lists an SPF record with `include:amazonses.com` for your domain. A domain can have only one SPF record, and a second one makes SPF fail for all email from the domain. If your domain already has an SPF record, add `include:amazonses.com` to it instead of creating a new record. Your domain verifies without it, because PostHog's email passes SPF on the bounce subdomain below. Skip it if your record is close to SPF's limit of 10 DNS lookups.
- **Bounce subdomain.** PostHog also needs a subdomain for bounces, `feedback` by default, with one MX record and one SPF record. Nothing else may use it to send or receive email. If something already uses `feedback`, set another **Bounce subdomain** under **Advanced** when you add the domain.
- **DMARC.** If your domain already has a DMARC record, keep it. PostHog only checks that one exists.

Your old provider keeps sending as long as its records stay in place. Remove them only after its last email goes out, or that email fails authentication.

### Move your email in steps

1. **Verify your domain.** Add your sending domain or subdomain on the [Channels tab](/docs/workflows/configure-channels), as above.
2. **Bring your lists first.** Import your old provider's bounces, complaints, and unsubscribes before the first send. Add bounces and complaints in bulk with the [messaging suppressions API](/docs/api/messaging-suppressions), and set each entry's `reason` to `bounce` or `complaint`. Only a `bounce` entry also blocks transactional email. Add unsubscribes with the [messaging preferences API](/docs/api/messaging-preferences). Sending to an address that already bounced or opted out costs reputation right away.
3. **Start with engaged recipients.** Move transactional email and your most active users first. Move the rest over a few weeks. The sending tiers pace the move as well. If they are too slow for your current volume, [request a higher allowance](#can-i-request-a-higher-tier).
4. **Watch the Reputation tab.** Check your bounce and complaint rates after each step, before you move more volume.

Amazon's [guide to migrating to Amazon SES](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/messaging-and-targeting/guide-to-ip-and-domain-warming-and-migrating-to-amazon-ses/) has more detail.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to warm up an IP address?

No. Your email goes out over shared IP addresses that already carry steady traffic, so there is no new IP address for you to warm up. The sending tiers raise your volume step by step, but they are a trust limit for your project, not IP warming.

### Can I get a dedicated IP address?

No. PostHog doesn't offer dedicated IP addresses, and there is no premium tier for transactional email. A dedicated IP address only builds a reputation with large, regular volume, and [Amazon recommends shared IP addresses](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/dedicated-ip.html) for everyone else. Transactional email gets its priority from its message category instead, as [the allowance answer below](#do-transactional-emails-count-against-the-allowance) explains.

### How do I keep marketing email from hurting transactional email?

Separate the two streams in three steps, from least to most isolation.

1. **Use a transactional category.** Put each transactional email step in a transactional [message category](/docs/workflows/opt-outs#message-categories). It then goes out ahead of queued marketing email and never waits for the daily cap. Use it only for email a person needs, such as receipts and password resets, because it also skips opt-outs.
2. **Use a subdomain per stream.** Send marketing from one subdomain, such as `news.example.com`, and transactional email from another, such as `account.example.com`. Verify both on the [Channels tab](/docs/workflows/configure-channels). A spike in marketing complaints then lands on the marketing subdomain's reputation, not on the one your password resets use.
3. **Use a project per stream.** In one project, both streams share a tier, an allowance, and an SES tenant. A suspension stops both. Send each stream from its own PostHog project to give each its own tier, allowance, and tenant. A new project starts at tier 0 and climbs on its own record, so move the stream that needs less volume, such as transactional email, which never waits for the allowance.

All projects in a region still share the regional SES account, so these steps reduce the risk but don't remove it.

### Can I send through my own SMTP server or email provider?

No. Workflow email always goes out through PostHog's Amazon SES setup. You bring the sending domain, not the mail server, so you can't connect your own SMTP server or an email provider account.

### Can I request a higher tier?

Sending earns tiers, and the daily review is the same for every project. If you have a sending history elsewhere, for example because you are moving from another provider, and need more volume sooner, ask for a higher allowance. Click **Request a higher allowance** on the sending allowance card of the [Reputation tab](https://app.posthog.com/workflows/reputation). It opens a support form, filled in with your project's sending record. Add your use case and send the form. The link shows for organizations that can open a support ticket, such as those on a paid plan. If you are on such a plan and don't see the link, [contact support](https://app.posthog.com/#panel=support) and explain your use case.

### Do transactional emails count against the allowance?

They count, but they never wait. An email in a transactional [message category](/docs/workflows/opt-outs#message-categories), and every email from the [transactional email API](/docs/workflows/transactional-emails), uses up allowance like any other email. When the allowance is empty, it still goes out at once, ahead of queued marketing email. A password reset that arrives two hours late has already failed.

Transactional email still shares the project's reputation. Its bounces and complaints count in your rates, and when sending is suspended for the project, transactional email stops with everything else.

### Does the allowance apply to SMS, push, or webhooks?

No. Only email touches the shared sending reputation, so only email steps and batch sends with an email step are capped.

### How do I keep my bounce and complaint rates low?

A bounce means the address does not exist or refuses mail. A complaint means a real person got your email and marked it as spam. Both come down to who you send to and what you send.

**Send only to people who asked.** Every address on your list should come from a signup, a purchase, or an explicit opt-in in your product. Do not import a purchased, scraped, or "partner" list. Those lists are the fastest way to a demotion, because they hold dead addresses that bounce and strangers who report spam.

**Trust the suppression list.** PostHog adds an address to the [suppression list](/docs/workflows/opt-outs#the-suppression-list) after it hard-bounces. Leave it there. Removing an entry to retry a bounced address only bounces again and counts twice.

**Make unsubscribing easy.** Put `{{unsubscribe_url}}` in every marketing email, in the footer where people expect it. A visible unsubscribe link is the difference between someone leaving your list and someone clicking "Report spam". Both remove them, but only one hurts your reputation. The [email merge tags](/docs/workflows/library#email-merge-tags) explain the one-click variant, which mailbox providers use for their own unsubscribe button.

**Send from a domain you set up properly.** Verify the domain on the [Channels tab](/docs/workflows/configure-channels) so SPF and DKIM pass, and add a DMARC record. Use a real sender name people recognize, and the same from-address every time. A changing sender or a generic "noreply" address gets more complaints.

**Match the subject to the content.** A subject line that promises one thing and delivers another is the most common reason a real customer reports an email. Skip all caps, bare exclamation marks, and "RE:" on emails that are not replies. Write the way you would to one person.

**Send steadily, not in bursts.** A weekly digest to an active list builds reputation. A one-off blast to a list that has not heard from you in six months burns it, because the addresses have gone stale and the recipients have forgotten who you are. If you must re-engage a dormant list, send to the most recently active users first and watch the rates on the Reputation tab before you continue.

**Prune addresses that never engage.** Someone who has not opened or clicked anything in a year is unlikely to start, and mailbox providers read a long run of ignored mail as a signal against you. Use a [cohort](/docs/data/cohorts) of engaged users as the audience instead of "everyone".

Here is what this looks like in practice.

<CalloutBox icon="IconWarning" title="Likely to bounce or get reported" type="caution">

Subject: `🔥🔥 LAST CHANCE!!! Your account will be DELETED`

Sent from `noreply@mail-blast-3.example.com` to every address ever entered into a signup form, including the ones that bounced last quarter. No unsubscribe link, one image with all the text inside it, and a link shortener in front of every URL.

</CalloutBox>

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Likely to land in the inbox" type="fyi">

Subject: `Your weekly usage summary for Example App`

Sent from `Jamie at Example App <jamie@example.com>` to users who signed up and have logged in within the last 90 days. Plain text with one clear link to the dashboard, a short line explaining why they got the email, and `{{unsubscribe_url}}` in the footer.

</CalloutBox>

Before a large send, use a [test send](/docs/workflows/library#testing-templates) to your own address and to a personal account on a big mailbox provider. If it lands in spam for you, it will for your users too. After the send, check the Reputation tab the next day. Complaint feedback lags by hours, so the rates for a send are only complete after that.
