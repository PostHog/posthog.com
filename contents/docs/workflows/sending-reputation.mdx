Every project holds a sending tier from 0 to 7. The tier caps how many emails your workflows send per hour and per day, and how large one batch audience can be. A new project starts at tier 0 and moves up one tier at a time as it sends with low bounce and spam complaint rates.

Why the caps? All workflow email leaves through PostHog's shared sending infrastructure. Mailbox providers judge that infrastructure on the bounce and complaint rates of everything it sends. One project with a bad list hurts deliverability for every project. The tiers keep an unproven project's volume small until its record shows it is safe.

You see both parts on the **Reputation** tab under Workflows in [PostHog](https://app.posthog.com/workflows/reputation). The sending health card shows your rates over the last 30 days with a per-workflow breakdown. The sending allowance card shows your tier, its caps, and how much of them you used.

## Sending health

The health card reports three numbers over a trailing 30-day window.

| Number | What it measures | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bounce rate | Hard bounces divided by emails sent | Stay under 2% |
| Spam complaint rate | Spam complaints divided by emails sent | Stay under 0.1% |
| Emails sent | Workflow emails sent in the window | |

Mailbox providers use the same thresholds. A project above either threshold cannot move up a tier, and PostHog can move it down. [Opt-outs and suppression](/docs/workflows/opt-outs) explains how PostHog keeps hard-bounced addresses off your lists. The [troubleshooting guide](/docs/workflows/troubleshooting#debugging-email-delivery-failures) shows how to find the invocations behind a bounce or complaint.

## Sending allowance tiers

| Tier | Emails per hour | Emails per day | Largest batch audience |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 50 | 100 | 100 |
| 1 | 200 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| 2 | 600 | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| 3 | 2,000 | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| 4 | 6,000 | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 5 | 20,000 | 100,000 | 100,000 |
| 6 | 60,000 | 300,000 | 300,000 |
| 7 | 200,000 | 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 |

Tier 0 is a probation tier for a domain with no sending record. Its caps sit far below tier 1 on purpose. A first real send from an unknown domain is the send most likely to generate complaints, and a small cap keeps the damage small while complaint feedback catches up.

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Emails above the cap wait, they are not lost" type="fyi">

An email that does not fit in the current hour or day stays queued. The worker sends it once the allowance frees up. The workflow's invocations show the delay.

A batch send is different. When the audience is larger than the tier allows, the trigger button blocks the launch and shows the current limit. Add filters to narrow the audience, or wait for the next tier. This batch cap only applies to workflows with an email step.

</CalloutBox>

## How a project moves up a tier

PostHog reviews every project's tier once a day. A project moves up one tier when all four conditions hold.

1. **Time at the tier.** The project held its current tier for at least 3 days at tiers 0 to 2, 5 days at tiers 3 and 4, and 7 days from tier 5 up. A full climb from tier 0 to tier 7 takes about five weeks, which matches the warmup period mailbox providers expect from a new sender.
2. **Use of the tier.** On at least 2 separate days since it reached the tier, the project sent at least half of its daily cap. A project that sends nothing has no record to judge, so an unused tier does not earn the next one.
3. **Clean rates over the last 30 days.** The bounce rate stays under 2% and the spam complaint rate under 0.1%. PostHog only judges a rate once there is enough volume to measure it: 200 sends for the bounce rate and 1,000 sends for the complaint rate. Below those volumes, 3 or more complaints still block the promotion.
4. **No reputation flag from the sending provider.** The provider tracks complaint rates against its own feedback loops, which can show a problem your internal rates do not.

A project moves up one tier per review. Sending a large volume once does not skip a tier.

## How a project moves down a tier

A project moves down one tier when any of the following happens over the last 7 days.

- Its bounce rate or spam complaint rate goes over the thresholds above.
- PostHog auto-pauses one of its workflows for reputation problems.
- The sending provider reports a high reputation impact for the project.

After such a demotion, PostHog waits 7 days before it demotes the project again for the same kind of reason. One incident costs one tier, not the whole ladder. The project can move up again once its 30-day rates are clean and it meets the other promotion conditions.

Two more events change a tier.

- **Inactivity.** A project above tier 0 that sends nothing for 30 days drops one tier, and one more for each further 30 days of silence. Mailbox providers keep about a month of reputation history, so an allowance the project stopped using no longer reflects a current record.
- **Sending suspended.** When PostHog staff or the sending provider suspend email sending for the project, the project returns to tier 0. It climbs again from there once sending resumes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I request a higher tier?

Sending earns tiers, and the daily review is the same for every project. If you have a verified sending history elsewhere and need volume from day one, [contact support](https://app.posthog.com/#panel=support) and explain your use case.

### Do transactional emails count against the allowance?

Yes. The allowance counts every email a workflow sends, regardless of message category.

### Does the allowance apply to SMS, push, or webhooks?

No. Only email touches the shared sending reputation, so only email steps and batch sends with an email step are capped.
