Before a workflow sends anything, PostHog checks two lists: the recipient's **opt-out preferences** and the project's **suppression list**. Both live under **Workflows** in [PostHog](https://app.posthog.com), and neither is something a workflow can override.

The difference matters. An opt-out is a choice the recipient made – they don't want this kind of message. A suppression is a delivery fact – mail to this address bounced, or you added it by hand, so PostHog stops trying.

## Message categories

Opt-outs are organized into **message categories**, so someone can decline your product newsletter and still get a password reset. Each category has a name, an internal description, a public description shown to recipients on the preferences page, and a type – **marketing** or **transactional**.

Create the categories you actually send against, then pick one on each message step in the [workflow builder](/docs/workflows/workflow-builder). Recipients who have opted out of that category are skipped. You can also manage categories over the [API](/docs/workflows/surfaces/api) or [MCP](/docs/workflows/surfaces/mcp).

## The opt-out list

The opt-out list shows who has opted out of which category. Recipients land on it in a few ways: a one-click unsubscribe from an email client, the preferences page behind `{{unsubscribe_url}}` in your [email templates](/docs/workflows/library#email-merge-tags), an import from another tool, or you adding them directly.

Every genuine transition from opted-in to opted-out also writes a `$workflows_email_unsubscribed` event, carrying the `category` and the `source` (`one_click` or `preferences_page`). See [workflow events](/docs/workflows/engagement-events#properties-on-unsubscribe-events) for the full property list, and note that repeated unsubscribe requests do not emit duplicate events.

Coming from Customer.io? [Import your opt-out lists](/docs/workflows/import-customerio-optouts) and keep them in sync with a reporting webhook.

## The suppression list

The suppression list is address-level and category-independent – a suppressed recipient receives nothing. Each entry records the recipient, how it got there (**bounces** or **manual**), the reason or last bounce diagnostic, and when it was suppressed.

PostHog adds addresses automatically when mail to them hard-bounces, which is what protects your sending domain's reputation. You can add an address by hand, and you can remove an entry if you believe the bounce was a one-off – but removing a genuine hard bounce means you'll bounce again.

## Frequency cap

The frequency cap limits how many marketing messages one person gets in a rolling number of days. It counts email, SMS, and push messages from all workflows and broadcasts together. With a cap of 2 messages in 7 days, a person who got 2 marketing messages gets no more until the older of the two is 7 days old.

Set the cap in PostHog under **Workflows** > **Messaging** > [**Settings**](https://app.posthog.com/workflows/settings):

- **Messages per person**: The most marketing messages one person can get in the window, from 1 to 1,000.
- **Within this many days**: The length of the window, from 1 to 30 days.
- **Hold capped messages for up to (hours)**: Optional. How long a message over the cap waits for a free slot, from 1 to 168 hours. See [holding messages over the cap](#holding-messages-over-the-cap).

Leave the first two fields empty to turn the cap off.

The cap applies with these rules:

- Transactional messages are never capped, and they don't count toward a person's cap.
- A message counts when PostHog sends it. A message that PostHog skips for an opt-out or a suppression doesn't use up the cap.
- A workflow run without a person isn't capped, for example a run that an event without a person profile triggered.
- Test sends aren't capped.

By default, PostHog skips a message over the cap, and the workflow moves on to the next step. The workflow's logs show the skip, and its metrics count the message as **Frequency capped**.

### Holding messages over the cap

When you set a hold time, a message over the cap waits instead. The workflow run stays on that step, and PostHog tries the send again when one of the person's earlier messages leaves the window. If no slot opens before the hold ends, PostHog skips the message and the workflow moves on. Later steps, including delays, start only after the held message sends or is skipped.

When more than one message waits for the same person, each free slot sends one of them. The others keep waiting for the next free slot. Held messages don't send in a fixed order.

A hold helps most when it is close to the window length. With a cap of 1 message in 7 days and a 24-hour hold, a held message only sends if the person's last message is more than 6 days old.

## Managing the lists over the API

Both lists are available over the [REST API](/docs/api), so your own product can honor an unsubscribe or a resubscribe without anyone opening PostHog.

The [messaging preferences API](/docs/api/messaging-preferences) manages the opt-out list: list a category's opt-outs, add them one at a time or up to 1,000 in bulk, remove one when a recipient opts back in, and export a category's list as CSV. Leave out the category to work with the global opt-out that covers all marketing messages.

Sends check the message's category and the global opt-out together, so opting someone back in to one marketing category also lifts a global opt-out if they have one. Their other marketing categories stay opted out, which keeps the resubscribe scoped to the category you named.

The [messaging suppressions API](/docs/api/messaging-suppressions) manages the suppression list: list entries, add one, or remove one.

Authenticate with a [personal API key](/docs/api/personal-api-keys) that has the `hog_flow:read` scope for reads or `hog_flow:write` for writes.
