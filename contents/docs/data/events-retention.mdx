---
title: Events data retention
---

PostHog Cloud keeps your events for a retention period, then deletes them. This page covers the default retention period, how to set a custom retention policy, and how to check which period applies to your project.

Some data follows different retention rules:

- [Session recordings](/docs/session-replay/recording-retention)  
- [AI events](/docs/ai-observability/data-retention)  
- [Logs](/docs/logs/pricing#retention)  
- [Data warehouse](/docs/data-warehouse) tables, which keep all of their rows  
- Person profiles and properties, which are stored indefinitely until you [delete them](/docs/privacy/data-storage#data-deletion)  
- Self-hosted instances, which keep events until you delete them

## Retention by plan

The Free plan has a default retention period of 1 year. Paid plans have a default retention period of 2 years. Your plan and [platform package](/platform-packages) set how far you can change it with a custom retention policy.

| Plan | Default retention period | Custom retention range |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| Free | 1 year | Not available |  
| Pay-as-you-go | 2 years | Up to 24 months (2 years) |  
| Pay-as-you-go with Boost | 2 years | Up to 36 months (3 years) |  
| Pay-as-you-go with Scale | 2 years | Up to 60 months (5 years) |  
| Pay-as-you-go with Enterprise | 2 years | Up to 84 months (7 years) |

After your default retention period ends, PostHog may keep events for a grace period (typically 1–3 months) before deleting them. Don't count on this grace period to recover expired data.

### Organizations on a paid plan before November 5, 2026

If your organization was on a paid plan before November 5, 2026, it keeps a 7-year default retention period and a custom retention range of up to 84 months. This lasts while it stays on the same plan and platform package, or moves to a bigger package. If it moves to the Free plan or to a smaller package, it gets the retention in the table above.

Organizations that start a paid plan on or after November 5, 2026 get the retention in the table, even if they were on the Free plan before.

## Custom retention

Custom retention isn't available on the Free plan. On paid plans, you can set a custom retention policy that's shorter or longer than your plan's default, up to the range for your plan. Common reasons to set a shorter policy include:

- Meeting compliance requirements  
- Data minimization, so you keep only the data you need  
- Faster dashboards, because queries scan less data

To keep events longer than 2 years, set a longer policy, up to your plan's range.

<CalloutBox type="caution" title="Deleted events can't be restored">

PostHog deletes events older than your custom retention policy at the next monthly cleanup.

</CalloutBox>

### To set a custom retention policy for your organization

Go to [**Settings > Organization > General > Events retention**](https://app.posthog.com/settings/organization-details#organization-events-retention). This policy applies to every project that doesn't have its own.

### To set a custom retention policy for one project

Go to [**Settings > Project > Product analytics > Events retention**](https://app.posthog.com/settings/environment-product-analytics#project-events-retention). This policy overrides the organization's.

If you remove a project's policy, the project uses the organization's policy. If the organization doesn't have one, the project uses your plan's default retention period.

### Who can change a retention policy

- Only organization admins can set or remove a custom retention policy. Project admins and members can view it but not change it.  
- Each setting shows the range of months your plan allows.  
- PostHog asks you to confirm before it saves or removes a custom retention policy.

## Check your retention period

Two read-only fields on the [projects API](/docs/api/projects) show your project's retention:

- `event_retention_months`: the length of the retention period in months. For example, `84` is 7 years.  
- `events_retention_enforced`: whether retention applies to your project yet.

<CalloutBox type="fyi" title="Enforcement is rolling out gradually">

PostHog is turning on retention project by project. While `events_retention_enforced` is `false`, retention doesn't remove any events from your project, whatever `event_retention_months` shows.

</CalloutBox>

## How retention works

Each project uses its own custom retention policy if it has one. If not, it uses the organization's policy, and if there isn't one, your plan's default retention period.

Once retention is enforced on your project:

- PostHog checks each event's `timestamp` against the retention period, not the time PostHog received the event. This way the event's real age decides when it expires.  
- Queries on the events table only return events inside your retention period.  
- If an insight's date range goes back further than your retention period, the insight shows a banner. That way you won't mistake missing data for a drop in your product.  
- Expired events are deleted after a grace period if you're on default retention, or at the next monthly cleanup if you've set a custom policy.

<CalloutBox type="caution" title="Migrating historical data">

Retention uses each event's `timestamp`, so events you bring in through a [migration](/docs/migrate) are deleted at the next cleanup if they're older than your retention period, even though you only just migrated them. Check your retention period before you migrate.

</CalloutBox>

## FAQ

#### Which events does retention apply to?

Every event in the events table, including error tracking exceptions, feature flag evaluations, survey responses and MCP analytics events.

#### Does retention delete person profiles?

No. Person profiles and their properties are stored indefinitely. To remove them, use the [data deletion tools](/docs/privacy/data-storage#data-deletion).

#### What happens to my retention period if I change plans?

If your custom retention policy is longer than your new plan allows, PostHog lowers it to the new plan's maximum. For example, a 60-month policy on Scale becomes 24 months on pay-as-you-go. Events older than the new period are deleted at the next monthly cleanup.

If you move to the Free plan, which doesn't offer custom retention, your projects go back to the 1-year default. If your organization kept 7-year retention from before November 5, 2026, moving to the Free plan or to a smaller package moves you to the default in the table. When you go back to a default retention period, events older than it are deleted after the usual grace period.

#### Do hidden events come back if I lengthen my retention period?

Yes, as long as PostHog hasn't deleted them yet. Events older than your retention period are hidden from queries until they're deleted. If you upgrade your plan or package, or set a longer custom retention policy, PostHog includes those events in queries again. Events that have already been deleted can't be restored.

#### When are expired events deleted?

On default retention, within a grace period of 1–3 months after the retention period ends. On a custom retention policy, at the next monthly cleanup. Deleted events can't be restored.

## Other ways to manage your data

Retention only deletes events once they expire. To delete data sooner, use the [data deletion tools](/docs/privacy/data-storage#data-deletion):

- Delete a person, along with their events and recordings, to handle a [right to be forgotten](/docs/privacy/data-storage#right-to-be-forgotten) request.  
- Delete a project or an organization to remove all of its events.

To keep events longer than your retention period, send them to your own storage with a [batch export](/docs/data-pipelines/batch-exports). Batch exports can only export events PostHog still has, so set one up before your events expire.
