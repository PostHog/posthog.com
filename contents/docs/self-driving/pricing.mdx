PostHog Self-driving is priced on usage. You pay for the work agents do on your behalf: watching the [sources](/docs/self-driving/inbox/sources) you turn on, running [scouts](/docs/self-driving/scouts), researching [reports](/docs/self-driving/reports), and opening pull requests.

Self-driving is charged in [AI credits](/docs/posthog-ai/pricing), the same unit other AI features in PostHog use. **1 AI credit = $0.01**.

The number of credits each piece of work uses varies with how much effort it takes. A quick check uses few credits, and a deep investigation uses more.

## Your first month

Your first month of Self-driving comes with **1,000 AI credits, worth $10**, so you can see what the agents find in your product before you pay anything. After that, there's no recurring free tier. You pay for what you use.

## How spend builds up

Knowing where spend comes from tells you which settings shape it.

- **Sources:** Each source you turn on sends [signals](/docs/self-driving/signals) to Self-driving. You pay for processing and grouping those signals, and for researching the reports they form.
- **Scout runs:** Each scheduled scout run costs money, whether it finds something or not. A scout's schedule affects its spend more than what it finds.
- **Pull requests:** Writing a pull request for an actionable report costs money.

## Track your spend

Self-driving shows your spend in these places:

- **Usage table:** An overview of spend per source type and across your scouts in the current billing period. Find it in the **Settings** tab in Self-driving.
- **Sources:** The **Sources** tab in Self-driving shows each source's spend in the current billing period.
- **Scouts:** The **Scouts** tab in Self-driving shows each scout's average cost per run in the scout list.

Self-driving spend also appears in your usage alongside the rest of your PostHog spend, on the [billing overview](https://app.posthog.com/organization/billing/overview) and [billing usage](https://app.posthog.com/organization/billing/usage) pages.

<CalloutBox icon="IconInfo" title="Important" type="fyi">

Usage data may take up to 24 hours to appear on the billing overview and billing usage pages.

</CalloutBox>

## Understand and optimize your usage

There are two ways to understand and optimize your Self-driving usage:

- [Ask your agent](#ask-your-agent) to analyze your usage and recommend changes. This is the easiest way.
- [Make changes manually](#make-changes-manually) to your sources, scouts, and other settings.

### Ask your agent

Ask your agent to run the [`managing-self-driving-spend`](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog/blob/master/products/signals/skills/managing-self-driving-spend/SKILL.md) skill through the [PostHog MCP](/docs/model-context-protocol), with a prompt like "Which sources and scouts drive my Self-driving usage?" or "How can I get more out of my Self-driving setup?"

The skill:

- Breaks your spend down by source, scout, and day
- Compares each source and scout's spend with how many of its reports got resolved
- Recommends changes ranked by impact, with what each one saves and what you give up
- Applies a change only after you confirm it

### Make changes manually

#### Sources

Your sources decide which reports get researched, so start here. [Turn off a source](/docs/self-driving/inbox/sources#configure-sources) to stop Self-driving from watching it. Reports that already exist stay in your inbox.

Before you turn off a whole source, check whether one part of it drives the spend. Some sources let you turn down part of the signals they send:

| Source                                         | What you can turn down                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Error tracking](/docs/error-tracking)         | Turn new, reopened, and spiking issues off separately.                                                                                                                  |
| [Replay Vision](/docs/replay-vision)           | Stop a scanner from sending findings to your inbox while it keeps scanning, lower the share of recordings it scans, or turn it off.                                     |
| [AI evals](/docs/ai-evals)                     | Turn off an evaluation report, run it less often, or cap its daily runs. This also changes that report's email and Slack delivery.                                     |
| [Alerts](/docs/alerts)                         | Turn off the agent that investigates an alert when it fires, or hold back investigations that come back inconclusive.                                                   |
| Linear                                         | Choose which teams' issues Self-driving watches.                                                                                                                        |

Other sources, such as support tickets, have no setting that selects part of the source. Keep them, turn them off, or [steer them](#steering-instructions).

#### Scouts

- **Pause a scout:** Stops its scheduled runs. Its past reports stay in your inbox.
- **[Run a scout less often](/docs/self-driving/scouts#setting-a-scouts-schedule):** Spend falls with the number of runs. Going from hourly to every 6 hours cuts that scout's spend by about five sixths.

A scout that rarely creates reports can still be useful if it keeps existing reports up to date. Check what it edits before you pause it.

#### Steering instructions

[Steering instructions](/docs/self-driving/steering) tell agents what to focus on and what to skip, for example "Skip support tickets that are low-priority questions about billing." They change what agents spend effort on, not how much work arrives, so their effect on spend is hard to predict. Check your spend again after two weeks.

#### Pull requests

- **PR generation:** Turn off automatic pull requests for actionable reports. Reports still land in your inbox.
- **Priority threshold:** Set the lowest report priority that opens a pull request automatically. Lower-priority fixes then need a manual start. You can also set your own threshold under **Your overrides**. The priorities, from highest to lowest, are **P0 – Critical**, **P1 – High**, **P2 – Medium**, **P3 – Low**, and **P4 – Minimal**.

#### Limits

| Limit                  | How it works                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Billing limit**      | A monthly cap on Self-driving spend. PostHog sets a default **$150** billing limit, which you can raise, lower, or remove on the [billing settings](https://app.posthog.com/organization/billing) page.                                |
| **Daily report limit** | Caps how many new reports appear each day, in your project's timezone. It saves money on days with spikes. Set it in the **Settings** tab in Self-driving.                                                                            |

When you reach either limit, new signals from your sources are dropped, scheduled scout runs are skipped, and research pauses. The billing limit resets at the next billing period, and the daily report limit resets at midnight.

<CalloutBox icon="IconWarning" title="Signals past a limit are lost" type="caution">

Limits drop new signals instead of queueing them. Issues found after you reach a limit never reach your inbox, even after the limit resets.

</CalloutBox>
