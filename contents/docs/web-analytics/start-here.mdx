<QuestLogItem title="Install PostHog on your site" subtitle="Where the data starts" icon="IconBook">

Web analytics runs on the `$pageview` events the PostHog SDK sends, so the only real setup step is getting PostHog onto your site. There are one-click options for Webflow, Framer, Shopify, WordPress, and GTM, and snippets for every major framework.

In a hurry? The [AI wizard](/docs/web-analytics/installation/ai-wizard) installs and configures PostHog for you in one command.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/web-analytics/installation">Install PostHog</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Read your dashboard" subtitle="Visitors, pages, and sources" icon="IconDashboard">

Once events arrive, open [Web analytics](https://app.posthog.com/web). The [dashboard](/docs/web-analytics/dashboard) gives you visitors, pageviews, [sessions](/docs/data/sessions), average session duration, and bounce rate, each compared to the previous period, plus your top paths, channels, referrers, UTMs, countries, and devices.

Click any row to filter the whole dashboard to it – then jump into the [session replays](/docs/session-replay) behind that number.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/web-analytics/dashboard">Explore the dashboard</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Add a conversion goal" subtitle="Traffic that actually matters" icon="IconFunnels">

Visitor counts only tell you half the story. A [conversion goal](/docs/web-analytics/conversion-goals) is the action you want people to take – a signup, a demo booking, a subscribe – and adding one puts conversions and conversion rate right next to your traffic numbers.

For signups, capture an explicit `user_signed_up` event rather than relying on autocapture, so the goal survives your next UI change.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/web-analytics/conversion-goals">Set up a goal</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Clean up your traffic" subtitle="Paths and bots" icon="IconFilter">

Two things make early numbers look messier than they are. [Path cleaning](/docs/web-analytics/path-cleaning) collapses URLs with IDs in them, so `/users/123` and `/users/456` stop competing for space in your top pages list. And [bot and traffic detection](/docs/web-analytics/bot-detection) classifies crawlers, AI agents, and automation at query time, so you can measure them, exclude them, or break them out separately.

<QuestLogItem title="Tag your campaigns" subtitle="So channels are right" icon="IconMegaphone">

PostHog derives each session's [channel type](/docs/data/channel-type) at ingestion from its entry referrer, [UTM tags](/docs/data/utm-segmentation), and ad click IDs. Tag your campaign links properly and paid, organic, social, and referral traffic separate themselves automatically. Skip it and that traffic quietly lands in Direct or Unknown.

Running paid ads? [Marketing analytics](/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics) (beta) pulls cost, clicks, and impressions from Google, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok, Reddit, and Bing so you can see CPC and ROAS per campaign next to real conversions.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics">Connect ad platforms</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Query traffic from your editor" subtitle="MCP and PostHog AI" icon="IconMagic">

You don't have to open the app to answer a traffic question. [PostHog AI](/docs/web-analytics/investigate-traffic-ai) investigates spikes and drops and builds dashboards from a plain-language question inside the web app, and the [MCP server](/docs/web-analytics/surfaces/mcp) gives your coding agent the same data in Cursor, Claude Code, or VS Code – handy for checking whether a deploy moved a page's traffic.

<OSButton variant="primary" asLink to="/docs/web-analytics/surfaces/mcp">Set up MCP</OSButton>

</QuestLogItem>

<QuestLogItem title="Let Self-driving watch it for you" subtitle="Traffic feeds the inbox" icon="IconMagic">

Nobody checks a dashboard every day. Your traffic is a signal source for [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving): a scout compares each channel, entry path, and referrer against its own seasonality-matched baseline and files a report when one steps out of line – an SEO drop, a paused ad account, broken UTM tagging – even when the site-wide total looks fine. The report goes to a human reviewer, not to a coding agent, because the fix usually lives in your site or campaign tooling rather than a repository.
