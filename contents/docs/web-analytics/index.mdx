Web analytics gives you a simple dashboard of the numbers every website team looks at – visitors, pageviews, [sessions](/docs/data/sessions), bounce rate, top pages, and where your traffic came from. It's built for people who really liked GA3, without the setup tax.

Because it runs on the same events as the rest of PostHog, a traffic number is never a dead end. Click through from a landing page to the [session replays](/docs/session-replay) behind it, tie ad spend to conversions in [marketing analytics](/docs/web-analytics/marketing-analytics), and let [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving) watch each channel against its own baseline so an SEO drop or broken UTM tagging surfaces before it shows up in a monthly report.

## Where its data comes from

Web analytics runs on the traffic your site already sends to PostHog. Pageviews and sessions come from the SDK, channels and campaigns are derived from each session's entry referrer and UTM tags, and marketing analytics adds cost data pulled from your ad platforms.

## How web analytics works with Self-driving

Your traffic is a [signal source](/docs/self-driving/inbox/sources) for [Self-driving](/docs/self-driving). A scout compares each channel, entry path, and referrer against its own seasonality-matched baseline – not against a flat total – so a single segment stepping out of line becomes a report even when the site-wide number looks fine.

A traffic problem is rarely something a coding agent can fix. An SEO drop, a paused ad account, or a campaign missing its UTM tags lives in your site, your campaign tooling, or your marketing stack, not in a repository PostHog can open a pull request against. So these scouts stop at the report and route it to a human reviewer.

The two scouts that watch this data are acquisition and site health – channel divergence, attribution breakage, bounce and 404 steps – and [web vitals](/docs/web-analytics/web-vitals) regressions per page. Other signal sources, like [error tracking](/docs/error-tracking), do open pull requests. See [PostHog Desktop](/docs/web-analytics/surfaces/desktop) for what a report looks like, and the [Self-driving docs](/docs/self-driving) for the full picture.
