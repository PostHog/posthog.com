# Get it fixed while you sleep

The previous page turned on a pipeline that misses nothing. This page adds the thing that pipeline does not have, which is an opinion about what matters.

## What a scout adds that a source cannot

A <Term name="scout" /> runs on a schedule rather than continuously, explores rather than following one stream, and decides what clears the bar you set. So it can rank, cluster, weigh who is affected, and stay quiet on a day when nothing important happened.

A source holds no opinion. That is its strength, and it is why the two compose: the source keeps its complete coverage, and the scout sits on top of it, bundling related items and flagging what nobody has actioned.

## The scouts that already watch errors

You do not have to write one. These ship with PostHog, and each is a skill you can read:

- **[Error tracking](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog/blob/master/products/signals/skills/signals-scout-error-tracking/SKILL.md)** – error spikes, stuck loops, and <Term name="fingerprint" /> clusters, triaged by how many users they hit.
- **[APM](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog/blob/master/products/signals/skills/signals-scout-apm/SKILL.md)** – new errors per service and operation, alongside error rate and latency regressions.
- **[Logs](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog/blob/master/products/signals/skills/signals-scout-logs/SKILL.md)** – new error message patterns, severity shifts, and services that went silent.
- **[CSP violations](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog/blob/master/products/signals/skills/signals-scout-csp-violations/SKILL.md)** – violation clusters, per-directive bursts, and third-party domains that should not be there.

Turn each on or off per project. A scout writes a <Term name="report" /> only when something clears its bar, like the one in <SeeFig n={1} />.

## Writing one for your own question

The error tracking scout triages by people. It stops short of asking what those people are worth to you, because that answer lives in your revenue data, not in the exception.

Extending it is a short edit rather than a new scout, and the self-driving volume works one through end to end.

## From report to pull request

An actionable report goes to an agent with its evidence attached. The agent reads the <Term name="stack trace" />, the sessions, and the code it points at, then opens a pull request in your repository, which you review like any other. Nothing merges without you.

Reports are free. A pull request costs $15, and the first three each month are free, with a billing limit you set.
