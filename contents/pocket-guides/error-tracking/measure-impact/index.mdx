# Measure impact with your other data

An exception count tells you how often something broke. To decide what to fix first, you also need to know who it hit and what it cost you.

Each <Term name="exception" /> is a `$exception` <Term name="event" />, stored with the rest of your product and business data. You can query exceptions alongside your other events, people, [groups](/docs/product-analytics/group-analytics), and sessions, and [join](/docs/data-warehouse/join) them to the revenue and plan data you sync from [sources](/docs/data-warehouse/sources) like Stripe or Postgres.

## Rank issues by revenue

Join each <Term name="issue" /> to the revenue of the accounts it hit. If MRR lives in Stripe or your own database, [warehouse properties](/docs/data-warehouse/warehouse-properties) copy it onto each organization as a group property.

Run this query in the [SQL editor](/docs/data-warehouse/query). It sums the MRR of the organizations behind each issue over the last 7 days. It reads organizations from `$group_0` (group index 0) and MRR from an `mrr` group property, so change those to match yours:

```sql
WITH affected AS (
    SELECT issue_id, $group_0 AS org, count() AS exceptions
    FROM events
    WHERE event = '$exception'
      AND timestamp >= now() - INTERVAL 7 DAY
      AND notEmpty($group_0)
    GROUP BY issue_id, org
)
SELECT
    a.issue_id,
    sum(a.exceptions) AS exceptions,
    count() AS organizations,
    round(sum(toFloat(g.properties.mrr))) AS org_mrr
FROM affected a
LEFT JOIN groups g ON g.key = a.org AND g.index = 0
GROUP BY a.issue_id
ORDER BY org_mrr DESC
LIMIT 10
```

In this example, checkout fails less often than search, but the organizations it hits pay twelve times as much in total:

| Issue | Exceptions | Organizations | Their MRR |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Checkout confirm fails | 620 | 240 | **$48,000** |
| Invoice download fails | 180 | 64 | $16,000 |
| Search request times out | 9,000 | 50 | $4,000 |

MRR is what those organizations pay you. It doesn't measure revenue the error lost.

The [Errors by account value](/pocket-guides/error-tracking/who-it-breaks) scout checks your issues every day and reports the ones concentrated in paying accounts.

## Find one account's errors

When a customer reports a problem, start from their account. Filter the issue list to their organization with a SQL expression like `$group_0 = 'acme'`, or to their users with a person property like email or a <Term name="cohort" />. Then sort by **Users** to see how many people each issue hit:

| Issue | Users affected | Exceptions |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Checkout confirm fails | **186** | 314 |
| Invoice download fails | 27 | 41 |
| Search request times out | 2 | 2,840 |

Checkout hit 186 of their users, so fix it first. Search fired 2,840 times from only 2 users, which points to a retry loop you can look into separately.

## Check conversion

A <Term name="funnel" /> from start to completion, with its graph type set to [historical trends](/docs/product-analytics/funnels#graph-type), shows whether conversion fell when the error appeared <SeeFig n={1} />. In this example, checkout starts hold steady at 1,000 a day, and completions drop from 800 to 500 when the error appears.

To see how the people who hit the error converted, create a cohort from the **Cohort** section of the issue page and [break the funnel down](/docs/product-analytics/funnels#breakdowns) by it. Compare their conversion with the funnel's overall rate, then watch a few of their sessions in <Term name="session replay" /> to see whether they got stuck, retried, or found another way.

## Compare experiment variants

An <Term name="experiment" /> can lift one metric and add errors elsewhere. Add `$exception` to the experiment as a [secondary metric](/docs/experiments/metrics#primary-and-secondary-metrics), and PostHog compares it across variants next to your primary metric.

To see when the errors started, chart the share of sessions with an exception in each variant <SeeFig n={2} />. Use a rate, so a variant with more traffic doesn't look worse just because it's bigger. [Exceptions as events](/pocket-guides/error-tracking/exceptions-as-events#turn-counts-into-rates) shows how to build one, and the Feature Flags pocket guide covers [running the experiment](/pocket-guides/feature-flags/flag-to-experiment).
