# What a fix is worth

By February, Unter had one backend engineer and a growing list of things that needed fixing. A slow form and a broken link both arrived as support tickets – <SeeFig n={1} />. Neither said what happened if they stayed broken, or which one was causing the most impact.

## Put the money next to the behavior

Unter already knew what people did on the site <SeeFig n={2} />, and what those people were worth: charges in Stripe, subscriptions, renewals.

So they put those together with the other things they knew – support tickets, their CRM, and an issue tracker – and suddenly a ticket stopped being just a ticket.

A host who never cut a hole might also be a subscription that never renews – <SeeFig n={3} />. That is a very different kind of bug.

## Some fixes matter more than others

This changed what got worked on first.

A broken button on a busy page looks important because lots of people see it. But a quieter problem can be worth more if it stops hosts from ever getting to the thing they came to do.

For Unter, one missing hole could mean a lost route, a lost host, and a subscription that never came back.

## One number, wherever you ask

Revenue metrics were defined once and reused everywhere.

The number in a Monday email, the number in the SQL editor, and the answer PostHog gave when someone asked for it in plain English all came from the same definition.
