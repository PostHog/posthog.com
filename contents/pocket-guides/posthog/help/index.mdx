# LLM analytics on a support bot

Unter's help page carried a chat widget, and behind the widget a bot that answered most of what it was asked <SeeFig n={1} />. The bot ran on a model, which meant every answer it gave had a cost, a latency and a chance of being wrong.

LLM analytics recorded all three <SeeFig n={2} />. A support bot is a product surface like any other, and an unrecorded one is a place where a company stops knowing what it is telling people.

## What people asked, and what they were told

The widget logged the question, the answer, the model, and the tokens either side <SeeFig n={3} />. Where a conversation ended in a handover, the survey underneath it recorded whether the person got what they came for.

## The way out, when the bot could not help

Every conversation had an escalation at the end of it, and the escalations were counted too <SeeFig n={4} />. In February the widget handled about forty questions a week and passed six of them to a person, which Unter read as the bot working.

By the end of February the help page was the best instrumented thing Unter owned. Every question, every answer, every handover, and a survey on the end of it.

None of that measures the people who never open the widget. A support queue can only report what arrives in it, and an empty one is the same shape whether nothing is wrong or nobody could be bothered to say so.
