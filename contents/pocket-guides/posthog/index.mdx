# PostHog 101

</LeftPage>

<RightPage>

Building a product means understanding how people use it. Product engineers use PostHog to do just that.

This pocket guide follows the product engineers building [Twig](https://twig.com), a fictional vacation-rental application, as they develop and improve the product. Their work highlights what PostHog does, what data it needs, and how its tools fit together to help build a better product.

## Meet Twig

Twig is the bird-run vacation-rental site at the center of this guide. You'll see snapshots of its pages, starting with "Find a stay". Twig itself is interactive: use **Explore Twig** to browse stays and learn about PostHog with hands-on tools.

<TwigIntroductionFigure />

## What you'll learn

The guide starts with a question: _what kind of stay are visitors looking for?_ That first question leads to bigger ones. Follow Twig's engineers as they:

- Record meaningful events, then use identity to connect activity to the people behind it.
- Use PostHog's tools to understand behavior, investigate problems, ship changes, and measure their impact.
- See how self-driving spots useful signals across those tools and helps the team decide what to do next.

The PostHog data in this guide is simulated. No account or installation is needed to read along.
