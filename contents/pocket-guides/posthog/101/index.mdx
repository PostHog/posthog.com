# Install, and what autocapture caught

On the twelfth of January, someone at Unter pasted eleven lines of JavaScript into a template and deployed it <SeeFig n={1} />. It took an afternoon, most of which went on deciding whether to do it at all.

That afternoon is the origin of every number in this guide, including the ones that eventually described a broken map to an agent at four in the morning <SeeFig n={2} />. Here is the site those eleven lines were added to, running as its users saw it.

## What arrived without anyone deciding

Most of what Unter collected in January, nobody chose <SeeFig n={3} />. Autocapture records clicks and pageviews, so the ranking of what people press exists whether or not anyone predicted which buttons would matter. The same eleven lines record where a visit came from, what it was running on, and which campaign sent it.

This is the unglamorous half and it is the half that paid. Two months later, nobody had to have anticipated a tile provider changing a config for the evidence of it to exist.

## The limits of taking everything

Autocapture is broad and shallow. It sees a press on a button with some classes on it, not a signup, and it does not survive a component rename.

Unter learned this the ordinary way. They renamed an event in April, and the four months either side of the rename have never lined up since. Event storage is write-only for practical purposes: renaming does not rewrite history, it forks it.

## Taking everything, except when asked not to

The same eleven lines carry the way out of them <SeeFig n={4} />. A visitor who opts out stops being recorded from that moment, and the call that does it sits in the footer next to the privacy notice, where Unter's lawyer wanted it.

Capture that cannot be turned off is capture nobody can defend, and the afternoon in January included wiring up the off switch.
