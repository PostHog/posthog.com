# What counts as activation

By February, Under needed one number to take into the Monday meeting <SeeFig n={1} />. Not the number of visits or signups, but the number that meant the product was actually being used.

They settled it in two meetings, on the eighteenth of February. Signups were the obvious answer. They were also the wrong one <SeeFig n={2} />.

A person can sign up, look around, and never cut a hole. For Under, that person had not really used the product.

## A hole cut is the event

So activation became a hole cut <SeeFig n={3} />.

That made the Monday number a count of people who had actually done the thing Under existed to help them do. And when the outage arrived in March, that definition mattered. The number moved after eleven days, not after a quarter.

The host page is where the hole gets registered, so that is where the number can be counted.

## The number was already there

Nothing new had to be instrumented to produce it.

The events Under had been collecting since January already described the path from booking to a finished hole. A funnel simply put those events in order and counted how many people made it through.

The important part was that they wrote the definition down.

The Monday email used it. The dashboards used it. And when an agent came looking for the number in March, it was the same definition waiting there.

One decision about what counted as a customer turned out to be the thing that made the outage countable.
