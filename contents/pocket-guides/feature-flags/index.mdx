# Feature Flags

A <Term name="feature flag">feature flag</Term> separates deploying code from releasing it. You merge the change turned off, then decide from PostHog who gets it: your team first, then 5% of users, then everyone. If errors climb at 5%, you flip the flag off, and nobody waits on a revert.

This guide follows one flag, `new-checkout`, through its whole life: checked in code, rolled out a few people at a time, switched off when something breaks, tuned with a payload, tested as an experiment, and removed once it has done its job.

## Where to start

New to flags? Start with [Feature Flags 101](/docs/feature-flags/learn/101).

Choose the lesson for what you are working on:

- Letting a few people try a change first: [Rollouts](/docs/feature-flags/learn/roll-out-gradually)
- Something broke and has to go dark now: [The off switch](/docs/feature-flags/learn/off-switch)
- Checking a flag on the server and in the browser: [Server and client](/docs/feature-flags/learn/server-and-client)
- Changing copy or limits without a deploy: [Payloads](/docs/feature-flags/learn/payloads)
- Testing whether a change is better: [Experiments](/docs/feature-flags/learn/flag-to-experiment)
- Deleting flags that finished their job: [Cleaning up](/docs/feature-flags/learn/cleanup)
