# Reverse proxy

You need a reverse proxy to reliably capture replays.

Ad blockers keep lists of know analytics domains and block requests to them. This will often result in incomplete or missing replays being captured. Using a reverse proxy lets you route through your own domain, so captures are not blocked.

## What gets blocked

These are the destinations required for Session Replay to work. They're also commonly blocked by ad blockers. When you set up a reverse proxy, these are the destinations to proxy.

| Serves | US Cloud | EU Cloud |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The SDK, the replay recorder, and your project's config<br />`/static/*` and `/array/*` | `us-assets.i.posthog.com` | `eu-assets.i.posthog.com` |
| Everything else: events, recorded session data, feature flags | `us.i.posthog.com` | `eu.i.posthog.com` |

Nothing errors when a recording is blocked. The recording just never appears in PostHog which is frustrating to debug.

## Setting one up with PostHog

PostHog runs a free, managed proxy for you. It sits on a subdomain you own, and PostHog handles the SSL
certificate, the routing, and the maintenance.

<CTA to="https://app.posthog.com/settings/organization-proxy">Set up a managed proxy</CTA>

Two important things to decide with your team:

**Pick a boring subdomain.** Anything containing `analytics`, `tracking`, `telemetry`, `posthog`,
or `ph` is already on the block lists you're trying to avoid.

**Turn your DNS provider's own proxy off.** On Cloudflare that's the grey cloud, not the orange
one. Leaving it on breaks certificate provisioning.

## Limits of a proxy

The managed proxy routes traffic through Cloudflare, a listed subprocessor in our DPA. It's also not HIPAA-compliant, so a BAA with PostHog doesn't cover it – run your own if you handle PHI.

DNS-level blocking follows your subdomain's CNAME chain, sees where it really points, and drops the request before the browser is involved. That needs a custom resolver like NextDNS or Pi-hole, so it's a small share of users, and no proxy gets around it. Browser extensions can't do this – they only see HTTP requests after DNS has already resolved.

Your own `Content-Security-Policy` will also stop `recorder.js` from being served. To make sure this doesn't happen, read or point your agent at [this docs page](/docs/advanced/content-security-policy).
