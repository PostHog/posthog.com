# Configure replays

When configuring replays, there are three questions to ask yourself:
- Which session replays to keep
- What those replays capture
- How long replays are retained

All three apply at capture time. Recording rules only affect sessions recorded after you change them, and a replay captured unmasked stays unmasked – there is no retroactive pass over what you already have.

Try to answer these questions before real traffic arrives. Most of the answers live in one place: your project's [replay settings](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay).

## Which sessions you record

Recording everything is rarely what you want, and it's the main driver of cost. Start by capturing 100% of sessions and decrease from there. That's the fastest way to see how many sessions you're recording and how much data you're collecting.

Your main knobs to turn are sampling, triggers, and minimum durations, all under [Ingestion controls](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay-ingestion).

### Sampling

Sampling records a percentage of all sessions. It's deterministic: when a session starts, PostHog hashes the session ID into a number between 0 and 1 and compares it to your sample rate. Under the rate, the session is recorded.

Because the same session ID always produces the same number, the decision holds for the whole session, including across page refreshes. What you can't do is choose *which* sessions get picked – the hash decides.

### Triggers

You can also record on a condition instead of a coin flip: a URL, an event (an exception, say), a feature flag, or a <Term name="cohort" />. [Trigger groups](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay-triggers) are the flexible form – each group carries its own URLs, events, flags, sample rate, minimum duration, and match type, and a session is recorded if it matches any group.

One trap worth knowing: "any" matching with 100% sampling records every session, and your other conditions stop mattering – the sampling condition alone matches everything. Lower the rate or switch to "all" matching.

### Minimum duration

A minimum duration drops sessions too short to be useful – under two seconds, say, where someone bounced before the page settled.

It's the setting most worth having. Every session counts for billing, so without a floor you collect a pile of short, low-value recordings and pay for all of them. Two seconds is the house recommendation, and the [same settings panel](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay-ingestion) carries the other tuning options.

## What's masked, and what isn't

PostHog's privacy controls run in the browser or the mobile app. Masked data is never sent over the network to PostHog – it doesn't arrive and get hidden, it never leaves the device.

Most of what follows is SDK config, but the project-wide defaults live under [Masking](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay-masking).

### The defaults

Any input element is highly likely to contain sensitive text, so inputs are masked by default. General text is not.

That asymmetry is what surprises people. If your app renders an email address, an invoice, or someone's name as ordinary text, it's in the replay until you say otherwise.

### Mask first, unmask later

Take the safer posture: mask everything, then reveal what you've confirmed is safe. Set `maskAllInputs` and `maskTextSelector: "*"`, then let `maskTextFn` reveal only elements carrying a data attribute you control.

The alternative – masking more each time you notice something – means every feature you ship is exposed until somebody remembers it. Masking first makes the default safe and revealing deliberate.

### The levers

`maskTextSelector: "*"` masks all non-input text. A CSS selector – `.email, #sensitive` – masks specific elements, and masking an element applies to its children, so `:not` selectors don't do what you want. And `maskTextFn` decides per string, which is how you mask only text that looks like an email.

For anything that isn't text or an input, add the class `ph-no-capture`. The element is replaced by a block of the same size in playback – tell your team, so nobody reports the blank rectangle as a bug. It also stops <Term name="autocapture" /> events firing from that element.

### The URL bar counts

The replay player shows the page URL on the timeline, and query strings carry auth tokens and user identifiers. The `maskCapturedNetworkRequestFn` callback runs against the captured page URL as well as network requests, so one config redacts both – in the browser, before anything is sent.

### Mobile screens

Native Android and iOS record a wireframe of the view hierarchy; screenshot mode captures the real screen instead, which means it can capture anything on it. React Native and Flutter always record in screenshot mode, so masking there isn't optional.

## What else you can turn on

Everything above removes data. These add it – each one off until you switch it on, and each with its own cost and privacy consequence.

### Console logs

[Console logs](/docs/session-replay/console-log-recording), info, warnings, and errors from your application. Useful for debugging and providing extra context on what is happening in your user's browser or mobile device environment. Turn it on under [Replay settings](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay).

### Network requests

[Network requests and responses](/docs/session-replay/network-recording), providing insights into network performance and potential issues, switched on under [Network capture](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay-network).

Request and response bodies are a switch of their own: turning network capture on doesn't turn them on with it. Bodies are where auth tokens and personal data live, so redact them with `maskCapturedNetworkRequestFn` before you enable them.

### Canvas

Canvas elements aren't covered by DOM-based masking, so PostHog doesn't capture them automatically. Once you're sure they're free of PII – or you've set up [canvas masking](/docs/session-replay/canvas-recording) – you can enable it under [Replay settings](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay). Canvas records at four frames per second.

### Iframes

[Iframes](/docs/session-replay/iframes) record when they're same-origin, or when you can install `posthog-js` inside them and set `recordCrossOriginIframes` with the right content security policy. Third-party embeds you don't control – Typeform, YouTube, Stripe Checkout – can't be recorded, and show up as empty elements.

## How long recordings are kept

Retention is a [project setting](https://app.posthog.com/settings/project-replay#replay-retention), and the ceiling depends on your plan: 30 days on Free, 90 on pay-as-you-go, a year on Boost and Scale, up to five years on Enterprise.

Changing it only affects recordings captured from that point on – existing recordings keep the retention they were captured with. Once a recording is deleted it can't be restored, which is the point if you're subject to data protection regulations. To keep one past its expiry, export it to JSON.

## What it costs

Session replay is free up to a monthly allowance of web and mobile recordings, then priced per recording. Recording rules and data retention are the two biggest levers on the bill, and you can set a [billing limit](https://app.posthog.com/organization/billing) so a traffic spike can't surprise you.

## When replay isn't an option

Some security policies prohibit session replay outright, and no amount of masking changes that.

If that's your situation, the rest of PostHog doesn't depend on it. Events, feature flags, experiments, and error tracking all work without a single recording – you lose the picture of what the person saw, not the data about what they did.
