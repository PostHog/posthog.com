# Session replay 101

Session replay records what real users do in your product and plays it back like a DVR, with a synced dev tool panel showing console logs, network requests, and errors at the exact moment they happened.

## Anatomy of a replay

A session replay is more than a screen recording. It records what happens in a user's session – clicks, scrolls, form inputs, page views, network requests, console logs – and plays it back _like_ video.

The replay page is <SeeFig n={1} />: the sessions you can watch on the left, the one you picked playing back in the middle, and everything PostHog recorded about it on the right.

The inspector <SeeFig n={2} /> lets you watch replays in context.

Session Replay aggregates all of the tools needed to understand what is happening in your user's environment:
- **[Events](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings)** – a list of all the pages visited, events captured, errors encountered during the recording.
- **[Console](/docs/session-replay/console-log-recording)** – console logs, info, warnings, and errors from your application. Useful for debugging and providing extra context on what is happening in your user's browser or mobile device environment.
- **[Network](/docs/session-replay/network-recording)** – network requests and responses, providing insights into network performance and potential issues.
- **[Comments](/docs/data/comments)** – written notes on a particular time on a session recording, so you can highlight what you see.

Console logs and network requests are opt-in, so those tabs are only as full as your capture config. The **[Network waterfall](/docs/session-replay/how-to-watch-recordings)** tab beside the inspector adds the page performance score and asset breakdown.

PostHog already captures your <Term name="event">events</Term>, so every replay is enriched with information about:
- The <Term name="person" /> and their <Term name="person property">properties</Term>
- <Term name="exception">Exceptions</Term> they encountered in the timeline
- <Term name="feature flag">Feature flag</Term> and <Term name="experiment" /> evaluation results

You can jump from a <Term name="funnel" /> drop-off to a session replay, and from the timeline to an error. This is why having all your customer context in PostHog is so powerful.

## Where the recording comes from

Recordings come from your PostHog SDKs: web, React Native, iOS, Android, and Flutter. Recording is a project setting as well as an SDK one, so it has to be switched on in project settings before anything is captured.

These replays are not videos on the web. The SDK takes a full snapshot of the page's DOM, then records only what changes – clicks, scrolls, mutations – and the player rebuilds the page from that stream. This helps keep cost and performance implications minimal.

Native Android and iOS record a wireframe of the view hierarchy rather than the real look and feel, unless you turn on screenshot mode; React Native and Flutter always record in screenshot mode.

## Watching replays

You watch, filter, and share replays in the PostHog web app. You can also ask PostHog AI to find sessions in plain language and summarize them, pull sessions into your editor over MCP, or read them from the API.

Or have your product watch itself: <Term name="replay vision">Replay Vision</Term> runs AI scanners over the same recordings and turns what they see into queryable data.
