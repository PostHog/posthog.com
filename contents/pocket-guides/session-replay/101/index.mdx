# Session replay 101

Session replay records what real users do in your product and plays it back like a DVR, with a synced DevTools panel showing console logs, network requests, and errors at the exact moment they happened.

Because PostHog already captures your events, every replay is enriched with the person, their properties, and the feature flags they had – so you can jump from a funnel drop-off or an exception straight to the session behind it.

## What a replay is made of

The player is <SeeFig n={1} />: playback, the session's event timeline beside it, and the DevTools panel. Console logs and the network waterfall are opt-in, so the panel is only as full as your capture config.

## Where the recording comes from

Recordings come from your PostHog SDKs: web, React Native, iOS, Android, and Flutter. Recording is a project setting as well as an SDK one, so it has to be switched on in project settings before anything is captured.

Web has the most complete feature support. Native Android and iOS record a wireframe of the view hierarchy rather than the real look and feel, unless you turn on screenshot mode; React Native and Flutter always record in screenshot mode.

## What you get for free, and what you configure

Once the SDK is installed and recording is on, the library starts recording sessions by default. Everything after that is a decision: which sessions you keep, what the browser panels collect, and what gets masked before it leaves the device.

Those three decisions are the next three pages of this guide.

## Who watches them

You watch, filter, and share replays in the PostHog web app. You can also ask PostHog AI to find sessions in plain language and summarize them, pull sessions into your editor over MCP, or read them from the API.

Or have your product watch itself: Replay Vision runs AI scanners over the same recordings and turns what they see into queryable data.

## What a replay won't tell you

A replay shows the symptom, not the cause. Rage clicks on one button could be a slow request, a confusing label, or a real bug – and telling those apart takes a person.
