# The seam: signals-scout-error-tracking discriminates on count vs distinct_users, so it knows
# how many people an issue reached but never which accounts. signals-scout-customer-analytics
# has the account join and weights by commercial ownership, but watches engagement, not errors.
# This scout borrows that join and points it at $exception.
#
# Paired from the error tracking volume – see contents/pocket-guides/error-tracking/who-it-breaks.
title: Find the errors your best accounts hit
shortTitle: Errors by account value
subtitle: 'Which broken thing is your paying customers'' problem, not just your loudest?'
pocketGuideOrder: 7
filters:
  type:
    - self-driving
watches:
  - name: Error Tracking
    detail: Every exception of the last seven days, grouped by issue and by the account that hit it.
  - name: Customer analytics
    detail: Your account roster, so an exception resolves to an organization rather than an anonymous person.
  - name: Revenue analytics
    detail: What those accounts pay, so an issue can be ranked by money rather than by volume.
requires:
  - label: Error Tracking instrumented, with exceptions reaching PostHog
    level: required
  - label: >-
      Group analytics configured, so exceptions carry the account that hit them – without it
      there is nothing to join an issue to
    level: required
  - label: >-
      Revenue connected to persons, through revenue events or a mapped warehouse source. Without
      it the scout can rank by account weight but not by amount
    level: recommended
  - label: A CSM or account executive assigned on your accounts, which is what makes weight legible
    level: optional
schedule: Daily
report:
  title: One issue is 71% concentrated in four paying accounts
  source: Scout · errors by account value
  receivedAgo: 3h
  affected: 4 accounts, ~$180k combined ARR
  body: >-
    `TypeError: Cannot read properties of null` fired 2,340 times this week across 31 accounts.
    Four of them account for 71% of those exceptions while making up 9% of your overall traffic,
    and all four carry a Stripe customer ID.


    The issue does not rank in your top ten by occurrences, because a single unauthenticated
    retry loop on a free account is generating more volume than all four combined.
  suggestedAction: >-
    Reproduce against one of the four accounts rather than the loudest one. Their shared trait is
    a workspace created before the schema change in March.
  actionNote: An agent opens the pull request – you review and merge.
