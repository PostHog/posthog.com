# Read one daily summary of your AI features

Checking on an AI feature means opening four things: the cost chart, the error list, the spend
per user, and the eval results. Each one is fine on its own. Together they are a morning you
spend reading dashboards that mostly say nothing changed.

This scout reads all four for you and writes one report a day.

## What the scout looks at

It compares the last complete 24 hours against the previous 24 hours and the recent 7-day
baseline, using the same weekday when your traffic is seasonal. Complete periods only, because a
partial day reads as a collapse every time.

Across four surfaces:

- **Cost and usage.** What you spent, on what, and whether the unit cost moved.
- **Errors and traces.** New or changed failure patterns, confirmed by opening a real trace.
- **Costly users.** User-level spend that reveals a controllable cause, not just a leaderboard.
- **Evaluations.** Pass-rate regressions and failure surges on the evals you have enabled.

## The one that reports every day

Most scouts in this book are quiet by default. They run, find nothing worth your attention, and
write nothing. That is the point of them.

This one is the exception, and it is deliberate. A digest that skips the quiet days is a digest
you stop trusting, because silence becomes ambiguous: did nothing happen, or did the scout fail?
So every run leaves exactly one report. On a day with nothing to say, that report reads `No
material regressions` and lists the surfaces it checked.

What it will not do is pad. Findings are capped at three bullets, ordered by impact, and a
recurring issue only comes back when it got worse, recovered, relapsed, or gained new evidence.
Routine metrics and unchanged leaderboards never appear.

If you want a scout that stays silent, the other chapters in this volume are that shape. Run
this one when you want a standing daily read on the whole surface.

## The pull request you merge

When a finding is worth acting on, PostHog dispatches an agent to open a pull request with the
report and evidence attached as context. You review and merge it like any other PR. Nothing
merges without you.

Reports are free. Pull requests are **$15 each**, with your first three a month **free**. See
self-driving [Pricing](/docs/self-driving/pricing).
