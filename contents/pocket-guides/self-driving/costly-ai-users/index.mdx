# Find the users whose AI spend is not normal

Sort your users by AI spend and the top of that list will look alarming. It usually isn't. The
heaviest user of a product is supposed to cost the most, and a leaderboard tells you who they
are, not whether anything is wrong.

The users worth finding are the ones whose unit economics broke: same work, more money. A retry
loop, a context that grows every turn, a model chosen badly, caching that stopped hitting, or
somebody using your API for free inference.

This scout finds the users whose spend stopped matching their behavior.

## What the scout looks at

Two users each spend $200 this week. Which one do you want to hear about?

- If their cost per trace matches the population and matches their own history, that's a heavy
  user. The scout stays _quiet_.
- If cost per trace jumped while their trace count held, something changed in how the work gets
  done. The scout _**reports**_.

That second pattern is what it looks for, and a leaderboard cannot show it.

## Where this differs from the cost chapter

[Tell an AI cost spike from real growth](/pocket-guides/self-driving/ai-spend-that-jumps) watches
one number for the whole product: cost per conversation, against its own baseline. It catches a
regression that hits everyone, usually right after a deploy.

This scout works per user. It catches the regressions that never move the aggregate, because they
only affect a few accounts, one workflow, or one shape of input. A retry loop on documents over
8k tokens is invisible in the average and obvious in the four accounts that send long documents.

You can run both. If you run only one, pick the aggregate scout when you ship often, and this one
when your traffic is concentrated in a handful of accounts.

## The report in your inbox

When your product emits <Term name="signal">signals</Term> that a user's spend has broken from its
own pattern, a <Term name="scout" /> opens a <Term name="report" />. <SeeFig n={2} /> breaks the
card down part by part. Click **Review** and the report opens <SeeFig n={3} /> with the full
evidence, the raw signals, and options to take action.

Users with the same root cause are grouped into one report, so four accounts hitting one retry
loop arrive as one finding rather than four.

This scout runs **daily at 9:00 AM** by default. You can choose to run it every 30 minutes up to
once a day. It only writes when something is wrong, so most days it's silent.
