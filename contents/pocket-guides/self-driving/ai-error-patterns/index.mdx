# Find the AI failures that return a 200

Error tracking catches the failures your code knows about: the timeout, the 429, the malformed
response that threw. Those are the easy half.

The other half return successfully. The model ignored an instruction, answered from the wrong
context, called a tool with the wrong arguments, looped, or stopped halfway through and returned
what it had. Every one of those is a 200 with a plausible-looking body, and none of them move
your error rate.

This scout reads real traces and finds the failures that never errored.

## What the scout looks at

Your summarizer is returning 200 on every request and your error rate is flat. Is it working?

- If the outputs are complete and the evals hold, yes. The scout stays _quiet_.
- If one shape of input keeps producing truncated answers, that's a failure mode with no error
  attached. The scout _**reports**_.

<SeeFig n={1} /> is one of the traces it read. Nothing in it is an error.

## Why it reads traces instead of counting errors

An error count tells you how often something threw. It cannot tell you what went wrong first,
and in a multi-step trace that distinction is the whole diagnosis: a tool call that returned bad
arguments produces three downstream symptoms, and counting symptoms gets you three findings
instead of one cause.

So the scout works one use case at a time, opens up to 25 traces per run, and groups them by the
first thing that went wrong. Queries choose what to read. Reading establishes the failure mode.

It will not infer a silent failure from text matching alone. A response containing "I don't know"
is not evidence of anything on its own.

## The report in your inbox

When your product emits <Term name="signal">signals</Term> that a failure mode is new or growing,
a <Term name="scout" /> opens a <Term name="report" />. <SeeFig n={2} /> breaks the card down part
by part. Click **Review** and the report opens <SeeFig n={3} /> with the full evidence, the raw
signals, and options to take action.

One report per root failure, not one per trace or error string.

## The pull request you merge

When you're ready to open a pull request, PostHog dispatches an agent to do it for you with the
report and evidence attached as context. You review and merge it like any other PR. Nothing
